# 6. Cases I: object, to, from, in

Quechua has no prepositions: "to the town" is *llaqtaman*, "in the house" *wasipi*. The case suffixes are the same for every noun, singular or plural, and come after the possessive. This lesson takes the four most frequent.

## Four cases

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | |
|---|---|---|---|
| -ta | object | T'antata mikhuni. | I eat bread. |
| -man | to, towards | Llaqtaman rini. | I go to the town. |
| -manta | from, about | Wasimanta hamuni. | I come from the house. |
| -pi | in, at, on | Wasipi tiyani. | I live in the house. |

- *-ta* marks the object of a verb, person or thing: *Mamata rikuni* "I see the mother".
- *-manta* also gives what a thing is made of and what one speaks about: *rumimanta* "of stone", *Qusqumanta rimani* "I speak about Cusco".
- The verbs here are in the first person: *mikhuni* "I eat", *rini* "I go", *hamuni* "I come", *tiyani* "I live". Lesson 8 gives the rest.

## Suffix after suffix

The order is fixed: noun, possessive, plural, case.

| Quechua | Pieces | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| wasiyman | wasi-y-man | to my house |
| llaqtaykipi | llaqta-yki-pi | in your town |
| wasinchikmanta | wasi-nchik-manta | from our house |
| wasiykunapi | wasi-y-kuna-pi | in my houses |

::: summary
- *-ta* object, *-man* to, *-manta* from or about, *-pi* in or at.
- Order: noun, possessive, plural, case: *wasi-y-kuna-pi*.
- Verbs met: *mikhuy* eat, *riy* go, *hamuy* come, *tiyay* sit, live.
:::
