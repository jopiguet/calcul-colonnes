# 9. How you know: -mi, -si, -chá

A Quechua statement says not only what is true but how the speaker knows it: seen with one's own eyes, heard from others, or guessed. Three small suffixes do this, and a sentence usually has one. This lesson gives them, with the other suffixes that attach to any word: *-qa*, *-pas*, *-lla*, *-ña*, *-raq*.

## The three evidentials

| Suffix | After a vowel | The speaker | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| -mi | -n | saw it, knows it | Parachkanmi. | It is raining (I see it). |
| -si | -s | was told | Parachkansi. | It is raining, they say. |
| -chá | -chá | supposes | Parachkanchá. | It is probably raining. |

- The evidential goes on the word in focus, the new or important piece: *Paqarinmi hamunqa* "He will come *tomorrow*".
- *-si* is the voice of tales and news: what one did not see oneself.
- Saying *-mi* about what one has not seen is overclaiming; speakers choose with care.
- *-n* after a vowel looks like the possessive *-n* "his": *wasin* is "his house" or "it is the house". The sentence decides.

## Suffixes on any word

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | |
|---|---|---|---|
| -qa | topic: as for | Ñuqaqa Qusqumanta kani. | As for me, I am from Cusco. |
| -pas | also, even | Qampas? | You too? |
| -lla | just, only; gently | Allillanmi. | I'm just fine. |
| -ña | already | Hamunña. | He is coming now. |
| -raq | still, yet | Manaraq. | Not yet. |

*-taq* goes on question words: *Imataq?* "What?", *Maytaq?* "Where?". *-qa* marks what the sentence is about; it often sits on the subject.

::: summary
- *-mi / -n*: I know it; *-si / -s*: I was told; *-chá*: probably.
- One evidential per sentence, on the word in focus.
- *-qa* topic, *-pas* also, *-lla* just, *-ña* already, *-raq* still; *-taq* on question words.
:::
