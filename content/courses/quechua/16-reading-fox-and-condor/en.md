# 16. Reading: the fox and the condor

The fox, *atuq*, is the trickster of Andean tales, greedy and boastful, and he often comes to grief. Many villages tell how he flew to a feast in the sky on a condor's back and fell. This lesson reads a short version written for this course, in the past of tales, then the three precepts every Quechua speaker knows.

## Atuqwan kunturwan

Huk kutis huk atuq kasqa. Atuqqa sapa p'unchaw mikhunata maskhasqa.

Huk p'unchawsi kunturqa atuqta nisqa: "Hanaq pachapi hatun raymi kanqa. Hamuy, ñuqawan phawasun."

Atuqqa kunturpa wasanman wichasqa, iskayninkutaq hanaq pachaman phawasqaku. Hanaq pachapi atuqqa anchata mikhusqa, upyasqa, tususqa.

Tutañataq kunturqa kutipusqa, atuqtataq hanaq pachapi saqisqa. Atuqqa huk waskhawan uraykamusqa. Ichaqa waskhaqa p'itikusqa, atuqtaq pampaman urmasqa.

Wiksanqa t'uqyasqa, ukhunmantataq papa, sara, kinuwa ima lluqsimusqa. Chaysi kunanqa kay pachapi papa, sara, kinuwa ima kan.

## The words of the tale

| Quechua | Meaning | Quechua | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| sapa | every | ancha | much, very |
| maskhay | to look for | kutipuy | to go back home |
| niy | to say | saqiy | to leave behind |
| hanaq pacha | the world above | waskha | rope |
| raymi | feast | uraykamuy | to come down |
| wasa | back | ichaqa | but |
| wichay | to climb | p'itikuy | to snap |
| iskayninku | the two of them | urmay | to fall |
| pampa | ground, plain | ukhu | inside |
| lluqsimuy | to come out | sara, kinuwa | maize, quinoa |
| wiksa | belly | t'uqyay | to burst |

Every verb of the tale is in *-sqa-*, and *-si / -s* (*kutis*, *p'unchawsi*, *Chaysi*) marks it as heard, not seen. *-taq* joins the sentences, "and then". *ima* at the end of a list means "and so on". *Chaysi* "so, they say" draws the moral.

## Three precepts

*Ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qilla*: "do not be a thief, do not be a liar, do not be lazy". The triad is often said to have been the Inca greeting, answered by *Qampas* "you too"; the claim is popular, but no source from Inca times records it. Bolivia's constitution of 2009 lists it among the moral principles of the state.

Two words for work together belong beside it: *ayni*, help given and returned, "today I help you, tomorrow you help me"; *minka*, work done by the whole community, for the community.

::: summary
- Tales are told in *-sqa-* with *-si*: *Huk kutis huk atuq kasqa*.
- *-taq* "and then"; *ima* "and so on"; *Chaysi* "so, they say".
- *Ama suwa, ama llulla, ama qilla*, answered *Qampas*.
- *ayni*, help returned; *minka*, communal work.
