# 7. Cases II: of, with, for

The second set of cases gives "of", "with", "for", "until", "because of" and "like". The genitive "of" works in two halves: the owner takes the genitive, and the thing owned takes the possessive "his, her": *wasiq punkun*, "the house's its-door".

## The genitive

In Cusco, the genitive is *-q* after a vowel and *-pa* after a consonant; Ayacucho uses *-pa* everywhere. The owner comes first; the thing owned carries *-n* "his, her, its".

| Quechua | Pieces | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| wasiq punkun | wasi-q punku-n | the door of the house |
| atuqpa chupan | atuq-pa chupa-n | the fox's tail |
| mamaypa wasin | mama-y-pa wasi-n | my mother's house |
| Mariaq wasin | Maria-q wasi-n | María's house |

*mamay* "my mother" ends in the consonant *y*, so it takes *-pa*.

## More cases

| Suffix | Meaning | Example | |
|---|---|---|---|
| -wan | with, and | mamaywan | with my mother |
| -paq | for | wawapaq | for the child |
| -kama | until, as far as | paqarinkama | until tomorrow |
| -rayku | because of | qamrayku | because of you |
| -hina | like | kunturhina | like a condor |

- *-wan* joins two nouns as "and", on both: *taytawan mamawan* "father and mother".
- Pronouns take cases like nouns: *ñuqapaq* "for me", *qamwan* "with you".
- *kay* also means "this": *Kayqa qampaq* "This is for you".

::: exercise suffix
Which suffix?

- I talk with my mother: Mamay… rimani. → ((-wan* / -paq / -kama))
- bread for the child: wawa… t'anta → ((-wan / -paq* / -hina))
- until tomorrow: paqarin… → ((-rayku / -paq / -kama*))
- it runs like a puma: puma… phawan. → ((-hina* / -wan / -kama))
:::

::: summary
- Genitive *-q* after a vowel, *-pa* after a consonant; the thing owned takes *-n*: *wasiq punkun*.
- *-wan* with, and; *-paq* for; *-kama* until; *-rayku* because of; *-hina* like.
- *kay* "this": *Kayqa qampaq*.
:::
