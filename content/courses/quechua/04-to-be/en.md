# 4. To be, yes and no

*kay* "to be" is the first verb to learn, and the simplest sentences need it only half the time: "she is a teacher" has no verb in Quechua. This lesson gives *kay*, the suffix *-mi* of a plain statement, and *-chu*, which makes both questions and negations.

## kay, to be

| Person | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ñuqa | kani | I am |
| qam | kanki | you are |
| pay | kan | he, she is |
| ñuqanchik | kanchik | we are (you included) |
| ñuqayku | kayku | we are (you excluded) |
| qamkuna | kankichik | you all are |
| paykuna | kanku | they are |

The endings *-ni, -nki, -n, -nchik, -yku, -nkichik, -nku* are those of every verb in the present (lesson 8). The pronoun may be dropped, since the ending names the person: *Qusqumanta kani* "I am from Cusco".

:::

## Statements: -mi

A plain statement puts *-mi* on its key word: *-mi* after a consonant, *-n* after a vowel. In the present, "he, she is" is left out:

| Quechua | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pay yachachiqmi. | She is a teacher. |
| Ñuqa yachachiqmi kani. | I am a teacher. |
| Wasiqa hatunmi. | The house is big. |
| Allinmi. | It is good. |

An adjective comes before its noun: *hatun wasi* "a big house", *huch'uy wasi* "a small house", *sumaq t'ika* "a beautiful flower". Lesson 9 says more of *-mi*.

## Questions and no: -chu

*-chu* on the key word asks a yes-or-no question. With *mana* "not" before, the same *-chu* makes a negation:

| Statement | Question | Negation |
|---|---|---|
| Yachachiqmi kanki. | Yachachiqchu kanki? | Mana yachachiqchu kanki. |
| Hatunmi. | Hatunchu? | Mana hatunchu. |

The answer: *Ari, yachachiqmi kani* "Yes, I am a teacher"; *Manan, mana yachachiqchu kani* "No, I am not a teacher".

:::

::: exercise ask
Which sentence asks the question?

- Are you from Cusco? → ((Qusqumantachu kanki?* / Qusqumantan kanki. / Mana Qusqumantachu kanki.))
- Is the house big? → ((Wasiqa hatunmi. / Wasiqa hatunchu?* / Hatun wasi.))
- Is she a teacher? → ((Pay yachachiqmi. / Mana yachachiqchu. / Pay yachachiqchu?*))
- Is it good? → ((Allinchu?* / Allinmi. / Mana allinchu.))
:::

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- Wasiqa hatunmi. → {{The house is big.}}
- Pay yachachiqmi. → {{She is a teacher. / He is a teacher.}}
- Mana yachachiqchu kani. → {{I am not a teacher. / I'm not a teacher.}}
- Huch'uy wasi. → {{A small house. / The small house. / Small house.}}
:::

::: summary
- kay: kani, kanki, kan, kanchik, kayku, kankichik, kanku; "he, she is" dropped in the present.
- *-mi* (after a vowel *-n*) marks a plain statement: *Allinmi*.
- *-chu* asks: *Hatunchu?*; *mana … -chu* denies: *Mana hatunchu*.
- The adjective comes first: *hatun wasi*.
:::
