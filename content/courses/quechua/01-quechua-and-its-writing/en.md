# 1. Quechua and its writing

Quechua was the language of the Inca state and is today the most widely spoken indigenous language of the Americas, with some eight to ten million speakers from Colombia to Argentina. This course teaches Southern Quechua as spoken in Cusco, written in Peru's official alphabet. This lesson places the language and teaches how it is written and stressed.

## A family of languages

Quechua is a family rather than a single language. Its two branches are not mutually intelligible.

| Branch | Where | Varieties |
|---|---|---|
| Quechua I, central | central highlands of Peru | Áncash, Huánuco, Junín |
| Quechua II, southern and northern | southern Peru, Bolivia, Argentina; Ecuador and Colombia | Cusco–Collao, Ayacucho–Chanka, Bolivian; Kichwa in Ecuador |

Speakers call their language *runasimi*, "the people's mouth": *runa* "person", *simi* "mouth, language". The name *qhichwa* first meant the temperate valleys. Quechua is an official language in Peru and Bolivia.

## The alphabet

The official alphabet of Cusco Quechua has three vowels, *a*, *i*, *u*, and these consonants:

| Kind | Letters |
|---|---|
| plain stops | p, t, ch, k, q |
| aspirated stops | ph, th, chh, kh, qh |
| ejective stops | p', t', ch', k', q' |
| others | h, l, ll, m, n, ñ, r, s, w, y |

*q* is said further back than *k*, at the uvula. Next to *q*, *qh* or *q'*, the vowels *i* and *u* sound like *e* and *o*: *qillqa* "writing" sounds "qellqa", *Qusqu* "Cusco" sounds "Qosqo". That is why older and Spanish spellings show five vowels.

:::

## Spanish spellings

Many Quechua words reached the world through Spanish spelling: *hu* for *w*, *c* and *qu* for both *k* and *q*, five vowels. The official alphabet writes each sound with one letter.

| Spanish spelling | Official | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Cuzco | Qusqu | Cusco |
| huasi | wasi | house |
| quipu | khipu | knotted-cord record |
| Huiracocha | Wiraqucha | the creator god |
| Titicaca | Titiqaqa | the lake |

## Stress

The stress falls on the next-to-last syllable, whatever the length of the word. Since Quechua adds suffix after suffix, the stress moves with them: *WA-si* "house", *wa-si-KU-na* "houses", *wa-si-ku-NA-pi* "in the houses".

- qillqa → ((qill* / qa))
- Wiraqucha → ((Wi / ra / qu* / cha))
:::

::: summary
- Quechua is a family; this course teaches Southern Quechua of Cusco, in the official alphabet.
- Three vowels, *a i u*; next to *q*, *i* and *u* sound *e* and *o*.
- Stops come plain, aspirated (*ph*) and ejective (*p'*); *q* is said at the uvula.
- The stress is on the next-to-last syllable and moves as suffixes are added.
:::
