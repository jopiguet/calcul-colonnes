# 13. Me and you inside the verb

"You see me", "I see you", "she helps you": Quechua says these in one word, by putting the object inside the verb. A third-person object needs nothing: *rikuni* is "I see him, her, it". This lesson gives the forms with "me", "you" and "us", in the present and in commands.

## The forms

| Who → whom | rikuy, to see | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| you → me | rikuwanki | you see me |
| he, she → me | rikuwan | he, she sees me |
| they → me | rikuwanku | they see me |
| he, she → us (you included) | rikuwanchik | he, she sees us |
| I → you | rikuyki | I see you |
| he, she → you | rikusunki | he, she sees you |
| they → you | rikusunkiku | they see you |
| I → him, her | rikuni | I see him, her |

- *-wa-* is "me": *riku-wa-nki*, *riku-wa-n*.
- "I → you" is the single suffix *-yki*: *rikuyki*, *kuyayki* "I love you".
- "he, she → you" is *-sunki*: *rikusunki*.
- In a command, *-wa-* comes before *-y*: *Yanapaway!* "Help me!".

::: exercise yanapay
Write the form of yanapay, to help.

- you help me → [[yanapawanki]]
- I help you → [[yanapayki]]
- he helps me → [[yanapawan]]
- she helps you → [[yanapasunki]]
:::

:::

## With pronouns

The pronoun is not needed, but it may be added for weight, with *-ta*: *Qamta kuyayki* "It is you I love". The person who acts can stand before the verb: *Mamay yanapawan* "My mother helps me".

::: exercise who
Who does what?

- kuyayki → ((I love you* / you love me / he loves you))
- kuyawanki → ((I love you / you love me* / he loves me))
- rikusunki → ((I see you / he sees you* / you see him))
- rikuni → ((I see her* / she sees me / I see you))
:::

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- Mamay yanapawan. → {{My mother helps me.}}
- Qamta kuyayki. → {{I love you.}}
- Suyaway, ama riychu! → {{Wait for me, don't go!}}
- Taytayki rikusunki. → {{Your father sees you. / Your father is watching you.}}
:::

::: summary
- *-wa-* me: *rikuwanki* you see me, *rikuwan* he sees me.
- *-yki* I → you: *rikuyki, kuyayki*; *-sunki* he → you: *rikusunki*.
- Command: *Yanapaway! Uyariway! Suyaway!*
- A third-person object needs no suffix: *rikuni* "I see her".
:::
