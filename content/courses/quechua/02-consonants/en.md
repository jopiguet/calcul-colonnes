# 2. Plain, aspirated and ejective

Cusco Quechua tells words apart by how a stop is released: plain, with a puff of breath, or with a sharp click of the throat. *pacha* is the earth, *p'acha* is clothing. This lesson trains the ear and the spelling for these three series and for the uvular *q*.

## Three series of stops

| Plain | Aspirated | Ejective |
|---|---|---|
| p | ph | p' |
| t | th | t' |
| ch | chh | ch' |
| k | kh | k' |
| q | qh | q' |

- A **plain** stop is released without breath, as in Spanish or French: *pacha*, *chaki*.
- An **aspirated** stop is followed by a puff of air, as English *p* in "pot", but stronger: *phuyu* "cloud", *qhatu* "market".
- An **ejective** stop is released with a closed throat, which gives a short pop: *t'anta* "bread", *t'ika* "flower".

The aspirates and ejectives are shared with the neighbouring Aymara language, under whose influence they are thought to have arisen. Ayacucho Quechua has none of them: there, bread is *tanta*.

## Pairs to hear

| Word | Meaning | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| pacha | earth, time | p'acha | clothing |
| chaki | foot | ch'aki | dry |
| killa | moon | qilla | lazy |

*k* is said with the back of the tongue against the soft palate, as in English; *q* further back, against the uvula, which lowers the vowels nearby: *qilla* sounds "qella".

## The other consonants

| Letter | Sound |
|---|---|
| h | as English *h* |
| ll | as Spanish *ll*, a *l* with the tongue flat on the palate |
| ñ | as Spanish *ñ*, French *gn* |
| r | a single tap, as Spanish *pero* |
| w, y | as English *w*, *y* |
| l, m, n, s | as in English |

At the end of a syllable, Cusco speakers soften *q* to a sound like Spanish *j*: *atuq* "fox" sounds close to "atoj".

::: summary
- Stops come in three series: plain *p t ch k q*, aspirated *ph th chh kh qh*, ejective *p' t' ch' k' q'*.
- The series tell words apart: *pacha* earth, *p'acha* clothing; *chaki* foot, *ch'aki* dry.
- *q* is said at the uvula: *killa* moon, *qilla* lazy; next to it, *i u* sound *e o*.
- Ayacucho Quechua has no aspirates or ejectives.
:::
