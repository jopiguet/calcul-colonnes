# 3. Greetings and pronouns

In the Andes a greeting is a short exchange about how one is, and a goodbye looks forward to the next meeting. This lesson gives the phrases of a first conversation and the seven personal pronouns, among them two words for "we".

## Greetings

| Quechua | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Napaykullayki. | I greet you (respectful). |
| Allillanchu? | Are you well? |
| Allillanmi. | I am well. |
| Imaynallan kachkanki? | How are you? |
| Imataq sutiyki? | What is your name? |
| Sutiyqa Rosa. | My name is Rosa. |
| Maymantataq kanki? | Where are you from? |
| Qusqumanta kani. | I am from Cusco. |
| Ari. Manan. | Yes. No. |
| Sulpayki. Añay. | Thank you. |
| Tupananchikkama. | Until we meet again. |

Every phrase is a word with suffixes: *alli-lla-n-chu* "good-just-it is-?", *suti-y-qa* "name-my-as for". The following lessons take these suffixes one by one.

## The personal pronouns

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | ñuqa I | ñuqanchik we, you included; ñuqayku we, you excluded |
| 2nd | qam you | qamkuna you all |
| 3rd | pay he, she | paykuna they |

- *pay* is both "he" and "she": Quechua has no grammatical gender.
- *ñuqanchik* includes the person spoken to: "we, you and I". *ñuqayku* leaves that person out: "we, my family and I, but not you".
- In speech, Cusco often says *ñuqanchis*; the official spelling is *ñuqanchik*.
- The plural is made with *-kuna*: *qam*, *qamkuna*; *pay*, *paykuna*.

::: exercise we
Which "we"?

- You tell a friend: "we, you and I, are going to the market" → ((ñuqanchik* / ñuqayku))
- You tell a stranger: "we, my family, live in Cusco" → ((ñuqanchik / ñuqayku*))
- The teacher tells the class: "today we all learn Quechua" → ((ñuqanchik* / ñuqayku))
- Two travellers tell the innkeeper: "we come from Puno" → ((ñuqanchik / ñuqayku*))
:::
