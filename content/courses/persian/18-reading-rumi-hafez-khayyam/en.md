# 18. Reading: Rumi, Hafez and Khayyam

Three poets whose lines are known by heart from Tehran to Kabul and Dushanbe. Rumi, in the 13th century, opened his *Masnavi* with the lament of a reed flute; Hafez, in 14th-century Shiraz, wrote the ghazals that families still open at random for an omen; the quatrains attributed to Khayyam, astronomer and mathematician of the 11th century, became famous in the West through FitzGerald's English version.

## Rumi, the first lines of the *Masnavi*

| Persian | Translation |
|---|---|
| بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند | Listen to this reed, how it complains, |
| از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند | telling a tale of separations. |
| کز نیستان تا مرا ببریده‌اند | "Since they cut me from the reed bed, |
| در نفیرم مرد و زن نالیده‌اند | men and women have wept in my cry." |

The reed نی *ney* is the soul cut off from its origin. بشنو *beshno* "listen" is the imperative of شنیدن; ببریده‌اند is a present perfect with the old prefix بـ.

## Hafez, the first ghazal

| Persian | Translation |
|---|---|
| الا یا ایها الساقی ادر کأسا و ناولها | Ho, cup-bearer, pass the cup around and hand it on, |
| که عشق آسان نمود اول ولی افتاد مشکل‌ها | for love seemed easy at first, but difficulties came. |

The first half-line is Arabic, as educated Persian verse allowed. ساقی *sâghi* is the cup-bearer; نمود *nemud* is classical for "seemed"; افتاد "fell", that is "arose".

## Khayyam, a quatrain

| Persian | Translation |
|---|---|
| این قافلهٔ عمر عجب می‌گذرد | This caravan of life passes strangely fast; |
| دریاب دمی که با طرب می‌گذرد | seize the moment that passes in joy. |
| ساقی غم فردای حریفان چه خوری | Cup-bearer, why grieve over your companions' tomorrow? |
| پیش آر پیاله را که شب می‌گذرد | Bring the cup: the night is passing. |

دریاب is the imperative of دریافتن "to grasp"; غم خوردن "to eat grief" is "to worry". A quatrain, رباعی *robâ'i*, rhymes in lines one, two and four.
