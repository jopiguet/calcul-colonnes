# 3. Greetings, pronouns and "to be"

With the pronouns and the verb "to be" you can introduce yourself, ask how someone is and say where you are from. Persian puts the verb at the end of the sentence, has no grammatical gender (او is both "he" and "she") and uses the plural شما *shomâ* as the polite "you".

## Personal pronouns and "to be"

| Pronoun | Meaning | "to be", full form | Short form (after a word) |
|---|---|---|---|
| من *man* | I | هستم *hastam* | ـم / ـام *-am* |
| تو *to* | you (familiar) | هستی *hasti* | ـی / ـای *-i* |
| او *u* | he, she | است *ast*, هست *hast* | است *ast* (spoken *-e*) |
| ما *mâ* | we | هستیم *hastim* | ـیم *-im* |
| شما *shomâ* | you (plural, polite) | هستید *hastid* | ـید *-id* |
| آنها *ânhâ* | they | هستند *hastand* | ـند *-and* |

The short forms are the common ones: من ایرانی‌ام *man irâni-am* "I am Iranian", ما خسته‌ایم *mâ khaste-im* "we are tired". The negative is نیست- for every person: نیستم *nistam* "I am not", نیست *nist* "he is not".

## Greetings

| Persian | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| سلام | *salâm* | hello |
| حال شما چطور است؟ | *hâl-e shomâ chetor ast?* | how are you? |
| خوبم، ممنون | *khubam, mamnun* | I'm well, thank you |
| اسم من سارا است | *esm-e man Sârâ ast* | my name is Sara |
| اهل کجا هستید؟ | *ahl-e kojâ hastid?* | where are you from? |
| خداحافظ | *khodâhâfez* | goodbye |

The question mark is turned: ؟. Word order does not change in a question: the voice or a question word does the work.

::: summary
- من، تو، او، ما، شما، آنها; no gender: او is he and she; شما is also the polite "you".
- هستم، هستی، است، هستیم، هستید، هستند; short -am, -i, ast, -im, -id, -and; negative نیستم.
- سلام، خداحافظ، ممنون; the verb comes last; ؟ is the question mark.
:::
