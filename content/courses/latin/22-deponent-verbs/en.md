# 22. Deponent and semi-deponent verbs

Some Latin verbs have **passive forms but active meanings**: *hortor* "I urge", *sequor* "I follow", *loquor* "I speak". The Roman grammarians called them *verba dēpōnentia*, verbs that have "laid aside" their active forms. You already know every form they use: they are conjugated exactly like the passive. The only new thing is to translate them actively.

## Recognizing a deponent

The dictionary entry gives three principal parts, all passive: present, infinitive, perfect.

| Conjugation | Principal parts | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | hortor, hortārī, hortātus sum | to urge, encourage |
| 2nd | vereor, verērī, veritus sum | to fear, respect |
| 3rd | sequor, sequī, secūtus sum | to follow |
| 3rd -iō | patior, patī, passus sum | to suffer, allow |
| 4th | orior, orīrī, ortus sum | to rise |

| Tense | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Present | *sequitur* | he follows |
| Imperfect | *sequēbātur* | he was following |
| Future | *sequētur* | he will follow |
| Perfect | *secūtus est* | he followed |
| Present subjunctive | *sequātur* | (that) he may follow |
| Infinitive | *sequī* | to follow |

Three active forms survive, because the passive has nothing to offer in their place:

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Present participle | *sequēns, sequentis* | following |
| Future participle | *secūtūrus* | about to follow |
| Future infinitive | *secūtūrus esse* | to be going to follow |

The perfect participle of a deponent is **active** in meaning: *hortātus* "having urged", *secūtus* "having followed". This fills a gap: an ordinary verb has no perfect active participle (*amātus* is "having been loved"), but *Caesar, mīlitēs hortātus, …* means "Caesar, having encouraged the soldiers, …".

:::

## Five deponents with the ablative

*Ūtor, fruor, fungor, potior, vēscor* take the **ablative**, not the accusative. A Roman mnemonic: they describe "benefiting oneself by means of" something.

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ūtor, ūtī, ūsus sum* | use | *Gladiō ūtitur.* He uses a sword. |
| *fruor, fruī, frūctus sum* | enjoy | *Vītā fruimur.* We enjoy life. |
| *fungor, fungī, fūnctus sum* | perform | *Officiō fungitur.* He performs his duty. |
| *potior, potīrī, potītus sum* | gain possession of | *Urbe potītus est.* He took possession of the city. |
| *vēscor, vēscī* | feed on | *Lacte vēscuntur.* They live on milk. |

## Semi-deponents

A handful of verbs are normal in the present system but deponent in the perfect:

| Verb | Present | Perfect |
|---|---|---|
| *audeō, audēre, ausus sum* | *audet*, he dares | *ausus est*, he dared |
| *gaudeō, gaudēre, gāvīsus sum* | *gaudet*, he rejoices | *gāvīsus est*, he rejoiced |
| *soleō, solēre, solitus sum* | *solet*, he is accustomed | *solitus est*, he was accustomed |
| *fīdō, fīdere, fīsus sum* | *fīdit*, he trusts | *fīsus est*, he trusted |

- Hostēs ((urbem / urbe*)) potītī sunt. → take possession of
- Puerī ((patrem* / patre)) sequuntur. → follow

:::

::: summary
- Deponents: passive forms, active meaning. Principal parts *sequor, sequī, secūtus sum*.
- Active forms kept: present participle, future participle, future infinitive.
- The perfect participle of a deponent is active: *hortātus*, having urged.
- *Ūtor, fruor, fungor, potior, vēscor* + ablative.
- Semi-deponents: *audeō ausus sum, gaudeō gāvīsus sum, soleō solitus sum, fīdō fīsus sum*.
:::
