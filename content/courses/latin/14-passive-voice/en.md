# 14. The passive voice: present, imperfect, future

"The girl is loved", "the city was being attacked", "the letters will be sent": English builds the passive with "to be". Latin builds it, in these three tenses, with a second set of personal endings. Change the ending, and *amat* (he loves) becomes *amātur* (he is loved).

## Passive endings

| Person | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | -ō / -m | **-or / -r** |
| 2nd sg. | -s | **-ris** (-re) |
| 3rd sg. | -t | **-tur** |
| 1st pl. | -mus | **-mur** |
| 2nd pl. | -tis | **-minī** |
| 3rd pl. | -nt | **-ntur** |

## The present passive

| Person | amāre | monēre | regere | audīre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | am**or** | mone**or** | reg**or** | audi**or** |
| 2nd sg. | amā**ris** | monē**ris** | reg**eris** | audī**ris** |
| 3rd sg. | amā**tur** | monē**tur** | reg**itur** | audī**tur** |
| 1st pl. | amā**mur** | monē**mur** | reg**imur** | audī**mur** |
| 2nd pl. | amā**minī** | monē**minī** | reg**iminī** | audī**minī** |
| 3rd pl. | ama**ntur** | mone**ntur** | reg**untur** | audi**untur** |

The third conjugation has *-eris* in the second singular (not *-iris*). *Capiō* goes *capior, caperis, capitur, capimur, capiminī, capiuntur*.

## Imperfect and future passive

The imperfect simply swaps the endings: *amābar, amābāris, amābātur, amābāmur, amābāminī, amābantur*; *regēbar*, *audiēbar*.

The future does the same: first and second conjugations *amābor, amāberis, amābitur, amābimur, amābiminī, amābuntur*; third and fourth *regar, regēris, regētur, regēmur, regēminī, regentur*; *audiar, audiēris…*

Note two near-twins in the third conjugation: *regeris* (you are ruled, present) and *regēris* (you will be ruled, future): only the length of the *e* differs.

## The passive infinitive

| Conjugation | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | amāre | amā**rī** |
| 2nd | monēre | monē**rī** |
| 3rd | regere | reg**ī** |
| 3rd *-iō* | capere | cap**ī** |
| 4th | audīre | audī**rī** |

*Amārī vult*: he wants to be loved. The third conjugation drops the whole *-ere* and adds *-ī*.

## Agent and means

In a passive sentence the doer, if a person, stands in the ablative with **ā, ab**; a thing used as an instrument stands in the plain ablative:

| Latin | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Urbs ā mīlitibus capitur.* | The city is taken by the soldiers. (agent) |
| *Urbs gladiīs capitur.* | The city is taken with swords. (means) |
| *Puer ā mātre vocābātur.* | The boy was being called by his mother. |
| *Epistulae crās mittentur.* | The letters will be sent tomorrow. |

::: summary
- Passive endings: *-or/-r, -ris, -tur, -mur, -minī, -ntur*.
- Present *amor, amāris, amātur…*; imperfect *amābar…*; future *amābor, amāberis…* / *regar, regēris…*
- Passive infinitive: *amārī, monērī, regī, capī, audīrī*.
- Agent (person): *ā, ab* + ablative; means (thing): ablative alone.
:::
