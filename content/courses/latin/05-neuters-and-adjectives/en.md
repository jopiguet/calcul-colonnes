# 5. Neuter nouns, adjectives and *possum*

Latin has three genders: masculine, feminine and neuter. This lesson adds the neuter nouns of the second declension, then the adjectives that combine the first two declensions, and how they agree with their noun. It ends with *possum*, "I can", built on *sum*.

## Neuter nouns of the second declension

Neuters in *-um* decline like *dominus*, with two differences that hold for every neuter in Latin:

1. The nominative, accusative and vocative are always the same.
2. In the plural, these three cases end in **-a**.

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | bell**um** | bell**a** |
| Genitive | bell**ī** | bell**ōrum** |
| Dative | bell**ō** | bell**īs** |
| Accusative | bell**um** | bell**a** |
| Ablative | bell**ō** | bell**īs** |

So *bella* (wars) is plural, not first declension: the dictionary entry *bellum, bellī* (n.) settles it. Common neuters: *dōnum* (gift), *oppidum* (town), *perīculum* (danger), *verbum* (word), *cōnsilium* (plan, advice), *auxilium* (help), *caelum* (sky).

## Adjectives in *-us, -a, -um*

Many adjectives use the second declension for the masculine and neuter, and the first for the feminine. The dictionary lists the three nominatives: *bonus, bona, bonum* (good).

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | bon**us** | bon**a** | bon**um** |
| Gen. sg. | bon**ī** | bon**ae** | bon**ī** |
| Dat. sg. | bon**ō** | bon**ae** | bon**ō** |
| Acc. sg. | bon**um** | bon**am** | bon**um** |
| Abl. sg. | bon**ō** | bon**ā** | bon**ō** |
| Nom. pl. | bon**ī** | bon**ae** | bon**a** |
| Gen. pl. | bon**ōrum** | bon**ārum** | bon**ōrum** |
| Dat., abl. pl. | bon**īs** | bon**īs** | bon**īs** |
| Acc. pl. | bon**ōs** | bon**ās** | bon**a** |

Some have *-er* in the masculine, keeping the *e* (*līber, lībera, līberum*, free; *miser, misera, miserum*, wretched) or dropping it (*pulcher, pulchra, pulchrum*, beautiful; *noster, nostra, nostrum*, our).

## Agreement

An adjective agrees with its noun in **gender, number and case**, not necessarily in ending: *agricola bonus* (a good farmer: *agricola* is masculine, first declension, so the adjective is *bonus*, not *bona*); *nauta Rōmānus*. The adjective usually follows the noun; adjectives of size and quantity often come first (*magna silva*, *multī virī*).

An adjective can stand alone as a noun: *bonī* (good men), *multa* (many things), *Rōmānī* (the Romans).

The verb *sum* links a subject to an adjective that agrees with it: *Via longa est* (the road is long), *Puerī laetī sunt* (the boys are happy), *Bellum malum est*.

## *Possum*, "I can, I am able"

*Possum* is *pot-* (able) + *sum*: *pot-* becomes *pos-* before *s*. It takes an infinitive, as "can" does.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | pos**sum** | pos**sumus** |
| 2nd | pot**es** | pot**estis** |
| 3rd | pot**est** | pos**sunt** |

Infinitive: *posse*. *Labōrāre nōn possum*: I cannot work. *Vidēre possumus*: we can see.

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- *Oppidum magnum et pulchrum est.* → {{The town is large and beautiful. / The town is big and beautiful.}}
- *Multa dōna habēmus.* → {{We have many gifts.}}
- *Servī miserī labōrāre nōn possunt.* → {{The wretched slaves cannot work. / The poor slaves cannot work.}}
- *Cōnsilium tuum bonum est.* → {{Your plan is good. / Your advice is good.}}
:::

::: summary
- Neuters: nominative = accusative; plural in *-a* (*bellum, bella*).
- Adjectives *bonus, -a, -um*: 2nd declension for masculine and neuter, 1st for feminine; some in *-er* (*līber, pulcher*).
- Agreement in gender, number and case, not always in ending (*agricola bonus*).
- *Possum, potes, potest, possumus, potestis, possunt*; *posse* + infinitive.
:::
