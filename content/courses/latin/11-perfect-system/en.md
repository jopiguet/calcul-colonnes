# 11. The perfect system: perfect, pluperfect, future perfect

So far every tense came from the present stem. The past of completed actions, "I loved", "I have loved", "I had loved", "I shall have loved", comes from a second stem, the **perfect stem**, given by the third principal part. Once you know it, the three tenses are perfectly regular, in all four conjugations.

## Principal parts

A Latin verb is learnt with four principal parts: present, infinitive, perfect, supine (or perfect participle).

| Present | Infinitive | Perfect | Supine | Perfect stem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| amō | amāre | amāvī | amātum | amāv- |
| moneō | monēre | monuī | monitum | monu- |
| regō | regere | rēxī | rēctum | rēx- |
| capiō | capere | cēpī | captum | cēp- |
| audiō | audīre | audīvī | audītum | audīv- |
| sum | esse | fuī | futūrus | fu- |

The first and fourth conjugations are mostly regular (*-āvī*, *-īvī*). The second often has *-uī*. The third must be learnt verb by verb: *-sī* (*scrīpsī*, *dīxī* = *dīc-sī*), a long vowel (*vēnī*, *lēgī*, *fēcī*), a doubled syllable (*dedī*, *cucurrī*). The perfect stem is the perfect minus its *-ī*.

## The perfect

The perfect has endings of its own, the same for every verb:

| Person | Ending | amāre | regere |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | -ī | amāv**ī** | rēx**ī** |
| 2nd sg. | -istī | amāv**istī** | rēx**istī** |
| 3rd sg. | -it | amāv**it** | rēx**it** |
| 1st pl. | -imus | amāv**imus** | rēx**imus** |
| 2nd pl. | -istis | amāv**istis** | rēx**istis** |
| 3rd pl. | -ērunt | amāv**ērunt** | rēx**ērunt** |

The perfect tells a completed past action, "I loved", or its present result, "I have loved": *Vēnī, vīdī, vīcī*, "I came, I saw, I conquered". Contrast with the imperfect: *Caesar scrībēbat* (was writing, used to write), *Caesar scrīpsit* (wrote, has written).

## Pluperfect and future perfect

Both add the forms of *sum* to the perfect stem, with small changes:

| Person | Pluperfect (stem + *eram*) | Future perfect (stem + *erō*) |
|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | amāv**eram** I had loved | amāv**erō** I shall have loved |
| 2nd sg. | amāv**erās** | amāv**eris** |
| 3rd sg. | amāv**erat** | amāv**erit** |
| 1st pl. | amāv**erāmus** | amāv**erimus** |
| 2nd pl. | amāv**erātis** | amāv**eritis** |
| 3rd pl. | amāv**erant** | amāv**erint** |

The future perfect's third plural is *-erint*, not *-erunt*. Latin uses it precisely where English uses a present: *Sī vēneris, gaudēbō*, "if you come (will have come), I shall be glad".

::: summary
- Four principal parts: *amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum*; the perfect stem is the third minus *-ī*.
- Perfect: *-ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt*: a completed act ("I did", "I have done").
- Pluperfect: stem + *-eram…*; future perfect: stem + *-erō, -eris, -erit, -erimus, -eritis, -erint*.
- Third-conjugation perfects must be learnt: *rēxī, dīxī, vēnī, lēgī, fēcī, cēpī, dedī*.
:::
