# 29. Numbers and the Roman calendar

Numbers in Latin come in two series: **cardinals** (one, two, three) and **ordinals** (first, second, third). Only the first three cardinals, the hundreds and *mīlia* decline; the rest are fixed. Roman numerals and Roman dates follow their own logic, counting backwards from three fixed days in each month.

## Cardinals and ordinals

| Value | Numeral | Cardinal | Ordinal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I | ūnus, -a, -um | prīmus |
| 2 | II | duo, duae, duo | secundus |
| 3 | III | trēs, tria | tertius |
| 4 | IV | quattuor | quārtus |
| 5 | V | quīnque | quīntus |
| 6 | VI | sex | sextus |
| 7 | VII | septem | septimus |
| 8 | VIII | octō | octāvus |
| 9 | IX | novem | nōnus |
| 10 | X | decem | decimus |
| 20 | XX | vīgintī | vīcēsimus |
| 50 | L | quīnquāgintā | quīnquāgēsimus |
| 100 | C | centum | centēsimus |
| 500 | D | quīngentī, -ae, -a | quīngentēsimus |
| 1000 | M | mīlle | mīllēsimus |

*Duo* and *trēs* decline:

| Case | duo (m.) | duae (f.) | trēs (m./f.) | tria (n.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | duo | duae | trēs | tria |
| Genitive | duōrum | duārum | trium | trium |
| Dative | duōbus | duābus | tribus | tribus |
| Accusative | duōs | duās | trēs | tria |
| Ablative | duōbus | duābus | tribus | tribus |

*Mīlle* is an indeclinable adjective in the singular (*mīlle mīlitēs*); the plural *mīlia, mīlium* is a neuter noun with a partitive genitive: *tria mīlia mīlitum*, three thousand soldiers. *Mīlle passūs* (a thousand paces) is the Roman mile, about 1.48 km.

In numerals, a smaller sign before a larger one subtracts: IV = 4, IX = 9, XL = 40, XC = 90, CD = 400, CM = 900.

## The Roman calendar

Each month has three named days:

| Day | Name | Date |
|---|---|---|
| First of the month | *Kalendae* | the 1st |
| "Ninth" day before the Ides | *Nōnae* | the 5th, or the 7th in March, May, July, October |
| Middle of the month | *Īdūs* | the 13th, or the 15th in March, May, July, October |

Other days are counted **backwards** to the next named day, **inclusively** (both ends count): *ante diem tertium Kalendās Iānuāriās*, abbreviated *a.d. III Kal. Ian.*, is the third day before the Kalends of January, counting the Kalends: 30 December. The day just before is *prīdiē*: *prīdiē Īdūs Mārtiās*, 14 March.

Years were named after the two consuls (*M. Tulliō Cicerōne C. Antōniō cōnsulibus*, 63 BC) or counted *ab urbe conditā* (A.U.C.), from the founding of Rome in 753 BC.

The month names are adjectives agreeing with *Kalendae, Nōnae, Īdūs*: *Iānuārius, Februārius, Mārtius, Aprīlis, Māius, Iūnius, Iūlius* (formerly *Quīntīlis*), *Augustus* (formerly *Sextīlis*), *September, Octōber, November, December*. The last four betray an older year that began in March: *September* is the "seventh" month.

::: summary
- *Ūnus, duo, trēs* decline; *quattuor* to *centum* do not; hundreds decline like *bonī*; *mīlle* indeclinable, *mīlia* + genitive.
- Ordinals in *-us, -a, -um*: *prīmus, secundus, tertius, … decimus*.
- Numerals: a smaller sign before a larger one subtracts (IV, IX, XL, XC, CD, CM).
- *Kalendae* 1st, *Nōnae* 5th/7th, *Īdūs* 13th/15th; count backwards inclusively; *prīdiē* the day before.
:::
