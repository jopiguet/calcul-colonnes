# 4. The second declension: masculine nouns

The second declension holds most masculine nouns: *dominus* (master), *servus* (slave), *amīcus* (friend), *puer* (boy). Its genitive singular ends in **-ī**. This lesson gives its three patterns, *-us*, *-er* and *-ir*, and the vocative, the one case where the second declension surprises.

## Nouns in *-us*

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | domin**us** | domin**ī** |
| Genitive | domin**ī** | domin**ōrum** |
| Dative | domin**ō** | domin**īs** |
| Accusative | domin**um** | domin**ōs** |
| Ablative | domin**ō** | domin**īs** |
| Vocative | domin**e** | domin**ī** |

Compare with the first declension: the dative and ablative plural are the same (*-īs*), and the accusative singular again ends in *-m*. The genitive plural *-ōrum* matches *-ārum*.

The vocative singular of *-us* nouns ends in **-e**: *Marce!*, *amīce!* Nouns in *-ius* contract it to **-ī**: *fīlius* → *fīlī!* (my son!), *Gāius* → *Gāī!* Everywhere else, the vocative is the nominative.

## Nouns in *-er* and *-ir*

Some nouns end in *-er* in the nominative singular. Their other cases take the usual endings, added either to the whole word (*puer*, *puerī*) or to a shorter stem that drops the *e* (*ager*, *agrī*). The genitive, which the dictionary gives, tells you which.

| Case | puer, puerī (boy) | ager, agrī (field) | vir, virī (man) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | puer | ager | vir |
| Gen. sg. | puer**ī** | agr**ī** | vir**ī** |
| Acc. sg. | puer**um** | agr**um** | vir**um** |
| Abl. sg. | puer**ō** | agr**ō** | vir**ō** |
| Nom. pl. | puer**ī** | agr**ī** | vir**ī** |
| Acc. pl. | puer**ōs** | agr**ōs** | vir**ōs** |

Like *puer*: *socer* (father-in-law), *līberī* (children, plural only). Like *ager*: *magister, magistrī* (teacher), *liber, librī* (book). *Vir* has its own form, and its compounds follow it: *triumvir*.

## Subject and object together

Now both declensions can meet in one sentence. Read the endings before the order:

| Latin | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Dominus servum vocat.* | The master calls the slave. |
| *Servum dominus vocat.* | The master calls the slave. |
| *Puerī magistrum timent.* | The boys fear the teacher. |
| *Fīlia amīcō librum dat.* | The daughter gives a book to the friend. |
| *Marce, cūr nōn labōrās?* | Marcus, why are you not working? |

::: exercise who-does-what
Who does the action? Read the endings.

- *Servum dominus laudat.* → ((the slave / the master*))
- *Puellās puerī vident.* → ((the girls / the boys*))
- *Agricolam virī vocant.* → ((the farmer / the men*))
- *Amīcōs fīlia exspectat.* → ((the friends / the daughter*))
:::

::: summary
- Second declension: genitive singular in -ī; mostly masculine.
- *-us*: -us, -ī, -ō, -um, -ō, voc. -e; -ī, -ōrum, -īs, -ōs, -īs.
- *-er* nouns keep the *e* (*puer, puerī*) or drop it (*ager, agrī*): the genitive tells. *Vir, virī* stands alone.
- Vocative: *-e* for *-us* (*Marce*), *-ī* for *-ius* (*fīlī*); otherwise like the nominative.
:::
