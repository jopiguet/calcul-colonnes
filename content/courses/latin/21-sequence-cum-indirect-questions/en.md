# 21. Sequence of tenses, cum clauses, indirect questions

The subjunctive has four tenses. In a subordinate clause the choice is not free: it follows the **sequence of tenses**, a strict rule linking the tense of the main verb to the tense of the subjunctive. This lesson completes the forms with the perfect and pluperfect, states the rule, and applies it to two very frequent clauses: ***cum* + subjunctive** and **indirect questions**.

## The perfect and pluperfect subjunctive

| Person | Perfect active | Pluperfect active | Perfect passive | Pluperfect passive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | amāv**erim** | amāv**issem** | amātus **sim** | amātus **essem** |
| 2nd sg. | amāv**eris** | amāv**issēs** | amātus **sīs** | amātus **essēs** |
| 3rd sg. | amāv**erit** | amāv**isset** | amātus **sit** | amātus **esset** |
| 1st pl. | amāv**erimus** | amāv**issēmus** | amātī **sīmus** | amātī **essēmus** |
| 2nd pl. | amāv**eritis** | amāv**issētis** | amātī **sītis** | amātī **essētis** |
| 3rd pl. | amāv**erint** | amāv**issent** | amātī **sint** | amātī **essent** |

Both active tenses are built on the perfect stem: *rēx-erim, rēx-issem; cēp-erim, cēp-issem; fu-erim, fu-issem*. The pluperfect subjunctive is the perfect infinitive + endings (*amāvisse-m*). The perfect subjunctive looks like the future perfect except in the first person singular (*amāverim* / *amāverō*).

## The sequence of tenses

| Main verb | Same time | Earlier |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary**: present, future, future perfect | present subjunctive | perfect subjunctive |
| **Secondary**: imperfect, perfect, pluperfect | imperfect subjunctive | pluperfect subjunctive |

Purpose and result clauses (lesson 20) already followed it: *venit ut videat*, *vēnit ut vidēret*.

## Cum + subjunctive

With the subjunctive, *cum* is a conjunction (not the preposition) and means "when, since, although". In narrative it usually stands with the imperfect or pluperfect subjunctive:

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| *Cum Caesar in Galliā esset, hostēs bellum parābant.* | When Caesar was in Gaul, the enemy were preparing war. |
| *Cum Caesar vēnisset, hostēs fūgērunt.* | When Caesar had come, the enemy fled. |
| *Cum dīves sit, nōn est fēlīx.* | Although he is rich, he is not happy. |
| *Quae cum ita sint, discēdite.* | Since this is so, go away. |

*Cum* with the **indicative** marks pure time ("at the time when, whenever"): *Cum Rōmae sum, forum vīsō*. A *tamen* in the main clause signals "although".

## Indirect questions

A question that depends on a verb of asking, saying, knowing or perceiving takes the **subjunctive**, following the sequence of tenses. The question word stays.

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| *Quid facis?* | *Rogō quid faciās.* I ask what you are doing. |
| *Quid fēcistī?* | *Rogō quid fēcerīs.* I ask what you did. |
| *Quid facis?* | *Rogāvī quid facerēs.* I asked what you were doing. |
| *Quid fēcistī?* | *Rogāvī quid fēcissēs.* I asked what you had done. |
| *Venitne?* | *Nesciō num veniat.* I do not know whether he is coming. |

Yes-or-no questions are introduced by *num* or *-ne*, "whether"; alternatives by *utrum … an*, "whether … or".

A future in an indirect question uses the future participle + *sim* or *essem*: *Nesciō quid factūrus sīs*, I do not know what you will do.

::: summary
- Perfect subjunctive *amāverim, amātus sim*; pluperfect *amāvissem, amātus essem*.
- Sequence: primary main verb → present (same time) or perfect (earlier); secondary → imperfect or pluperfect.
- *Cum* + subjunctive: when, since, although (*tamen* signals although). *Cum* + indicative: pure time.
- Indirect questions: question word + subjunctive; *num / -ne* whether; future with *-ūrus sim / essem*.
:::
