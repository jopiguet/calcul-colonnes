# 12. The relative pronoun and questions

"The man **who** came", "the book **that** I read", "**whose** house": Latin uses one pronoun for all of these, *quī, quae, quod*. Its rule is simple and strict: it takes its gender and number from the word it refers to, and its case from its own clause. The same forms, almost, ask questions: *quis?* who?, *quid?* what?

## *Quī, quae, quod*

| Case | Masc. | Fem. | Neut. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | quī | quae | quod |
| Gen. sg. | cuius | cuius | cuius |
| Dat. sg. | cui | cui | cui |
| Acc. sg. | quem | quam | quod |
| Abl. sg. | quō | quā | quō |
| Nom. pl. | quī | quae | quae |
| Gen. pl. | quōrum | quārum | quōrum |
| Dat., abl. pl. | quibus | quibus | quibus |
| Acc. pl. | quōs | quās | quae |

## Agreement: two sources

The relative agrees with its **antecedent** (the word it refers to) in gender and number; its **case** depends on its job in the relative clause.

| Latin | Antecedent | Job of the relative | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Puella quae cantat Iūlia est.* | puella: f. sg. | subject: nom. | The girl who is singing is Julia. |
| *Puella quam vidēs Iūlia est.* | puella: f. sg. | object: acc. | The girl whom you see is Julia. |
| *Vir cuius domum vidēs rēx est.* | vir: m. sg. | owner: gen. | The man whose house you see is the king. |
| *Amīcī quibus librum dedī abiērunt.* | amīcī: m. pl. | to whom: dat. | The friends to whom I gave the book have left. |
| *Gladius quō pugnābat novus erat.* | gladius: m. sg. | with which: abl. | The sword he was fighting with was new. |

The relative often stands with *cum* attached: *quōcum*, *quibuscum* (with whom).

::: exercise relative
Choose the right form of the relative.

- *Puer, … laudās, meus fīlius est.* (whom) → ((quī / quem* / cuius))
- *Urbs, … mūrī altī sunt, Rōma est.* (whose) → ((quae / quam / cuius*))
- *Verba … dīxistī bona erant.* (which, object) → ((quae* / quās / quōs))
- *Fēminae … dōna dedimus laetae sunt.* (to whom) → ((quās / quibus* / quārum))
- *Oppidum … vīdimus magnum erat.* (which, object) → ((quem / quam / quod*))
- *Mīlitēs … in castrīs erant fūgērunt.* (who) → ((quī* / quōs / quae))
:::

## Questions

The interrogative pronoun *quis, quid* (who? what?) has the relative's forms, except in the nominative singular (*quis*, *quid*) and the neuter accusative (*quid*). Before a noun, the interrogative adjective is just *quī, quae, quod*: *Quem librum legis?* "Which book are you reading?"

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Quis venit?* | Who is coming? |
| *Quid dīcis?* | What are you saying? |
| *Cuius est hic liber?* | Whose book is this? |
| *Cui hoc dabis?* | To whom will you give this? |
| *Quōcum ambulās?* | With whom are you walking? |

Other question words: *ubi?* (where?), *quō?* (where to?), *unde?* (from where?), *quandō?* (when?), *cūr?* (why?), *quōmodo?* (how?), *quot?* (how many?). Yes-or-no questions use *-ne* (open), *nōnne* (expecting yes) or *num* (expecting no): *Nōnne venīs?* "You're coming, aren't you?"; *Num timēs?* "Surely you aren't afraid?"

::: summary
- *Quī, quae, quod*: gender and number from the antecedent, case from its own clause.
- Genitive *cuius*, dative *cui*; *quōcum, quibuscum*.
- *Quis? quid?* who? what?; *quī, quae, quod* + noun: which?
- *-ne* (open), *nōnne* (yes expected), *num* (no expected); *ubi, quō, unde, quandō, cūr, quōmodo, quot*.
:::
