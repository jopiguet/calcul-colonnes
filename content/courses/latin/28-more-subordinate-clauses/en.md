# 28. More subordinate clauses

With purpose, result, *cum*, indirect questions and conditions, you can read most Latin sentences. A few more clause types complete the picture. Each has a small set of introducing words, and each decides between indicative and subjunctive for a reason: the subjunctive marks what is feared, intended, characteristic or merely alleged, the indicative what is stated as fact.

## Fearing, preventing, doubting

| Clause | Introduced by | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fear that | *timeō nē* + subj. | *Timeō nē veniat.* | I fear that he will come. |
| Fear that not | *timeō ut* (or *nē nōn*) + subj. | *Timeō ut veniat.* | I fear that he will not come. |
| Preventing | *impediō nē / quōminus* + subj. | *Impedit quōminus abeam.* | He prevents me from leaving. |
| Not doubting | *nōn dubitō quīn* + subj. | *Nōn dubitō quīn vērum sit.* | I do not doubt that it is true. |
| Not preventing | *nōn impediō quīn* + subj. | *Nōn impediō quīn abeās.* | I do not stop you from leaving. |

The fear clause reverses English: *nē* is positive ("that"), *ut* negative ("that … not"). Think of *nē* as a wish turned around: "I fear; may he not come!"

## Relative clauses with the subjunctive

A relative clause takes the subjunctive when it describes a **kind** rather than a fact (relative clause of characteristic), or when it expresses purpose, cause or concession.

| Type | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Characteristic | *Sunt quī dīcant…* | There are people who say (people of the kind who say)… |
| Characteristic | *Nēmō est quī nesciat.* | There is no one who does not know. |
| Characteristic | *dignus quī laudētur* | worthy to be praised |
| Purpose | *Lēgātōs mīsit quī pācem peterent.* | He sent envoys to ask for peace. |
| Cause | *Ō fortūnāte adulēscēns, quī tuae virtūtis Homērum praecōnem invēnerīs!* | O fortunate young man, since you found Homer as herald of your valour! |

## Causal, concessive and temporal clauses

| Conjunction | Mood | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *quod, quia, quoniam* | indicative (the speaker's reason) | because, since |
| *quod* + subjunctive | subjunctive (someone else's reason) | because, as he said |
| *quamquam* | indicative | although (fact) |
| *quamvīs, licet* | subjunctive | however much, although |
| *etsī, etiamsī* | as in conditions | even if |
| *postquam, ubi, ut, simul ac* | perfect indicative | after, when, as soon as |
| *dum* + present indicative | indicative | while |
| *dum, dōnec, quoad* + subjunctive | subjunctive (expectation) | until |
| *antequam, priusquam* | indicative (fact) or subjunctive (expectation) | before |

*Athēniēnsēs Aristīdem expulērunt quod nimis iūstus esset.* The Athenians (so they said) expelled Aristides because he was too just. The subjunctive shows that the reason is theirs, not the writer's.

*Dum haec geruntur, Caesar advēnit.* While this was going on, Caesar arrived. After *dum* "while", Latin keeps the **present** indicative even in a past narrative.

::: exercise fear
*Nē* or *ut*?

- Timeō [[nē]] hostēs veniant. → I fear that the enemy will come
- Timeō [[ut]] amīcus veniat. → I fear that my friend will not come
- Verēbantur [[nē]] urbs caperētur. → they feared that the city would be taken
- Metuit [[ut]] satis cibī habeant. → he fears that they do not have enough food
:::

::: exercise mood
Indicative or subjunctive? Choose the form.

- Nēmō est quī hoc ((scit / sciat*)). → there is no one who knows this
- Dum Rōmānī ((dēlīberant* / dēlīberent)), Saguntum captum est. → while the Romans deliberated
- Quamquam fessus ((erat* / esset)), labōrābat. → although he was tired
- Exspectā dum ((redit / redeat*)). → wait until he comes back
- Nōn dubitō quīn ((venit / veniat*)). → I do not doubt that he is coming
:::

::: exercise conjunction
Which conjunction fits?

- because (my reason) → ((quod* / quamvīs / nē))
- however much → ((quoniam / quamvīs* / postquam))
- as soon as → ((simul ac* / quīn / dōnec))
- before → ((priusquam* / quoad / quia))
:::

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- *Timēbant nē Caesar redīret.* → {{They were afraid that Caesar would return. / They feared that Caesar might come back.}}
- *Sunt quī putent deōs nōn esse.* → {{There are people who think that the gods do not exist. / Some think that there are no gods.}}
- *Postquam urbem cēpit, domum rediit.* → {{After he took the city, he returned home. / After he had captured the city, he went back home.}}
- *Nōn dubitō quīn tū vērum dīcās.* → {{I do not doubt that you are telling the truth. / I have no doubt that you speak the truth.}}
:::

::: summary
- Fear: *nē* that, *ut* that … not. Preventing: *nē, quōminus*; negative doubting and preventing: *quīn*.
- Relative + subjunctive: characteristic (*sunt quī dīcant*), purpose, cause.
- *Quod, quia, quoniam* + indicative; *quod* + subjunctive = reason reported. *Quamquam* + indicative; *quamvīs, licet* + subjunctive.
- *Postquam, ubi, simul ac* + perfect indicative; *dum* while + present indicative; *dum, antequam* + subjunctive = until, before (expected).
:::
