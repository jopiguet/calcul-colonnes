# 18. Infinitives and indirect statement

"He says that the enemy are coming." English reports speech and thought with *that* and a normal clause. Latin does not: after verbs of saying, thinking, knowing and perceiving it puts the subject in the **accusative** and the verb in the **infinitive**: *Dīcit hostēs venīre*, literally "he says the enemy to come". This construction, the **accusative and infinitive**, is everywhere in prose. To read it you need all six infinitives.

## The six infinitives

| Infinitive | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Present | amā**re** | amā**rī** |
| Perfect | amāv**isse** | amātus, -a, -um **esse** |
| Future | amātūrus, -a, -um **esse** | amātum **īrī** (rare) |

| Conjugation | Present active | Present passive | Perfect active |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st amō | amāre | amārī | amāvisse |
| 2nd moneō | monēre | monērī | monuisse |
| 3rd regō | regere | regī | rēxisse |
| 3rd -iō capiō | capere | capī | cēpisse |
| 4th audiō | audīre | audīrī | audīvisse |
| sum | esse | — | fuisse |

The third-conjugation present passive drops the whole *-er-*: *regī, capī, mittī*, not *regerī*. The future infinitive of *sum* is *futūrus esse*, often shortened to *fore*.

## The accusative and infinitive

The head verb (*dīcō, putō, sciō, audiō, videō, nūntiō, crēdō, spērō, negō*) is followed by an accusative subject and an infinitive. The infinitive's tense is **relative** to the head verb:

| Infinitive | Relation | Latin | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present | same time | *Dīcit hostēs venīre.* | He says that the enemy are coming. |
| Perfect | before | *Dīcit hostēs vēnisse.* | He says that the enemy have come. |
| Future | after | *Dīcit hostēs ventūrōs esse.* | He says that the enemy will come. |
| Present | same time | *Dīxit hostēs venīre.* | He said that the enemy were coming. |
| Perfect | before | *Dīxit hostēs vēnisse.* | He said that the enemy had come. |
| Future | after | *Dīxit hostēs ventūrōs esse.* | He said that the enemy would come. |

The participle inside a compound infinitive agrees with the accusative subject: *Dīcit urbem captam esse* (that the city has been taken), *puellās ventūrās esse*.

"Himself, herself" in the reported clause, referring to the speaker, is the reflexive *sē*; anyone else is *eum, eam, eōs*: *Mārcus dīcit sē aegrum esse* (Marcus says that he, Marcus, is ill); *Mārcus dīcit eum aegrum esse* (that he, someone else, is ill).

*Negō* means "I say that … not": *Negat sē vēnisse*, he says that he did not come.

::: summary
- Infinitives: present *amāre / amārī*; perfect *amāvisse / amātus esse*; future *amātūrus esse*. Third-conjugation present passive *regī*.
- Verbs of saying, thinking, knowing, perceiving take the accusative and infinitive.
- The infinitive's tense is relative: present = same time, perfect = before, future = after.
- *Sē* refers to the speaker; *negō* = say that … not.
:::
