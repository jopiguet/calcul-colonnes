# 27. Uses of the cases; place and time

You have met each case with its basic job: subject, possession, indirect object, direct object, means. Real texts use them for much more. This lesson gathers the uses you will meet most often, then settles two questions every reader asks: how Latin says **where** and **when**.

## The genitive and the dative

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Genitive of possession | *domus patris* | the father's house |
| Partitive genitive | *pars mīlitum*, *satis pecūniae* | part of the soldiers, enough money |
| Genitive of description | *vir magnae virtūtis* | a man of great courage |
| Objective genitive | *amor patriae*, *metus mortis* | love for one's country, fear of death |
| Dative of possession | *Mihi est liber.* | I have a book. |
| Dative of purpose | *Hoc mihi cūrae est.* | This is a concern to me. |
| Dative of reference | *Mihi pulcher vidētur.* | He seems handsome to me. |
| Dative with adjectives | *amīcus Caesarī*, *similis patrī* | friendly to Caesar, like his father |
| Dative with special verbs | *Tibi crēdō. Mihi persuāsit.* | I believe you. He persuaded me. |

The dative of purpose often pairs with a dative of reference, the "double dative": *Caesar duās legiōnēs subsidiō castrīs relīquit*, Caesar left two legions as support for the camp.

## The accusative and the ablative

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Accusative of extent (space, time) | *tria mīlia passuum*, *multōs annōs* | three miles, for many years |
| Accusative of exclamation | *Ō fortūnātam Rōmam!* | O fortunate Rome! |
| Ablative of means | *gladiō pugnat* | he fights with a sword |
| Ablative of manner | *magnā cum cūrā*, *summā dīligentiā* | with great care, with the utmost diligence |
| Ablative of cause | *timōre fūgit* | he fled out of fear |
| Ablative of respect | *nōmine rēx* | king in name |
| Ablative of degree of difference | *paulō post*, *multō maior* | a little later, much greater |
| Ablative of description | *vir summā prūdentiā* | a man of the greatest wisdom |
| Ablative of separation | *līberāre metū* | to free from fear |

## Place: where, where to, where from

| Question | General rule | Towns, small islands, *domus*, *rūs* |
|---|---|---|
| Where? | *in* + ablative: *in urbe* | **locative**: *Rōmae*, *Athēnīs*, *domī*, *rūrī* |
| Where to? | *in, ad* + accusative: *in urbem* | **accusative alone**: *Rōmam*, *Athēnās*, *domum*, *rūs* |
| Where from? | *ā, ē, ex* + ablative: *ex urbe* | **ablative alone**: *Rōmā*, *Athēnīs*, *domō*, *rūre* |

The **locative** looks like the genitive in the first and second declensions singular (*Rōmae, Corinthī*) and like the ablative elsewhere (*Athēnīs, Carthāgine*). It is a remnant of an old eighth case.

## Time: when, within, how long

| Question | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| When? | ablative | *prīmā lūce*, *eō diē* |
| Within what time? | ablative | *tribus diēbus*, within three days |
| How long? | accusative | *trēs diēs*, for three days |
| How long ago? | *abhinc* + accusative | *abhinc annōs decem*, ten years ago |

::: summary
- Genitive: possession, partitive (*pars mīlitum*), description, objective (*amor patriae*).
- Dative: possession (*mihi est*), purpose (*cūrae*), reference, with adjectives and special verbs.
- Accusative: extent of space and time. Ablative: means, manner, cause, respect, degree of difference, description, separation.
- Towns, *domus*, *rūs*: locative (*Rōmae, domī*), accusative of goal (*Rōmam*), ablative of origin (*Rōmā*), no preposition.
- Time when and within: ablative; how long: accusative.
:::
