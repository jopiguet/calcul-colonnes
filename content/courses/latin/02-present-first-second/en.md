# 2. The present tense: first and second conjugations

A Latin verb says who acts by its ending alone: *amat* is "he loves", "she loves" or "it loves", with no pronoun needed. In this lesson you learn the six personal endings, the present tense of the first two groups of verbs, and the verb *sum*, "I am".

## Personal endings

Every active verb form ends in one of six endings. Learn them once: they come back in almost every tense.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | -ō / -m (I) | -mus (we) |
| 2nd | -s (you) | -tis (you, several) |
| 3rd | -t (he, she, it) | -nt (they) |

## The first conjugation: *amāre*

Verbs whose infinitive ends in **-āre** form the first conjugation. Take away *-re* and you have the stem, *amā-*, to which the endings are added. The *ā* shortens before *-t* and *-nt*, and disappears before *-ō*.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | am**ō** I love | amā**mus** we love |
| 2nd | amā**s** you love | amā**tis** you love |
| 3rd | ama**t** he, she loves | ama**nt** they love |

## The second conjugation: *monēre*

Verbs in **-ēre** form the second conjugation: stem *monē-*. The *ē* stays before *-ō*, which is how you tell *moneō* (second) from *amō* (first).

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | mone**ō** I warn | monē**mus** we warn |
| 2nd | monē**s** you warn | monē**tis** you warn |
| 3rd | mone**t** he, she warns | mone**nt** they warn |

The Latin present covers both English presents: *ambulat* is "she walks" and "she is walking".

## The verb *sum*

*Sum*, "I am", is irregular in every language, and Latin is no exception. It is the most frequent verb of all: learn it by heart.

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | sum I am | sumus we are |
| 2nd | es you are | estis you are |
| 3rd | est he, she, it is; there is | sunt they are; there are |

## Sentences: no pronoun, *nōn*, and questions

- No subject pronoun is needed: *labōrō*, "I work". A noun subject agrees in number: *Marcus labōrat*, *Marcus et Iūlia labōrant*.
- *Nōn* goes just before the verb: *nōn rīdent*, "they are not laughing".
- A yes-or-no question adds **-ne** to its first word: *Vidēsne?* "Do you see?"
- The verb usually stands last: *Iūlia saepe cantat*, "Julia often sings".

| Latin | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Cōgitō.* | I think. |
| *Nōn timēmus.* | We are not afraid. |
| *Ambulatisne?* | Are you (several) walking? |
| *Marcus et Iūlia rīdent.* | Marcus and Julia are laughing. |
| *Ubi es?* | Where are you? |

::: summary
- Six personal endings: -ō/-m, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt.
- First conjugation: infinitive -āre, stem amā-; second: -ēre, stem monē- (*moneō* keeps its *e*).
- *Sum*: sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt.
- No pronoun needed; *nōn* before the verb; *-ne* asks a question.
:::
