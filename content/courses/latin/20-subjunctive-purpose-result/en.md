# 20. The subjunctive: purpose and result

Until now every verb has been in the indicative, the mood of facts. The **subjunctive** is the mood of the intended, the possible, the dependent. Its main use is in subordinate clauses, and two of them come first because they are everywhere: **purpose** ("in order that") and **result** ("so that, with the result that"). This lesson gives the two simple tenses, present and imperfect.

## The present subjunctive

The vowel changes. A mnemonic for the characteristic vowel: *w**e** f**ea**r **a** l**ia**r* (1st *-e-*, 2nd *-ea-*, 3rd *-a-*, 3rd -iō and 4th *-ia-*).

| Person | 1st amō | 2nd moneō | 3rd regō | 3rd -iō capiō | 4th audiō | sum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | am**em** | mone**am** | reg**am** | capi**am** | audi**am** | sim |
| 2nd sg. | am**ēs** | mone**ās** | reg**ās** | capi**ās** | audi**ās** | sīs |
| 3rd sg. | am**et** | mone**at** | reg**at** | capi**at** | audi**at** | sit |
| 1st pl. | am**ēmus** | mone**āmus** | reg**āmus** | capi**āmus** | audi**āmus** | sīmus |
| 2nd pl. | am**ētis** | mone**ātis** | reg**ātis** | capi**ātis** | audi**ātis** | sītis |
| 3rd pl. | am**ent** | mone**ant** | reg**ant** | capi**ant** | audi**ant** | sint |

The passive takes the passive endings: *amer, amēris, amētur, amēmur, amēminī, amentur*. *Possum* follows *sum*: *possim, possīs, possit*.

Careful: *regam* is both the present subjunctive and the future indicative in the first person singular; *regēs, reget* are only future, *regās, regat* only subjunctive.

## The imperfect subjunctive

The easiest of all: the present active infinitive + the personal endings.

| Person | amō | regō | audiō | sum | possum |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | amāre**m** | regere**m** | audīre**m** | esse**m** | posse**m** |
| 2nd sg. | amārē**s** | regerē**s** | audīrē**s** | essē**s** | possē**s** |
| 3rd sg. | amāre**t** | regere**t** | audīre**t** | esse**t** | posse**t** |
| 1st pl. | amārē**mus** | regerē**mus** | audīrē**mus** | essē**mus** | possē**mus** |
| 2nd pl. | amārē**tis** | regerē**tis** | audīrē**tis** | essē**tis** | possē**tis** |
| 3rd pl. | amāre**nt** | regere**nt** | audīre**nt** | esse**nt** | posse**nt** |

Passive: *amārer, amārēris, amārētur…*. Which tense to use depends on the main verb: present subjunctive after a present or future main verb, imperfect after a past one (the full rule comes in the next lesson).

## Purpose clauses

*Ut* + subjunctive, "in order that, (so as) to"; the negative is ***nē***, "so that … not, lest". English often uses an infinitive.

| Latin | English |
|---|---|
| *Venit ut videat.* | He comes to see. |
| *Vēnit ut vidēret.* | He came to see. |
| *Fugiunt nē capiantur.* | They flee so as not to be captured. |
| *Mīlitēs mīsit quī urbem dēfenderent.* | He sent soldiers to defend the city (relative of purpose). |

Latin never uses a bare infinitive for purpose: "he came to see" is *vēnit ut vidēret*, never *vēnit vidēre*.

## Result clauses

*Ut* + subjunctive after a signal word in the main clause, "so … that"; the negative is ***ut nōn***.

| Signal word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *tam* | so (with adjectives and adverbs) |
| *ita, sīc* | in such a way, so |
| *tantus, -a, -um* | so great |
| *tot* | so many |
| *tālis, -e* | of such a kind |
| *adeō* | to such a degree |

*Tam fortis erat ut omnēs eum laudārent.* He was so brave that everyone praised him.
*Tantus erat metus ut nēmō exīret.* The fear was so great that no one went out.
*Ita cucurrit ut nōn caperētur.* He ran in such a way that he was not caught.

Purpose or result? The negative tells: *nē* is purpose, *ut nōn* is result; and a signal word announces a result.

::: summary
- Present subjunctive: *amem, moneam, regam, capiam, audiam; sim*. Vowel *e / ea / a / ia*.
- Imperfect subjunctive: infinitive + endings: *amārem, regerem, essem, possem*.
- Purpose: *ut / nē* + subjunctive; never a bare infinitive. Also *quī* + subjunctive.
- Result: signal word (*tam, ita, tantus, tot, tālis, adeō*) + *ut / ut nōn*.
:::
