# 24. Commands, wishes and the independent subjunctive

The subjunctive is not only for subordinate clauses. Standing alone in a main clause, it expresses what the speaker wants, fears or imagines: "let us go", "may the gods help you", "what am I to do?", "someone might say". This lesson brings together all the ways Latin gives an order, forbids something, urges or wishes.

## The imperative

| Conjugation | Singular | Plural | Passive / deponent sg. | Passive / deponent pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st amō | amā | amāte | hortāre | hortāminī |
| 2nd moneō | monē | monēte | verēre | verēminī |
| 3rd regō | rege | regite | sequere | sequiminī |
| 3rd -iō capiō | cape | capite | patere | patiminī |
| 4th audiō | audī | audīte | orīre | orīminī |
| sum | es | este | — | — |
| eō, ferō | ī, fer | īte, ferte | — | — |

Four verbs drop the final *-e* in the singular: ***dīc, dūc, fac, fer*** (say, lead, do, carry). The deponent imperative looks like an active infinitive: *sequere!* "follow!", *loquere!* "speak!". A future imperative in *-tō, -tōte* appears in laws and recipes: *estō* "let him be", *mementō* "remember".

## Negative commands

Latin does not use *nōn* + imperative. It has two standard forms:

| Construction | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *nōlī, nōlīte* + infinitive (polite) | *Nōlī timēre.* | Do not be afraid. |
| *nē* + perfect subjunctive (firm) | *Nē timueris.* | Do not fear. |

## The independent subjunctive

| Use | Negative | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hortatory** (1st pl.) | *nē* | *Eāmus!* | Let us go! |
| **Jussive** (3rd person) | *nē* | *Veniat.* | Let him come. |
| **Deliberative** (question) | *nōn* | *Quid faciam?* | What am I to do? |
| **Optative**, possible wish | *nē* | *Utinam veniat!* | May he come! I hope he comes. |
| **Optative**, unreal present | *nē* | *Utinam adesset!* | If only he were here! |
| **Optative**, unreal past | *nē* | *Utinam vēnisset!* | If only he had come! |
| **Potential** | *nōn* | *Dīcat aliquis…* | Someone may say… |

The tense of a wish shows whether it can still come true: present subjunctive = possible; imperfect = impossible now; pluperfect = impossible in the past. *Utinam* "if only" often introduces a wish; *Vīvat rēx!* "Long live the king!" needs none.

::: exercise which-use
Name the use of the subjunctive.

- *Gaudeāmus igitur!* → ((hortatory* / deliberative / potential))

- *Utinam nē vēnissent!* → ((jussive / deliberative / optative*))
- *Dī tē servent!* → ((deliberative / optative* / potential))
- *Cōnsulēs videant nē quid rēs pūblica dētrīmentī capiat.* → ((jussive* / deliberative / potential))
:::

::: exercise wish
Choose the tense that fits the wish.

- If only he were here now (he is not) → Utinam ((adsit / adesset* / adfuisset))!

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- *Nōlīte fugere, mīlitēs!* → {{Do not flee, soldiers! / Don't run away, soldiers!}}
- *Pugnēmus prō patriā!* → {{Let us fight for our country! / Let's fight for the fatherland!}}
- *Utinam pater meus vīveret!* → {{If only my father were alive! / I wish my father were alive!}}
- *Quid dīcam?* → {{What am I to say? / What should I say?}}
:::

::: summary
- Imperative: *amā, amāte; rege, regite; audī, audīte*; *dīc, dūc, fac, fer*; deponent *sequere, sequiminī*.
- Prohibition: *nōlī / nōlīte* + infinitive, or *nē* + perfect subjunctive.
- Hortatory *eāmus*; jussive *veniat*; deliberative *quid faciam?*; potential *dīcat aliquis*.
- Wishes, often with *utinam*: present possible, imperfect unreal present, pluperfect unreal past; negative *nē*.
:::
