# 19. Comparison of adjectives and adverbs

"Longer, longest; more quickly, most quickly." Latin builds the comparative and the superlative with suffixes, for adjectives and adverbs alike. A small group of very common words is irregular, as in English (*good, better, best*), and those must be learned by heart.

## Regular comparison

| Degree | Suffix | longus | fortis | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | — | longus, -a, -um | fortis, -e | long, brave |
| Comparative | stem + *-ior* (m./f.), *-ius* (n.) | longior, longius | fortior, fortius | longer, braver |
| Superlative | stem + *-issimus, -a, -um* | longissimus | fortissimus | longest, very long |

The stem is the genitive minus its ending: *long-ī → long-*, *fort-is → fort-*, *fēlīc-is → fēlīc-*.

The comparative is a third-declension adjective of two endings, but **not** an i-stem: ablative singular *-e*, genitive plural *-um*, neuter plural *-a*.

| Case | m./f. sg. | n. sg. | m./f. pl. | n. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | longior | longius | longiōrēs | longiōra |
| Genitive | longiōris | longiōris | longiōrum | longiōrum |
| Dative | longiōrī | longiōrī | longiōribus | longiōribus |
| Accusative | longiōrem | longius | longiōrēs | longiōra |
| Ablative | longiōre | longiōre | longiōribus | longiōribus |

Two groups shift the superlative:

- adjectives in *-er* add *-rimus* to the nominative: *miser → miserrimus*, *pulcher → pulcherrimus*, *ācer → ācerrimus*;
- six adjectives in *-lis* add *-limus*: *facilis → facillimus*, also *difficilis, similis, dissimilis, gracilis, humilis*.

## Irregular comparison

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|
| bonus, good | melior, melius | optimus |
| malus, bad | peior, peius | pessimus |
| magnus, great | maior, maius | maximus |
| parvus, small | minor, minus | minimus |
| multus, much | plūs (n. noun, + genitive) | plūrimus |

English has kept these in its own words: *ameliorate, pejorative, major, minor, plural, optimum, maximum, minimum*.

## How to say "than"

Two constructions, both common:

| Construction | Latin | English |
|---|---|---|
| *quam* + the same case | *Mārcus est altior quam Tullia.* | Marcus is taller than Tullia. |
| ablative of comparison (no *quam*) | *Mārcus est altior Tulliā.* | Marcus is taller than Tullia. |
| *quam* + superlative | *quam celerrimē* | as quickly as possible |
| superlative + genitive | *fortissimus omnium* | the bravest of all |

The superlative also means "very": *vir fortissimus*, a very brave man.

## Adverbs

Adverbs are formed from adjectives: first and second declension add *-ē* to the stem, third declension adds *-iter* (*-ter* after *-nt-*). The comparative adverb is the **neuter** comparative in *-ius*; the superlative changes *-us* to *-ē*.

| Adjective | Adverb | Comparative | Superlative |
|---|---|---|---|
| lātus, wide | lātē | lātius | lātissimē |
| fortis, brave | fortiter | fortius | fortissimē |
| celer, quick | celeriter | celerius | celerrimē |
| prūdēns, wise | prūdenter | prūdentius | prūdentissimē |
| bonus, good | bene | melius | optimē |
| magnus, great | magnopere | magis | maximē |

::: summary
- Comparative *-ior, -ius* (third declension, ablative *-e*); superlative *-issimus*; *-er* adjectives *-errimus*; six in *-lis* have *-illimus*.
- Irregular: *melior optimus, peior pessimus, maior maximus, minor minimus, plūs plūrimus*.
- "Than": *quam* + the same case, or the ablative alone. *Quam* + superlative: as … as possible.
- Adverbs: *-ē*, *-iter*; comparative *-ius*; superlative *-issimē*. *Bene, melius, optimē*.
:::
