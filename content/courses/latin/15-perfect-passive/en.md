# 15. The perfect passive participle and the perfect passive tenses

"Was loved", "had been taken", "will have been sent": the completed passive tenses are not made with endings but, as in English, with a helper verb. Latin combines the **perfect passive participle** (from the fourth principal part) with *sum*. The participle is also an adjective in its own right, one of the most useful words in Latin.

## The perfect passive participle

Take the fourth principal part, the supine in *-um*, and decline it as *bonus, -a, -um*:

| Verb | Supine | Participle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| amō, amāre, amāvī, amātum | amātum | amātus, -a, -um | (having been) loved |
| moneō, monēre, monuī, monitum | monitum | monitus, -a, -um | warned |
| regō, regere, rēxī, rēctum | rēctum | rēctus, -a, -um | ruled |
| capiō, capere, cēpī, captum | captum | captus, -a, -um | taken, captured |
| audiō, audīre, audīvī, audītum | audītum | audītus, -a, -um | heard |

It agrees like any adjective: *urbs capta* (the captured city), *litterae missae* (the letters that were sent), *mīlitēs vulnerātī* (the wounded soldiers).

## The perfect passive tenses

Participle + *sum* (perfect), *eram* (pluperfect), *erō* (future perfect). The participle agrees with the subject in gender and number.

| Person | Perfect | Pluperfect | Future perfect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | amātus sum | amātus eram | amātus erō |
| 2nd sg. | amātus es | amātus erās | amātus eris |
| 3rd sg. | amātus est | amātus erat | amātus erit |
| 1st pl. | amātī sumus | amātī erāmus | amātī erimus |
| 2nd pl. | amātī estis | amātī erātis | amātī eritis |
| 3rd pl. | amātī sunt | amātī erant | amātī erunt |

Mind the shift: *amātus est* is not "he is loved" (that is *amātur*) but "he was loved, has been loved". The *sum* gives the time relative to the participle, which is already past. A woman says *amāta sum*; of a city, *capta est*; of the letters, *missae sunt*.

The perfect passive infinitive is the participle + *esse*: *amātus esse*, "to have been loved".

## Reading the forms

| Latin | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Carthāgō dēlēta est.* | Carthage was destroyed. |
| *Hostēs victī erant.* | The enemies had been defeated. |
| *Cum epistula scrīpta erit, eam mittam.* | When the letter has been written, I shall send it. |
| *Mīlitēs vulnerātī in castra portātī sunt.* | The wounded soldiers were carried into the camp. |

- *captae sunt* → ((are captured / were captured*))
- *capiuntur* → ((are captured* / were captured))
:::

::: summary
- Perfect passive participle: from the supine, declined as *-us, -a, -um* (*amātus, captus, missus*).
- Perfect passive: participle + *sum*; pluperfect: + *eram*; future perfect: + *erō*. The participle agrees with the subject.
- *Amātus est* = he was (has been) loved; *amātur* = he is loved.
- Perfect passive infinitive: *amātus esse*.
:::
