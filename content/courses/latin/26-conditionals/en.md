# 26. Conditional sentences

"If it rains, we stay home"; "if it rained, we would stay home"; "if it had rained, we would have stayed home". A conditional sentence has two halves: the **protasis** (the "if" clause, *sī* or *nisi*) and the **apodosis** (the main clause). Latin classifies conditions by mood: the **indicative** for facts and real possibilities, the **subjunctive** for the imagined and the contrary to fact. The tense of the subjunctive tells which.

## The types of condition

| Type | Protasis | Apodosis | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple, present | present indicative | present indicative | *Sī hoc dīcis, errās.* If you say this, you are wrong. |
| Simple, past | past indicative | past indicative | *Sī hoc dīxistī, errāvistī.* If you said this, you were wrong. |
| Future more vivid | future (perfect) indicative | future indicative | *Sī hoc dīxeris, errābis.* If you say this, you will be wrong. |
| Future less vivid | present subjunctive | present subjunctive | *Sī hoc dīcās, errēs.* If you were to say this, you would be wrong. |
| Contrary to fact, present | imperfect subjunctive | imperfect subjunctive | *Sī hoc dīcerēs, errārēs.* If you were saying this (you are not), you would be wrong. |
| Contrary to fact, past | pluperfect subjunctive | pluperfect subjunctive | *Sī hoc dīxissēs, errāvissēs.* If you had said this, you would have been wrong. |

Three remarks:

- In the future more vivid, Latin is more precise than English: the protasis is future or future perfect (*sī vēneris*, "if you come", literally "if you will have come"), where English uses a present.
- Mixed conditions combine the two contrary-to-fact tenses: *Sī mē audīvissēs, nunc fēlīx essēs*, if you had listened to me, you would be happy now.
- The contrary-to-fact subjunctive does not follow the sequence of tenses: the imperfect means present time, the pluperfect past time.

## Sī, nisi and their companions

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *sī* | if | *sī vīs*, if you want |
| *nisi* | if … not, unless | *nisi pluit*, unless it rains |
| *sī nōn* | if … not (stressing the *nōn*) | *sī nōn vīs*, if you do not want |
| *sīn* | but if | *sīn autem*, but if on the other hand |
| *sī quis, sī quid* | if anyone, if anything | *sī quis venit*, if anyone comes |
| *dum, dummodo* + subjunctive | provided that | *dum nē*, provided that … not |

After *sī, nisi, num, nē*, the indefinite *aliquis* loses its *ali-*: *sī quis*, *nē quid*.

::: summary
- Indicative: simple conditions (present, past) and future more vivid (*sī vēneris, gaudēbō*).
- Present subjunctive: future less vivid, "if … were to …, would …".
- Imperfect subjunctive: contrary to fact, present; pluperfect: contrary to fact, past. Mixed conditions are possible.
- *Nisi* unless; *sīn* but if; *sī quis* if anyone; *dum(modo)* + subjunctive, provided that.
:::
