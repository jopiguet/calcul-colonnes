# 9. Demonstratives: *is*, *hic*, *ille* and their family

"He", "she", "it", "this", "that", "the same", "himself": Latin expresses them with a small family of words that decline in all three genders. They share an unusual genitive singular in **-īus** and dative in **-ī**. Learn *is*, *hic* and *ille* well; the others follow.

## *Is, ea, id*: he, she, it; this, that

*Is* is the plain pronoun of the third person, and also a weak "this, that" before a noun (*is vir*, that man).

| Case | Masc. | Fem. | Neut. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | is | ea | id |
| Gen. sg. | eius | eius | eius |
| Dat. sg. | eī | eī | eī |
| Acc. sg. | eum | eam | id |
| Abl. sg. | eō | eā | eō |
| Nom. pl. | eī | eae | ea |
| Gen. pl. | eōrum | eārum | eōrum |
| Dat., abl. pl. | eīs | eīs | eīs |
| Acc. pl. | eōs | eās | ea |

*Eius* means "his, her, its" and *eōrum, eārum* "their", when the owner is someone other than the subject: *Mārcus eius librum legit*, Marcus reads his (someone else's) book. For the subject's own, Latin uses *suus* (lesson 10).

## *Hic, haec, hoc*: this (here); *ille, illa, illud*: that (there)

| Case | hic (m.) | haec (f.) | hoc (n.) | ille (m.) | illa (f.) | illud (n.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | hic | haec | hoc | ille | illa | illud |
| Gen. sg. | huius | huius | huius | illīus | illīus | illīus |
| Dat. sg. | huic | huic | huic | illī | illī | illī |
| Acc. sg. | hunc | hanc | hoc | illum | illam | illud |
| Abl. sg. | hōc | hāc | hōc | illō | illā | illō |
| Nom. pl. | hī | hae | haec | illī | illae | illa |
| Gen. pl. | hōrum | hārum | hōrum | illōrum | illārum | illōrum |
| Dat., abl. pl. | hīs | hīs | hīs | illīs | illīs | illīs |
| Acc. pl. | hōs | hās | haec | illōs | illās | illa |

*Hic* points to what is near the speaker, *ille* to what is far, in space, time or fame: *ille Caesar*, "the famous Caesar". Paired, *ille… hic* mean "the former… the latter". *Iste, ista, istud* (that of yours) declines like *ille* and often sounds contemptuous: *iste homō*, "that fellow".

## *Idem* and *ipse*

*Īdem, eadem, idem* (the same) is *is* + *-dem*: *eiusdem*, *eīdem*, *eundem*, *eandem*, *eōdem*. *Ipse, ipsa, ipsum* (himself, herself, itself; the very) intensifies: *rēx ipse*, the king himself; *eō ipsō diē*, on that very day. Both have the genitive *-īus* (*ipsīus*) and the dative *-ī* (*ipsī*).

## The *ūnus nauta* adjectives

Nine adjectives decline like *bonus* except for the same genitive *-īus* and dative *-ī*: **ūnus** (one), **ūllus** (any), **nūllus** (no), **sōlus** (alone), **neuter** (neither), **alius** (other), **uter** (which of two), **tōtus** (whole), **alter** (the other of two). Their initials spell a mnemonic: UNUS NAUTA. *Tōtīus urbis*, of the whole city; *nūllī virō*, to no man.

::: summary
- *Is, ea, id*: he, she, it; *eius* his, her (not the subject's own).
- *Hic, haec, hoc*: this (near); *ille, illa, illud*: that (far, famous); *iste*: that of yours.
- *Īdem, eadem, idem*: the same; *ipse, ipsa, ipsum*: himself, the very.
- Genitive *-īus*, dative *-ī*: these pronouns and the UNUS NAUTA adjectives (*ūnus, nūllus, sōlus, tōtus, alius, alter…*).
:::
