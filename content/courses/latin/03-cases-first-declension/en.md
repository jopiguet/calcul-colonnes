# 3. Cases and the first declension

English shows who does what by word order: "the girl loves the poet" is not "the poet loves the girl". Latin shows it by endings: *puella poētam amat* and *poētam puella amat* mean the same. This lesson introduces the six cases and their jobs, and the first family of nouns.

## Six cases, six jobs

A Latin noun changes its ending according to its role in the sentence. Each role is a **case**.

| Case | Main job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | the subject | ***Puella** cantat.* The girl sings. |
| Genitive | "of": owner, part | *rosa **puellae*** the girl's rose |
| Dative | "to, for": indirect object | ***Puellae** rosam dō.* I give a rose to the girl. |
| Accusative | the direct object; "towards" | *Poēta **puellam** amat.* The poet loves the girl. |
| Ablative | "by, with, from, in" | ***Aquā** lavō.* I wash with water. |
| Vocative | calling someone | ***Iūlia**, venī!* Julia, come! |

Learning a noun means learning its nominative, its genitive and its gender: *puella, puellae* (f.), "girl". The genitive tells you the declension.

## The first declension

Nouns whose genitive singular ends in **-ae** belong to the first declension. Almost all are feminine.

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | puell**a** | puell**ae** |
| Genitive | puell**ae** | puell**ārum** |
| Dative | puell**ae** | puell**īs** |
| Accusative | puell**am** | puell**ās** |
| Ablative | puell**ā** | puell**īs** |
| Vocative | puell**a** | puell**ae** |

Some endings serve several cases. *Puellae* can be genitive or dative singular, or nominative plural; *puellīs* dative or ablative plural. The verb and the sense decide: *puellae cantant* (plural verb) can only be "the girls sing".

A few first-declension nouns name men's trades and are masculine: *agricola* (farmer), *nauta* (sailor), *poēta* (poet), *incola* (inhabitant).

## Word order and prepositions

Because endings carry the roles, word order is free; it serves emphasis. The common order is subject, object, verb: *Agricola terram arat*, "the farmer ploughs the land".

Prepositions take the accusative or the ablative:

| With the accusative | With the ablative |
|---|---|
| *ad* to, towards | *ā, ab* from, away from |
| *in* into, onto (motion) | *in* in, on (place) |
| *per* through | *cum* with |
| *ante* before | *dē* down from; about |
| *post* after, behind | *ē, ex* out of |

*In* changes meaning with its case: *in silvam* "into the forest", *in silvā* "in the forest".

::: summary
- Six cases: nominative (subject), genitive (of), dative (to, for), accusative (object, towards), ablative (by, with, from, in), vocative (address).
- A noun is learnt with its genitive and gender: *puella, -ae*, f.
- First declension: -a, -ae, -ae, -am, -ā; -ae, -ārum, -īs, -ās, -īs. Mostly feminine; *agricola*, *nauta*, *poēta* are masculine.
- Prepositions take the accusative (*ad*, *per*, *in* + motion) or the ablative (*ab*, *cum*, *ex*, *dē*, *in* + place).
:::
