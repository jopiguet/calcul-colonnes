# 8. The third and fourth conjugations, and the imperative

Two more groups of verbs complete the picture. The third conjugation (*regere*, with a short *e*) is the largest and the least regular; a branch of it, the *-iō* verbs (*capere*), borrows from the fourth (*audīre*). They share a future in *-am, -ēs*, not in *-bō*. This lesson also gives the imperative, for orders.

## Telling the conjugations apart

The infinitive and the first person together tell you the conjugation:

| Infinitive | 1st sg. | Conjugation |
|---|---|---|
| amāre (long ā) | amō | 1st |
| monēre (long ē) | moneō | 2nd |
| regere (short e) | regō | 3rd |
| capere (short e) | capiō | 3rd, *-iō* |
| audīre (long ī) | audiō | 4th |

*Monēre* and *regere* look alike in writing; only the length of the *e*, and the *-eō* of *moneō*, separate them. Dictionaries give both: *regō, regere*.

## The present

| Person | regere (rule) | capere (take) | audīre (hear) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | reg**ō** | capi**ō** | audi**ō** |
| 2nd sg. | reg**is** | cap**is** | audī**s** |
| 3rd sg. | reg**it** | cap**it** | audi**t** |
| 1st pl. | reg**imus** | cap**imus** | audī**mus** |
| 2nd pl. | reg**itis** | cap**itis** | audī**tis** |
| 3rd pl. | reg**unt** | capi**unt** | audi**unt** |

The third conjugation weakens its *e* to *i* (*regis, regit*) and to *u* before *-nt* (*regunt*). *Capiō* keeps an *i* where *audiō* does: in *capiō* and *capiunt*.

## The imperfect and the future

The imperfect of all three uses **-ēba-**: *regēbam*, *capiēbam*, *audiēbam*.

The future uses **-a-** in the first person and **-ē-** elsewhere:

| Person | regere | capere | audīre |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | reg**am** | capi**am** | audi**am** |
| 2nd sg. | reg**ēs** | capi**ēs** | audi**ēs** |
| 3rd sg. | reg**et** | capi**et** | audi**et** |
| 1st pl. | reg**ēmus** | capi**ēmus** | audi**ēmus** |
| 2nd pl. | reg**ētis** | capi**ētis** | audi**ētis** |
| 3rd pl. | reg**ent** | capi**ent** | audi**ent** |

Beware: *reget* (he will rule) is future, *monet* (he warns) is present. The conjugation decides what an *e* means.

## The imperative

An order uses the stem alone in the singular, the stem with *-te* in the plural. The third conjugation shows an *e* in the singular and an *i* in the plural.

| Conjugation | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | amā! | amāte! |
| 2nd | monē! | monēte! |
| 3rd | rege! | regite! |
| 3rd *-iō* | cape! | capite! |
| 4th | audī! | audīte! |

Four short verbs drop the *e*: *dīc!* (say!), *dūc!* (lead!), *fac!* (do!), *fer!* (bring!). A negative order uses *nōlī* / *nōlīte* + infinitive: *Nōlī timēre!* "Don't be afraid!"

::: summary
- 3rd: *regō, regere* (short e); 3rd *-iō*: *capiō, capere*; 4th: *audiō, audīre*.
- Present: *regō, regis, regit, regimus, regitis, regunt*; *capiunt*, *audiunt*.
- Imperfect *-ēbam*; future *-am, -ēs, -et, -ēmus, -ētis, -ent* (not *-bō*).
- Imperative: stem / stem + *-te* (*rege, regite*); *dīc, dūc, fac, fer*; *nōlī* + infinitive forbids.
:::
