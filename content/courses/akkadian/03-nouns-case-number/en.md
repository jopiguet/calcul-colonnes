# 3. Nouns: gender, case and number

Akkadian nouns work like those of Latin, but with fewer forms. There are two genders, three cases and three numbers, and the endings are almost the same for every noun. The cases are the old Semitic ones that Classical Arabic still has: nominative in *-u*, genitive in *-i*, accusative in *-a*, each followed in Old Babylonian by the mimation *-m*.

## The singular

| Case | Use | Masculine *šarrum* king | Feminine *šarratum* queen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | subject | *šarr-um* | *šarrat-um* |
| Genitive | after a noun or a preposition | *šarr-im* | *šarrat-im* |
| Accusative | object, and some adverbs | *šarr-am* | *šarrat-am* |

The feminine is marked by *-t-* or *-at-* before the ending: *bēlum* lord, *bēltum* lady; *šarrum* king, *šarratum* queen; *ilum* god, *iltum* goddess. Some nouns are feminine without it: *ummum* mother, *qātum* hand.

## The plural and the dual

| Case | Masculine plural | Feminine plural | Dual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | *šarr-ū* | *šarr-ātum* | *šēp-ān* two feet |
| Genitive / accusative | *šarr-ī* | *šarr-ātim* | *šēp-īn* |

The masculine plural has no mimation and only two forms: *-ū* for the subject, *-ī* for everything else. The dual is used for pairs of the body: *īnān* "the two eyes", *uznān* "the two ears", *šēpān* "the two feet".

## Prepositions

Prepositions take the genitive:

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ina* | in, at, from, with | *ina bītim* in the house |
| *ana* | to, for | *ana šarrim* to the king |
| *itti* | with (a person) | *itti aḫim* with the brother |
| *ištu* | from, since | *ištu ālim* from the city |
| *eli* | on, above, more than | *eli šarrim* above the king |
| *ša* | of, the one of | *bītum ša šarrim* the house of the king |

::: summary
- Singular: nominative *-um*, genitive *-im*, accusative *-am*; feminine with *-t-* / *-at-* (*šarratum*).
- Plural: masculine *-ū* / *-ī*; feminine *-ātum* / *-ātim*. Dual *-ān* / *-īn* for body pairs.
- Prepositions take the genitive: *ina*, *ana*, *itti*, *ištu*, *eli*, *ša*.
:::
