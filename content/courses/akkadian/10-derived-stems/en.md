# 10. The derived stems: D, Š, N

A root does not make only one verb. Akkadian builds from it several **stems**, each with its own meaning: the basic stem **G** (*Grundstamm*), the doubled stem **D**, the stem with *š*, **Š**, and the stem with *n*, **N**. From *parāsum* "to decide" come *uparris* "he separated", *ušapris* "he made decide", *ipparis* "it was decided". Learn to see the stem first; the dictionary lists the verb under its G infinitive.

## The forms of *parāsum*

| Stem | Infinitive | Preterite | Durative | Perfect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G | *parāsum* | *iprus* | *iparras* | *iptaras* |
| D | *purrusum* | *uparris* | *uparras* | *uptarris* |
| Š | *šuprusum* | *ušapris* | *ušapras* | *uštapris* |
| N | *naprusum* | *ipparis* | *ipparras* | *ittapras* |

Three marks: D doubles the middle radical (*-parr-*); Š puts *ša-* or *šu-* before the root; N puts *n* before it, which is absorbed into the next consonant (*in-paris* → *ipparis*). D and Š take the prefixes *u-* (3rd and 1st sg.), *tu-*, *nu-*, instead of *i-*, *a-*, *ta-*, *ni-*; their preterite ends in *-i-*: *uparris*, *ušapris*.

## What the stems mean

| Stem | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| D | makes a state happen, or intensifies | *damāqum* be good → *udammiq* he made good; *šebērum* break → *ušebbir* he smashed |
| Š | causative: makes someone do | *lamādum* learn → *ušalmid* he taught |
| N | passive of G | *ṣabātum* seize → *iṣṣabit* he was seized; *šakānum* → *iššakin* it was placed |

::: summary
- G basic; D doubles the middle radical (*uparris*); Š adds *ša-/šu-* (*ušapris*); N adds *n* (*ipparis*).
- D and Š: prefix *u-*, preterite in *-i-*.
- D factitive or intensive, Š causative, N passive.
:::
