# 1. Akkadian and its writing

Akkadian is the oldest attested Semitic language, a relative of Hebrew and Arabic, written from about 2500 BC to the first century AD. It was the language of Sargon of Akkad, of Hammurabi's Babylon and of the Assyrian empire, and for centuries the international language of diplomacy from Egypt to Iran. The Akkadians took over the Sumerian cuneiform script and adapted it to a very different language. This lesson shows what that script does with Akkadian and the two ways of writing it in Latin letters.

## Dialects and periods

| Period | Dates (approximate) | Texts |
|---|---|---|
| Old Akkadian | 2350–2000 BC | inscriptions of the kings of Akkad |
| Old Babylonian / Old Assyrian | 2000–1500 BC | Hammurabi's laws, letters, merchants of Assur |
| Middle Babylonian / Middle Assyrian | 1500–1000 BC | Amarna letters, Assyrian laws |
| Neo-Babylonian / Neo-Assyrian | 1000–600 BC | royal annals, letters of the empire |
| Standard Babylonian | literary, from 1500 BC on | Gilgamesh, Enuma Elish, hymns |

This course teaches **Old Babylonian**, the classical grammar, with the literary language where it differs.

## Three kinds of sign

| Kind | Example | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| syllable: vowel | 𒀀 | *a* |
| syllable: consonant + vowel | 𒊭 𒊒 𒈾 𒈠 | *ša*, *ru*, *na*, *ma* |
| syllable: vowel + consonant | 𒌝 𒅋 | *um*, *il* |
| logogram (a Sumerian word read in Akkadian) | 𒈗 | LUGAL = *šarrum* "king" |
| determinative | 𒀭 before a god | ᵈ*Marduk* |

Logograms, called **Sumerograms**, are transliterated in capitals: LUGAL, É, DUMU. A syllable after them, the **phonetic complement**, gives the ending: LUGAL-*um* = *šarrum*, LUGAL-*am* = *šarram*.

## Transliteration and normalization

| Step | Example | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| signs | 𒊭𒅈𒊒𒌝 | the tablet |
| transliteration | *ša-ar-ru-um* | each sign, joined by hyphens |
| normalization | *šarrum* | the word as pronounced, long vowels marked |

Normalization marks long vowels with a macron (*ā*) or, for vowels from a contraction, a circumflex (*â*): *a-wi-lum* is *awīlum* "man", *i-ba-aš-ši* is *ibašši* "there is". Writing does not show length or doubled consonants consistently: *ša-ru-um* is also *šarrum*.

In the exercises, type š, ṣ, ṭ, ḫ and the long vowels with the buttons, or as *sh*, *s*, *t*, *kh* and plain vowels: *sharrum* is *šarrum*.

- Akkadian: East Semitic; Old Akkadian, Babylonian (Old, Middle, Neo, Standard) and Assyrian; written 2500 BC to the first century AD.
- Signs: syllables (V, CV, VC, CVC), logograms in capitals (LUGAL = *šarrum*) with phonetic complements, determinatives (ᵈ).
