# 14. Reading: Hammurabi and Gilgamesh

Everything so far meets in two famous texts. The **Laws of Hammurabi**, king of Babylon in the 18th century BC, were carved on a basalt stele now in the Louvre: some 280 cases, each one sentence, *šumma* "if" followed by the penalty. The **Epic of Gilgamesh**, in its Standard Babylonian version, opens with a line every Assyriologist knows by heart. Read slowly: find the verb, its stem and tense, then the rest.

## Three laws

| Law | Akkadian | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| § 196 | *šumma awīlum īn mār awīlim uḫtappid, īnšu uḫappadū* | If a man has put out the eye of a man's son, they shall put out his eye. |
| § 197 | *šumma eṣemti awīlim ištebir, eṣemtašu išebberū* | If he has broken a man's bone, they shall break his bone. |
| § 200 | *šumma awīlum šinni awīlim meḫrišu ittadi, šinnašu inaddû* | If a man has knocked out the tooth of a man of his rank, they shall knock out his tooth. |

The pattern is always the same: *šumma* with the **perfect** (*uḫtappid*, *ištebir*, *ittadi*) for what was done, the **durative** 3rd plural for the penalty, "they shall…": *uḫappadū*, *išebberū*, *inaddû*. *Mār awīlim* "son of a man" means a free man, of the class called *awīlum*.

The prologue gives the purpose: *dannum enšam ana lā ḫabālim* "so that the strong may not oppress the weak": *ana* with the infinitive in the genitive, negated by *lā*.

## The first line of Gilgamesh

*ša naqba īmuru išdī māti*\
He who saw the Deep, the foundations of the land.

*Ša* … *īmuru*: a relative clause, the verb (*amārum*, preterite) in the subjunctive. *Naqbum* is the deep water under the earth; *išdī* "foundations" is plural in the construct state before *māti*.

And from the Descent of Ishtar: *ana kurnugi qaqqari lā târi* "to Kurnugi, the land of no return": *lā târi*, the infinitive of *târum*, negated, in the genitive.

::: summary
- Laws: *šumma* + perfect for the act, durative 3rd pl. for the penalty.
- Purpose: *ana* + infinitive, negated by *lā*: *ana lā ḫabālim*.
- *ša naqba īmuru*: relative clause, subjunctive.
:::
