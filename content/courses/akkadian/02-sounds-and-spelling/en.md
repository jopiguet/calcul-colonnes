# 2. Sounds and spelling

To read Akkadian you go from signs to sounds: *ša-ar-ru-um* is the word *šarrum*. That step needs two things: the sounds of the language, which are those of a Semitic language, and the habits of the scribes, who wrote those sounds with syllabic signs made for Sumerian. This lesson gives both, so that you can normalize any syllabic spelling.

## Consonants

| Letter | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ʾ* | glottal stop (the *aleph*) | *šaʾālum* to ask |
| *b p m w* | as in English | *bītum* house |
| *d t ṭ* | *d*, *t*, an emphatic *t* | *ṭuppum* tablet |
| *g k q* | *g*, *k*, a *k* deep in the throat | *qātum* hand |
| *z s ṣ* | *z*, *s*, an emphatic *s* | *ṣābum* troops |
| *š* | *sh* | *šarrum* king |
| *ḫ* | Scottish *ch* | *aḫum* brother |
| *l r n y* | as in English | *rabûm* great |

The three **emphatics**, *ṭ*, *q*, *ṣ*, are typical of Semitic languages; Arabic still has them.

## Vowels

Four vowels, *a*, *e*, *i*, *u*, short or long. Normalization writes:

| Mark | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| none | short | *šarrum* |
| macron *ā* | long | *bītum* house, *awīlum* man |
| circumflex *â* | long, from two vowels contracted | *rabûm* great (*rabi-um*) |

## How the scribes wrote

| Rule | Spelling | Normalized |
|---|---|---|
| a closed syllable CVC is written CV-VC | *ša-ar-ru-um* | *šarrum* |
| or with a CVC sign | *šar-ru-um* | *šarrum* |
| a doubled consonant may be written single | *ša-ru-um* | *šarrum* |
| a long vowel is sometimes written twice | *ma-a-tum* | *mātum* |
| one sign may stand for *e* and *i* | *be-lum* or *bi-lum* | *bēlum* lord |
| one sign may stand for voiced and voiceless | *ta* / *da* / *ṭa* | context decides |

Nouns of Old Babylonian end in *-m* (**mimation**): *šarrum*, *šarrim*, *šarram*. Later texts drop it: *šarru*.

::: summary
- Semitic consonants: *ʾ*, emphatics *ṭ q ṣ*, *š*, *ḫ*.
- Vowels *a e i u*, short or long: *ā* long, *â* long by contraction.
- Spelling: CVC as CV-VC or CVC; doubled consonants and long vowels not always written; mimation *-m* in Old Babylonian.
:::
