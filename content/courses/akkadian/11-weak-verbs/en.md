# 11. Weak verbs

*Parāsum* has three solid radicals. Many common verbs do not: one radical is *n*, a vowel, *w* or a lost guttural (*ʾ*), and it assimilates, lengthens a vowel or disappears. The forms look irregular, but each class follows one rule. Knowing them is what lets you read a real text: *iddin* "he gave", *īmur* "he saw", *ibni* "he built", *illik* "he went".

## The classes

| Class | Example | Preterite | Durative | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I-n | *nadānum* give | *iddin* | *inaddin* | *n* assimilates to the next consonant: *indin* → *iddin* |
| I-ʾ (*a*) | *amārum* see | *īmur* | *immar* | the lost first radical lengthens the vowel |
| I-ʾ (*e*) | *epēšum* do, make | *īpuš* | *ippeš* | the same, with *e* colouring |
| I-w | *wašābum* sit | *ūšib* | *uššab* | *w* disappears, the prefix becomes *ū-*, *u-* |
| II-weak | *târum* return | *itūr* | *itâr* | a long vowel stands in the middle |
| III-weak | *banûm* build | *ibni* | *ibanni* | the last radical is a vowel |

*Alākum* "to go" is a class of its own: preterite *illik*, durative *illak*, perfect *ittalak*, imperative *alik*. *Leqûm* "to take" is III-weak with *e*: *ilqe*, *ileqqe*.

## Recognising the root

A short form often hides its root. Work back from the vowel and the doubling:

| Form | Root | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| *iddin* | n-d-n | *nadānum* |
| *ikkal* | ʾ-k-l | *akālum* eat (durative) |
| *imūt* | m-w-t | *mâtum* die |
| *ilqe* | l-q-ʾ | *leqûm* take |
