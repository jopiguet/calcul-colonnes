# 12. Subordinate clauses

"The man **who** took the city", "**if** a man has stolen", "**when** the king came": Akkadian marks the verb of a subordinate clause with a short ending, the **subjunctive** *-u*. Nothing else changes: the clause keeps its order, verb last. *Šarrum ālam iṣbat* "the king took the city" becomes *šarrum ša ālam iṣbatu* "the king who took the city".

## The subjunctive

| Main clause | Subordinate | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| *iprus* | *iprusu* | *-u* after a consonant |
| *iparras* | *iparrasu* | the same in the durative |
| *ibni* | *ibnû* | *i* + *u* contract to *û* |
| *damiq* | *damqu* | the stative takes it too |
| *iprusū* | *iprusū* | a form with a vowel ending does not change |

The subjunctive is used after *ša* "who, which" and after the conjunctions. It is not used after *šumma* "if": the laws say *šumma awīlum … iṣbat* without *-u*.

## The conjunctions

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ša* | who, which, that | *bītum ša ibnû* the house that he built |
| *šumma* | if | *šumma awīlum imūt* if a man dies |
| *inūma* | when | *inūma šarrum illiku* when the king went |
| *kīma* | as; that | *kīma taqbû* as you said |
| *adi* | until | *adi allaku* until I go |
| *aššum* | because; concerning | *aššum lā illiku* because he did not go |

A negated subordinate clause takes *lā*, never *ul*: *ša lā iṣbatu* "who did not seize".

::: summary
- Subordinate verb: subjunctive *-u* (*iprusu*, *ibnû*, *damqu*); vowel endings unchanged.
- After *ša* and *inūma*, *kīma*, *adi*, *aššum*; not after *šumma*.
- Negation in subordinate clauses: *lā*.
:::
