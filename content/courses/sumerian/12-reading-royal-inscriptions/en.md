# 12. Reading: royal building inscriptions

Thousands of Sumerian bricks, clay cones and door sockets carry the same kind of text: a ruler records that he built a temple for a god. The pattern never changes: the god in the dative, the ruler in the ergative, the building in the absolutive, and *mu-na-du₃* "he built it for him". This last lesson reads two famous examples, from Ur-Namma of Ur and from Gudea of Lagash, with the grammar of the whole course. The transliterations follow the published texts; the translations are ours.

## Ur-Namma, king of Ur (about 2100 BC)

ᵈ*nanna lugal-a-ni* \
*ur-*ᵈ*namma lugal urim₂*ᵏⁱ*-ma-ke₄* \
*e₂-a-ni mu-na-du₃* \
*bad₃-a-ni mu-na-du₃*

"For Nanna, his lord, Ur-Namma, king of Ur, built his temple, built his wall."

| Phrase | Grammar |
|---|---|
| ᵈ*nanna lugal-a-ni* | the god, with *lugal* "lord" + *-a-ni* "his"; the dative *-ra* is understood |
| *ur-*ᵈ*namma* | "servant of Namma", a genitive name |
| *lugal urim₂*ᵏⁱ*-ma-ke₄* | "king of Ur": *urim₂-ak* genitive, then *-e* ergative: *-ak-e* written *-ke₄* |
| *e₂-a-ni* | "his house", absolutive object |
| *mu-na-du₃* | *mu-* + *-na-* "for him" + *-n-* "he" + *du₃* "built" |

## Gudea, ruler of Lagash (about 2125 BC)

ᵈ*nin-ĝir₂-su* \
*ur-saĝ kalag-ga* ᵈ*en-lil₂-la₂-ra* \
*gu₃-de₂-a ensi₂ lagaš*ᵏⁱ*-ke₄* \
*e₂-ninnu-ni mu-na-du₃*

"For Ningirsu, the mighty hero of Enlil, Gudea, ruler of Lagash, built his Eninnu."

| Phrase | Grammar |
|---|---|
| *ur-saĝ kalag-ga* | "mighty hero": noun + adjective *kalag* + *-a* |
| ᵈ*en-lil₂-la₂-ra* | genitive of Enlil (*-ak*) + dative *-ra* closing the whole phrase "for Ningirsu, hero of Enlil" |
| *gu₃-de₂-a* | Gudea, "the one called (by the god)" |
| *ensi₂* | ruler, governor of a city |
| *lagaš*ᵏⁱ*-ke₄* | "of Lagash" + ergative: *-ak-e* |
| *e₂-ninnu-ni* | "his Eninnu", the temple "House of Fifty", with the possessive *-ni* |

::: summary
- Building inscriptions: god (+ *-ra*, often understood), ruler + *-ke₄*, building + *-a-ni*, *mu-na-du₃*.
- *-ke₄* = genitive *-ak* + ergative *-e*: *lugal urim₂*ᵏⁱ*-ma-ke₄* "the king of Ur (did)".
- Ur-Namma built for Nanna at Ur; Gudea built the Eninnu for Ningirsu at Lagash.
- Every element of the noun chain and the verbal chain appears in these few lines.
:::
