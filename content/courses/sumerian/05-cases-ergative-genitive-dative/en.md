# 5. Cases I: ergative, absolutive, genitive, dative

Sumerian marks the role of a noun phrase with a case ending at the end of its chain. Its most unusual feature is **ergativity**: the subject of a transitive verb ("the king **built** the house") takes a special ending, *-e*, while the subject of an intransitive verb ("the king **went**") looks exactly like the object of a transitive one. This lesson gives that system and the two cases most common in inscriptions: the genitive *-ak* and the dative *-ra*.

## Ergative and absolutive

| Role | Case | Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| subject of a transitive verb | ergative | *-e* | ***lugal-e*** e₂ mu-du₃ "the king built the house" |
| object of a transitive verb | absolutive | none | lugal-e ***e₂*** mu-du₃ |
| subject of an intransitive verb | absolutive | none | ***lugal*** i₃-ĝen "the king went" |

English groups the two subjects together ("the king built", "the king went") and sets the object apart; Sumerian groups the intransitive subject with the object and sets the transitive subject apart. After a vowel the ergative *-e* often merges: *dumu-e* or just *dumu*.

## The genitive -ak

"X of Y" is *X Y-ak*: the genitive ending goes on the possessor, which follows the possessed.

| Written | Underlying | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *e₂ lugal-la* | *e₂ lugal-ak* | the house of the king |
| *dumu lugal-la* | *dumu lugal-ak* | the son of the king |
| *e₂ lugal-la-ka* | *e₂ lugal-ak-a* | in the house of the king (with locative *-a*) |

The *k* is written only before a vowel; otherwise the scribe doubles the last consonant and writes *-a*: *lugal-la*. Genitives can be stacked: *e₂ dumu lugal-la-ka* "the house of the son of the king" (*-ak-ak*).

## The dative -ra

The dative "to, for" is used for people and gods only (human class):

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *lugal-ra* | for the king |
| ᵈ*nin-ĝir₂-su-ra* | for (the god) Ningirsu |
| *dumu-ĝu₁₀-ra* | to my son |

Building inscriptions begin with it: "For Ningirsu, … Gudea … built his temple."

::: summary
- Ergative *-e*: subject of a transitive verb. Absolutive (no ending): object, and subject of an intransitive verb.
- Genitive *-ak* on the possessor, after the possessed: *e₂ lugal-la(k)*; *k* written only before a vowel.
- Dative *-ra* for people and gods: *lugal-ra*, ᵈ*nin-ĝir₂-su-ra*.
:::
