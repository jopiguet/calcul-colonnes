# 4. Possessive suffixes

"My house", "your son", "his king": Sumerian marks the possessor with a suffix on the noun phrase, in the fourth position of the noun chain, after any adjective and before the plural and the case. The third person keeps the distinction of the two classes: *-a-ni* for a human possessor, *-bi* for a non-human one. These suffixes are written with syllabic signs and are among the first things to recognize in any line.

## The forms

| Person | Suffix | Written | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | *-ĝu₁₀* | 𒈬 | *e₂-ĝu₁₀* | my house |
| 2nd sg. | *-zu* | 𒍪 | *e₂-zu* | your house |
| 3rd sg. human | *-a-ni* (after a vowel *-ni*) | 𒀀𒉌 | *e₂-a-ni* | his, her house |
| 3rd non-human | *-bi* | 𒁉 | *e₂-bi* | its house; their house |
| 1st pl. | *-me* | 𒈨 | *e₂-me* | our house |
| 2nd pl. | *-zu-ne-ne* | | *e₂-zu-ne-ne* | your house (of several) |
| 3rd pl. human | *-a-ne-ne* | | *e₂-a-ne-ne* | their house |

The sign 𒈬 is *mu* "name, year" and also *ĝu₁₀* "my": the subscript 10 shows it is the tenth sign read *ĝu*.

## Class decides the third person

| Possessor | Suffix | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| a man, a woman, a god | *-a-ni* | *dumu lugal-a-ni* … | the king's son, his … |
| a thing, a place, an animal | *-bi* | *e₂ uru-bi* | the temple of the city, its temple |

There is no "his" against "her": *e₂-a-ni* is "his house" or "her house"; the context decides.

## In the chain

*e₂ gibil-a-ni* "his new house": noun, adjective, possessive. *dumu-ni* "his son" (vowel + *-ni*). A possessive can follow a genitive: *ama dumu-ni* "the mother of his son" is *ama* + *dumu-ni-(ak)*; you will see the genitive in lesson 5.

::: summary
- *-ĝu₁₀* my, *-zu* your, *-a-ni* his/her (human), *-bi* its (non-human), *-me* our, *-zu-ne-ne* your (pl.), *-a-ne-ne* their (human).
- After a vowel *-a-ni* is *-ni*: *dumu-ni*.
- Fourth in the chain: noun, adjective, genitive, **possessive**, plural, case.
:::
