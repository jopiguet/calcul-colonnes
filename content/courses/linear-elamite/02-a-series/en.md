# 2. The a series

Most signs join a consonant to a vowel. This lesson gives the signs ending in *a*, twelve of them, and shows how one value can have several forms, depending on the region.

- Several signs are built on the diamond of :  splits it with a line,  adds a line and a bar,  puts a star on top,  a star below.
-  is a line broken in two,  a line with a dot in the middle,  three lines with a dot.
-  is a single curve, like a crescent.
- *š* is the sound of English *sh*. You can type *sh*.

## One value, several forms

Many values were written differently from one place to another. The 2025 plate of the inscriptions sorts the forms into three groups: general forms, forms of the south-west (Susa and Fars), and forms of the south-east (Kerman and the silver vessels).

| Value | General | South-west | South-east |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ha* |    | | |
| *za* |    | |  |
| *wa* | |   |   |
| *ra* |  |  | |

The forms of one value count as one answer: any of them is read *ha*, *za*, *wa* or *ra*.
