# 5. Reading names

The decipherment began with names: kings, gods and the land itself, also known from cuneiform. This lesson reads some of them, as they appear in the inscriptions, and a few words of their titles.

## The land and its rulers

| Signs | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
|  | ha-ta-m-ti | Hatamti, the land of Elam |
|  | ze-m-t | king, lord |
|  | me-ni-r | ruler |
|  | e-pa-r-ti | Eparti, a king |
|  | pu-zu-r | Puzur, the first part of a king's name |

-  *ha-ta-m-ti* is the Elamites' own name for their land: *Hatamti*. The consonant sign  closes the syllable *tam*.
-  *ze-m-t*, *zemt*, is the old Elamite word for "king" and for a god as "lord".
-  *e-pa-r-ti* is Eparti, a king of the early 2nd millennium BCE whose name appears in several inscriptions from Fars.

## Gods and a king of Susa

| Signs | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
|  | su-ši-na-k | Sušinak, god of Susa |
|  | na-pi-re-ša | Napireša, a great god |
|  | pu-zu-r-su-ši-na-k | Puzur-Sušinak, king at Susa |

- Puzur-Sušinak ruled at Susa around 2150–2100 BCE. His inscriptions, some in Akkadian cuneiform and some in Linear Elamite, write his name  *pu-zu-r-su-ši-na-k*: *Puzur*, then the god's name.
- The god of Susa, later called Inšušinak, appears here as *Sušinak*.
-  *na-pi-re-ša* was first transliterated *na-pi-ri-ša*, after the cuneiform spelling *Napiriša*. With *re* for the third sign, the name reads *Napireša*.
