# 1. Linear Elamite

Linear Elamite was written in southern Iran between about 2300 and 1880 BCE, for the Elamite language. Its signs stand for syllables and single sounds, and it is one of the most recently deciphered scripts. This lesson tells its story, explains how its signs work and gives the five vowels.

## A script from Elam

Elam was the land of south-western Iran, with Susa as one of its great cities; the Elamites called it Hatamti. Elamite is a language with no known relatives. For most of its history it was written in cuneiform, borrowed from Mesopotamia, but for about four centuries it also had a script of its own.

- In 1903, French excavators at Susa found inscriptions in an unknown script. In the 1960s it was named Linear Elamite.
- About forty texts are known: from Susa, from Fars (around Persepolis and Kam-Firuz), and from Kerman province in the south-east (Shahdad, Konar Sandal). They are carved on stone, written on clay, or engraved on silver vessels.
- The direction varies: right to left, left to right, or top to bottom.
- In 2018, eight inscribed silver beakers were published. They gave the names of kings also known from cuneiform, and in 2022 François Desset, Kambiz Tabibzadeh, Matthieu Kervran, Gian Pietro Basello and Gianni Marchesi published a decipherment.

The decipherment is recent. Most readings are well supported, some rest on a single text, and specialists still discuss it; the values themselves have been revised since 2022. This course teaches the latest values and gives the earlier ones where they differ.

## How the signs work

Linear Elamite is an **alphasyllabary**. It has three kinds of signs:

| Kind | Example | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Vowel |  | *a* |
| Consonant and vowel |  | *ka* |
| Consonant alone |  | *r* |

The consonant signs close a syllable:  *pu-zu-r* is *puzur*. About 70 values are known, written with some 140 signs: many values have several forms, some general, some used in one region only. The other signs of the script, about 200, have no agreed value yet.

Luma's font numbers the signs LE001 to LE348, after the sign list of Desset and his colleagues:  is LE001.

## The five vowels

| Sign | Reading | Number |
|---|---|---|
|  | *a* | LE001 |
|  | *e* | LE099 |
|  | *i* | LE160 |
|  | *o* | LE309 |
|  | *u* (or *w*) | LE171 |

-  is a plain diamond, and  a cross over a triangle, with a dot at each end of the bar.
-  is a line between two dots. It also stands for *w*.
- On the 2025 plate of the inscriptions,  is read *u* and  is read *u₂*: the vowel *o* was recognised later. Both readings count as answers here.
