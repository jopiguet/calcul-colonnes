# 4. o, u, ů and the consonants

The signs in *o* and *u* complete the syllables. A third vowel of this group, written *ů*, has a series of its own. Last come the consonant signs, which close syllables and let the script write any word.

|  *ko* | LE237 |  *ku* | LE084 |  *hů* | LE009 |
|  *mo* | LE253 |  *mu* | LE346 |  *lů* | LE078 |
|  *po* | LE141 |  *pu* | LE135 |  *nů* | LE179 |
|  *so* | LE008 |  *su* | LE203 |  *rů* | LE181 |
|  *zo* | LE065 |  *zu* | LE182 |  *šů* | LE288 |
| | |  *tu* | LE225 | | |

-  is a cup with two dots;  two half-discs;  two lines.
-  is a cross with dots;  a line with four dots around it.
- *ů* marks a vowel of the *u* group that the script keeps apart from *u* and *o*; its exact sound is still discussed. You can type *u*.
- The 2025 plate numbers these signs too: *ko* is *ku₂* there, *mo* is *mu*, *hů* is *hu*, *rů* is *ru*.  was read *lu?* in 2022 and left without a value in 2025; Desset's latest table reads it *lů*.

## Consonant signs

A consonant sign writes a consonant with no vowel. It closes a syllable or stands at the end of a word.

| Sign | Reading | Sign | Reading | Sign | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|  | *h* |  | *m* |  | *s* |
|  | *k* |  | *n* |  | *š* |
|  | *l* |  | *p* |  | *t* |
|  | *r* |  | *z* |  | *w* |

-  is a line with three dots;  is the same sign as , a line with two.
-  is a triangle pointing left;  a long hexagon with two bars.
- A word such as *puzur* is written  *pu-zu-r*: two syllables, then the closing *r*.
