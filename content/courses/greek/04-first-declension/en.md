# 4. Cases and the first declension

Greek nouns change their ending with their role in the sentence, as Latin and German nouns do. There are five cases and three groups of endings, the **declensions**. The first declension holds mostly feminine nouns in -α and -η; it comes with the feminine article, which declines alongside its noun and is the best guide to the case.

## The cases

| Case | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | subject | ἡ θεὰ λέγει the goddess speaks |
| genitive | of; possession; from | ἡ οἰκία τῆς θεᾶς the house of the goddess |
| dative | to, for; with (instrument); in, at | λέγει τῇ θεᾷ he speaks to the goddess |
| accusative | direct object; towards | τιμῶσι τὴν θεάν they honour the goddess |
| vocative | addressing | ὦ θεά O goddess |

Word order is free; the endings, not the place, say who does what.

## Three patterns

| Case | τιμή honour | χώρα land | θάλαττα sea | Article |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | τιμή | χώρα | θάλαττα | ἡ |
| Gen. sg. | τιμῆς | χώρας | θαλάττης | τῆς |
| Dat. sg. | τιμῇ | χώρᾳ | θαλάττῃ | τῇ |
| Acc. sg. | τιμήν | χώραν | θάλατταν | τήν |
| Nom. pl. | τιμαί | χῶραι | θάλατται | αἱ |
| Gen. pl. | τιμῶν | χωρῶν | θαλαττῶν | τῶν |
| Dat. pl. | τιμαῖς | χώραις | θαλάτταις | ταῖς |
| Acc. pl. | τιμάς | χώρας | θαλάττας | τάς |

After ε, ι or ρ the α stays long through the singular (χώρα, οἰκία, ἡμέρα); otherwise η (τιμή, ψυχή). A short α (θάλαττα, γλῶττα) changes to η only in the genitive and dative singular. The plural is the same for all, and the genitive plural always has the circumflex: -ῶν.

A few masculines follow this declension with -ης or -ας: ὁ πολίτης "citizen", gen. τοῦ πολίτου; ὁ νεανίας "young man".

::: summary
- Nominative subject, genitive *of*, dative *to/with*, accusative object, vocative address.
- First declension: τιμή, τιμῆς, τιμῇ, τιμήν; χώρα (α after ε ι ρ); θάλαττα, θαλάττης.
- Plural for all: -αι, -ῶν, -αις, -ας; article ἡ τῆς τῇ τήν, αἱ τῶν ταῖς τάς.
:::
