# 23. Homer

The *Iliad* and the *Odyssey* are older than Attic prose by three centuries and were composed for singing. Their language is an artificial mix, mostly Ionic with older forms, shaped by the metre, the **dactylic hexameter**. Once you know a few correspondences with Attic, Homer reads more easily than one fears: the grammar is the one you have learnt.

## The first lines

| Text | Translation |
|---|---|
| μῆνιν ἄειδε, θεά, Πηληϊάδεω Ἀχιλῆος | Sing, goddess, the wrath of Achilles son of Peleus, |
| οὐλομένην, ἣ μυρί' Ἀχαιοῖς ἄλγε' ἔθηκε | the accursed wrath, which laid countless pains on the Achaeans |
| ἄνδρα μοι ἔννεπε, μοῦσα, πολύτροπον, ὃς μάλα πολλὰ | Tell me, Muse, of the man of many turns, who very much |
| πλάγχθη, ἐπεὶ Τροίης ἱερὸν πτολίεθρον ἔπερσεν | wandered, after he sacked the holy citadel of Troy |

The first two lines open the *Iliad* (1.1–2), the last two the *Odyssey* (1.1–2). Each poem names its subject in its first word: μῆνιν "wrath", ἄνδρα "the man".

## Homeric and Attic

| Homer | Attic | Feature |
|---|---|---|
| θεοῖο | θεοῦ | genitive in -οιο |
| Πηληϊάδεω, Ἀτρεΐδαο | -ου | masculine first declension, gen. -αο, -εω |
| ἀνθρώποισι, θεῇσι | ἀνθρώποις, θεαῖς | dative plural in -οισι, -ῃσι |
| Τροίης | Τροίας | Ionic η for Attic long α |
| ἔννεπε, πλάγχθη | ἔννεπε, ἐπλάγχθη | augment often left out |
| ὁ, ἡ, τό | οὗτος, he, she | the article is still a pronoun: ὁ δέ "and he" |

Fixed epithets fill the line: πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς "swift-footed Achilles", γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη "grey-eyed Athena", ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς "rosy-fingered Dawn".

## The hexameter

Six feet; each is a **dactyl**, long-short-short (— ∪ ∪), or a **spondee**, long-long (— —). The fifth foot is nearly always a dactyl, the sixth has two syllables. A syllable is long if its vowel is long or a diphthong, or if two consonants follow.

μῆνιν ἄ | ειδε θε | ὰ Πη | ληϊά | δεω Ἀχι | λῆος: dactyl, dactyl, spondee, dactyl, dactyl, and two syllables. In δεω the two vowels are read as one syllable.

::: summary
- Homer: genitive -οιο, -αο, -εω; dative plural -οισι, -ῃσι; Ionic η; augment optional; ὁ as a pronoun.
- Fixed epithets: πόδας ὠκὺς Ἀχιλλεύς, γλαυκῶπις Ἀθήνη, ῥοδοδάκτυλος Ἠώς.
- Hexameter: six feet, dactyls — ∪ ∪ or spondees — —; fifth foot a dactyl.
