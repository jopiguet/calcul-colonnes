# 19. The optative

The optative is the mood of wishes and of what *would* happen. Latin and most modern languages lost it; Greek kept it through the classical period, and it is one of the marks of a careful Attic style. Its sign is a ι in the ending, usually οι or αι: λύοιμι "may I loose", λύσαιμι.

## The forms

| Person | Present active | Aorist active | εἰμί |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | λύοιμι | λύσαιμι | εἴην |
| 2nd sg. | λύοις | λύσαις, λύσειας | εἴης |
| 3rd sg. | λύοι | λύσαι, λύσειε(ν) | εἴη |
| 1st pl. | λύοιμεν | λύσαιμεν | εἶμεν |
| 2nd pl. | λύοιτε | λύσαιτε | εἶτε |
| 3rd pl. | λύοιεν | λύσαιεν, λύσειαν | εἶεν |

The middle has -οίμην, -οιο, -οιτο: γένοιτο "may it happen". The second aorist has the present endings on its own stem: λάβοιμι, ἔλθοι. The aorist passive: λυθείην, λυθείη.

## The uses

| Use | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| wish | optative, often with εἴθε or εἰ γάρ | μὴ γένοιτο "may it not happen" |
| potential | optative + ἄν | ἡδέως ἂν ἀκούσαιμι "I would gladly hear" |
| condition, "should… would" | εἰ + optative, optative + ἄν | εἰ ἔλθοι, ἴδοι ἄν "if he were to come, he would see" |
| after a past main verb | the optative may replace the subjunctive | ἦλθεν ἵνα μάθοι "he came in order to learn" |

In Sophocles, Ajax says to his son: ὦ παῖ, γένοιο πατρὸς εὐτυχέστερος, "O child, may you be luckier than your father". Paul's μὴ γένοιτο, "by no means", is the same wish.

The negation of the wish is μή; of the potential, οὐ: οὐκ ἂν εἴποιμι "I would not say".
