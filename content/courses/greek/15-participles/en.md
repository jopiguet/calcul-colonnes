# 15. Participles

Greek writers build their sentences around participles. Where English says "when he had seen the enemy, he fled", Greek says ἰδὼν τοὺς πολεμίους ἔφυγεν, "having seen the enemy, he fled". A participle is a verbal adjective: it has tense and voice, takes objects, and agrees with a noun in gender, number and case.

## The forms

| Participle | m. | f. | n. | gen. m./n. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| present active | λύων | λύουσα | λῦον | λύοντος |
| aorist active | λύσας | λύσασα | λῦσαν | λύσαντος |
| second aorist | λαβών | λαβοῦσα | λαβόν | λαβόντος |
| of εἰμί | ὤν | οὖσα | ὄν | ὄντος |
| present middle-passive | λυόμενος | λυομένη | λυόμενον | λυομένου |
| aorist middle | λυσάμενος | λυσαμένη | λυσάμενον | λυσαμένου |

The active masculine and neuter follow the third declension (λύοντος, λύοντι, λύοντα; dat. pl. λύουσι), the feminine the first (λυούσης). The middle participles decline like ἀγαθός. πᾶς, πᾶσα, πᾶν "all, every" goes like λύσας.

The tense of a participle is relative: the present is at the same time as the main verb, the aorist before it.

## Three uses

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| with the article: a noun | ὁ λέγων | the speaker, the one who speaks |
| without the article: a circumstance | ταῦτα εἰπὼν ἀπῆλθεν | having said this, he left |
| genitive absolute | τοῦ βασιλέως ἐλθόντος, ἐφύγομεν | when the king had come, we fled |

A circumstantial participle can mean "when", "because", "although" or "if": the context, and small words such as καίπερ "although" and ὡς "on the ground that", decide. The **genitive absolute**, a noun and a participle both in the genitive, stands apart from the rest of the sentence, like the Latin ablative absolute.

::: exercise agree
Which participle agrees?

- ἡ γυνή, … "speaking" → ((λέγων / λέγουσα* / λέγον))
- τῶν ἀνδρῶν, … "fighting" → ((μαχομένων* / μαχόμενοι / μαχομένους))
- τὸ τέκνον, … "having seen" → ((ἰδών / ἰδοῦσα / ἰδόν*))
- τοὺς πολίτας, … "being" → ((ὄντας* / ὄντες / οὔσας))
:::

::: exercise use
What does it mean?

- οἱ φεύγοντες → ((those who flee* / while fleeing / having fled))
- ταῦτα ἀκούσας → ((hearing this, now / having heard this* / those who heard this))
- τοῦ πολέμου ὄντος → ((the war's being / while there was a war* / of the existing war))
- καίπερ σοφὸς ὤν → ((because he is wise / although he is wise* / if he is wise))
:::

::: exercise translate
Translate.

:::

::: summary
- λύων, λύουσα, λῦον (λύοντος); λύσας, λύσασα, λῦσαν; λαβών; ὤν, οὖσα, ὄν; λυόμενος, -η, -ον.
- Present: same time; aorist: before the main verb.
- With the article a noun (ὁ λέγων); without, a circumstance; genitive absolute for an independent subject.
:::
