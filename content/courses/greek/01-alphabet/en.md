# 1. The alphabet and its sounds

Greek has been written with the same 24 letters for 2,500 years. Most of them you already half know: from mathematics (α, π, Σ), from the Latin alphabet, which comes from a Greek one, and from the words English took over: φιλοσοφία, δημοκρατία, θέατρον. This lesson gives the letters, their names, the standard transliteration and the pronunciation of classical Athens, the dialect of this course, Attic, of the 5th and 4th centuries BC.

## The letters

| Letter | Name | Transliteration | Sound in Athens |
|---|---|---|---|
| Α α | alpha | a | *a*, short or long |
| Β β | beta | b | *b* |
| Γ γ | gamma | g | *g* in *go*; *n* before γ κ ξ χ |
| Δ δ | delta | d | *d* |
| Ε ε | epsilon | e | short *e* |
| Ζ ζ | zeta | z | *zd* |
| Η η | eta | ē | long open *e*, as in *air* |
| Θ θ | theta | th | *t* with a puff of breath, as in *top* |
| Ι ι | iota | i | *i*, short or long |
| Κ κ | kappa | k | *k* without breath, as in *skin* |
| Λ λ | lambda | l | *l* |
| Μ μ | mu | m | *m* |
| Ν ν | nu | n | *n* |
| Ξ ξ | xi | x | *ks* |
| Ο ο | omicron | o | short *o* |
| Π π | pi | p | *p* without breath |
| Ρ ρ | rho | r | rolled *r* |
| Σ σ ς | sigma | s | *s*; ς at the end of a word |
| Τ τ | tau | t | *t* without breath |
| Υ υ | upsilon | y, u | French *u*, German *ü* |
| Φ φ | phi | ph | *p* with breath, later *f* |
| Χ χ | chi | ch | *k* with breath, later as in *Bach* |
| Ψ ψ | psi | ps | *ps* |
| Ω ω | omega | ō | long open *o*, as in *law* |

Two pairs need care: ε and η are both *e*, short and long; ο and ω both *o*. The names say it: *o-mikron* "small o", *ō-mega* "big o".

## Diphthongs

| Diphthong | Transliteration | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| αι | ai | as in *aisle* |
| ει | ei | long closed *e*, as in *they* |
| οι | oi | as in *oil* |
| αυ | au | as in *how* |
| ευ | eu | *e* + *u* |
| ου | ou | as in *food* |

In transliteration, υ is *y* alone and *u* in a diphthong: μῦθος *mythos*, οὐρανός *ouranos*.

Answers can be typed in Greek letters or in transliteration, with or without accents: λόγος, λογος and *logos* are one answer. The buttons under each exercise write the Greek letters. Because ε and η, ο and ω fall together when typed, the exercises on long and short vowels are choices.

::: summary
- 24 letters; ε/η short and long *e*, ο/ω short and long *o*; σ, at the end of a word ς.
- θ φ χ are *t p k* with breath: *th ph ch*; ξ ψ are *ks ps*; γ before γ κ ξ χ is *n*.
- Diphthongs αι ει οι αυ ευ ου; υ is *y*, in diphthongs *u*.
:::
