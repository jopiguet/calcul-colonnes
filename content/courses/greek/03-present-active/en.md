# 3. The verb: present active

A Greek verb carries its subject in its ending: λέγω is "I say", λέγομεν "we say". The pronoun is used only for emphasis. This lesson gives the present active of the verbs in -ω, the largest class, with λύω "I loose, I free" as the model, then the infinitive, the imperative and the negation.

## The present indicative

| Person | Ending | λύω loose | γράφω write |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | -ω | λύω I loose | γράφω |
| 2nd sg. | -εις | λύεις you loose | γράφεις |
| 3rd sg. | -ει | λύει he, she, it looses | γράφει |
| 1st pl. | -ομεν | λύομεν we loose | γράφομεν |
| 2nd pl. | -ετε | λύετε you loose | γράφετε |
| 3rd pl. | -ουσι(ν) | λύουσι(ν) they loose | γράφουσι(ν) |

The stem (λυ-, γραφ-) takes a thematic vowel, ο before μ and ν, ε elsewhere, then the ending. The ν in brackets, *movable nu*, is added before a vowel or at the end of a sentence: λύουσιν ἀνθρώπους.

The present has two meanings, as in English: λύω is "I loose" and "I am loosing".

## Infinitive and imperative

| Form | λύω | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| infinitive | λύειν | to loose |
| imperative, sg. | λῦε | loose! (one person) |
| imperative, pl. | λύετε | loose! (several) |

## Negation

The verb is denied with **οὐ**, which becomes **οὐκ** before a smooth breathing and **οὐχ** before a rough one: οὐ λέγει, οὐκ ἔχει, οὐχ ἕπεται. Commands and infinitives take **μή**: μὴ λέγε "don't speak".

A question is marked by the sign ; and often by ἆρα, which has no translation: ἆρα γράφεις; "are you writing?".

:::

::: summary
- Present: -ω, -εις, -ει, -ομεν, -ετε, -ουσι(ν); infinitive -ειν; imperative -ε, -ετε.
- The ending gives the subject; the pronoun is for emphasis.
- οὐ / οὐκ / οὐχ for statements, μή for commands and infinitives; ἆρα asks.
:::
