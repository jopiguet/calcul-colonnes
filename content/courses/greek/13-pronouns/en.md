# 13. Pronouns

The pronouns are short and frequent; a page of Plato has dozens. This lesson gives the personal pronouns, the three uses of αὐτός, the demonstratives "this" and "that", the relative "who, which" and the question word τίς "who?".

## Personal pronouns

| Case | I | you (sg.) | we | you (pl.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | ἐγώ | σύ | ἡμεῖς | ὑμεῖς |
| Gen. | ἐμοῦ, μου | σοῦ, σου | ἡμῶν | ὑμῶν |
| Dat. | ἐμοί, μοι | σοί, σοι | ἡμῖν | ὑμῖν |
| Acc. | ἐμέ, με | σέ, σε | ἡμᾶς | ὑμᾶς |

The short forms (μου, μοι, με) are unemphatic and lean on the word before them. The nominative is used for emphasis only: ἐγὼ μὲν λέγω, σὺ δὲ σιγᾷς "*I* am talking, but *you* are silent".

## αὐτός, three meanings

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| alone in an oblique case | ὁρῶ αὐτόν | I see him |
| next to a noun, outside the article | αὐτὸς ὁ βασιλεύς | the king himself |
| inside the article | ὁ αὐτὸς βασιλεύς | the same king |

αὐτός, αὐτή, αὐτό declines like ἀγαθός, with the neuter in -ο.

## This and that

| | m. | f. | n. |
|---|---|---|---|
| this | οὗτος | αὕτη | τοῦτο |
| that | ἐκεῖνος | ἐκείνη | ἐκεῖνο |

A demonstrative stands outside the article: οὗτος ὁ ἀνήρ or ὁ ἀνὴρ οὗτος "this man". οὗτος has τ- wherever the article has it: τούτου, ταύτης, τούτῳ, τοῦτον; plural οὗτοι, αὗται, ταῦτα.

## Relative and interrogative

The relative ὅς, ἥ, ὅ "who, which" agrees with its antecedent in gender and number and takes its case from its own clause: ὁ ἀνὴρ ὃν εἶδες "the man whom you saw". Its forms are those of the article with a rough breathing: ὅς, οὗ, ᾧ, ὅν; ἥ, ἧς, ᾗ, ἥν; ὅ.

τίς, τί "who? what?" (gen. τίνος) always has the acute on the ι. Without accent, τις, τι is "someone, something": ἀνήρ τις "a certain man".

::: summary
- ἐγώ, ἐμοῦ/μου, ἐμοί/μοι, ἐμέ/με; σύ; ἡμεῖς, ὑμεῖς; nominative for emphasis.
- αὐτός: him (oblique), self (outside the article), same (inside the article).
- οὗτος, αὕτη, τοῦτο; ἐκεῖνος; relative ὅς, ἥ, ὅ; τίς who?, τις someone.
:::
