# 2. Breathings, accents and punctuation

Every Greek word in a printed text carries small signs above its letters. They came late: scholars in Alexandria added them around 200 BC to help foreigners read Homer, and they became regular in manuscripts of the Middle Ages. They matter for reading aloud and they sometimes tell two words apart: ἡ "the" and ἤ "or". This lesson teaches you to read them; the course will not ask you to type them.

## Breathings

A word beginning with a vowel or a diphthong carries a **breathing** over that vowel (over the second letter of a diphthong):

| Sign | Name | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ἀ | smooth breathing | nothing | ἀγαθός *agathos* good |
| ἁ | rough breathing | *h* before the vowel | ἡμέρα *hēmera* day |
| ῥ | rough breathing on ρ | *rh* | ῥήτωρ *rhētōr* orator |
| οὐ, αἱ | on the second vowel | | οὐρανός *ouranos*, αἷμα *haima* |

Initial ρ and initial υ always take the rough breathing: ῥ, ὑ. On a capital the breathing stands before the letter: Ἑλλάς *Hellas* Greece.

## Accents

Greek had a pitch accent: the accented syllable was sung higher, not stressed. Three signs mark it:

| Sign | Name | Where |
|---|---|---|
| ά | acute | on one of the last three syllables; on the third from the end only if the last syllable is short |
| ᾶ | circumflex | rise and fall, on a long vowel or diphthong, on one of the last two syllables |
| ὰ | grave | replaces an acute on the last syllable when another word follows: ὁ θεὸς λέγει |

Verbs throw the accent as far back as the rules allow (*recessive*): λύομεν. Nouns keep it where the nominative has it, as far as the rules allow (*persistent*): ἄνθρωπος, ἀνθρώπου.

## Iota subscript and punctuation

A long ᾱ, η or ω followed by ι writes the ι small, under the vowel: ᾳ ῃ ῳ. It was no longer pronounced in later times; τῷ λόγῳ "to the word" is transliterated *tōi logōi* or *tō logō*.

| Sign | Value |
|---|---|
| . | full stop |
| , | comma |
| · | colon or semicolon (a raised dot) |
| ; | question mark |

So τί λέγεις; is "what are you saying?".

::: summary
- Breathings: smooth ἀ (nothing), rough ἁ (*h*); initial ρ and υ are rough: ῥ, ὑ.
- Accents: acute ά on the last three syllables, circumflex ᾶ on a long vowel in the last two, grave ὰ for a final acute before another word.
- ᾳ ῃ ῳ: iota subscript; ; is a question mark, · a colon.
:::
