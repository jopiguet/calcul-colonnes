# 5. The second declension and the article

The second declension holds the masculines in -ος, such as λόγος and ἄνθρωπος, and the neuters in -ον, such as δῶρον "gift". With it comes the rest of the article, which Greek uses much as English uses "the", and for more: with a proper name, with an abstract noun, with an infinitive.

## The endings

| Case | λόγος word (m.) | δῶρον gift (n.) |
|---|---|---|
| Nom. sg. | λόγος | δῶρον |
| Gen. sg. | λόγου | δώρου |
| Dat. sg. | λόγῳ | δώρῳ |
| Acc. sg. | λόγον | δῶρον |
| Voc. sg. | λόγε | δῶρον |
| Nom. pl. | λόγοι | δῶρα |
| Gen. pl. | λόγων | δώρων |
| Dat. pl. | λόγοις | δώροις |
| Acc. pl. | λόγους | δῶρα |

Two rules hold for every neuter, in every declension: nominative and accusative are the same, and in the plural they end in -α.

A few nouns in -ος are feminine: ἡ ὁδός "road", ἡ νῆσος "island", ἡ νόσος "illness". The article shows it.

## The article

| Case | m. | f. | n. | m. pl. | f. pl. | n. pl. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nom. | ὁ | ἡ | τό | οἱ | αἱ | τά |
| Gen. | τοῦ | τῆς | τοῦ | τῶν | τῶν | τῶν |
| Dat. | τῷ | τῇ | τῷ | τοῖς | ταῖς | τοῖς |
| Acc. | τόν | τήν | τό | τούς | τάς | τά |

There is no indefinite article: ἄνθρωπος is "a man", ὁ ἄνθρωπος "the man". Greek also says ὁ Σωκράτης "Socrates" and ἡ ἀρετή "virtue".

## The neuter plural takes a singular verb

τὰ δῶρα πείθει θεούς: "gifts persuade gods". A neuter plural subject is treated as one mass and has its verb in the singular.

::: summary
- Second declension: λόγος, -ου, -ῳ, -ον, -ε; -οι, -ων, -οις, -ους. Neuter δῶρον, pl. δῶρα.
- Neuters: nominative = accusative, plural in -α; a neuter plural subject takes a singular verb.
- Article ὁ ἡ τό; no indefinite article; ἡ ὁδός, ἡ νῆσος are feminine.
:::
