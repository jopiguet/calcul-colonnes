# 21. Conditions and indirect speech

Two pieces of syntax that hold whole paragraphs together. Greek conditions are precise: the mood and the particle ἄν say whether the speaker treats the condition as real, likely, remote or contrary to fact. Indirect speech has three constructions, chosen by the verb that introduces it.

## Conditions

| Kind | "if" clause | Main clause | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| simple, present or past | εἰ + indicative | indicative | εἰ τοῦτο λέγει, ἁμαρτάνει if he says this, he is wrong |
| future, likely | ἐάν + subjunctive | future | ἐὰν ἔλθῃ, ὄψεται if he comes, he will see |
| future, remote | εἰ + optative | optative + ἄν | εἰ ἔλθοι, ἴδοι ἄν if he were to come, he would see |
| contrary to fact, present | εἰ + imperfect | imperfect + ἄν | εἰ ἦν σοφός, οὐκ ἂν ἔλεγε ταῦτα if he were wise, he would not say this |
| contrary to fact, past | εἰ + aorist | aorist + ἄν | εἰ ἦλθεν, εἶδεν ἄν if he had come, he would have seen |
| general | ἐάν + subjunctive | present | ἐάν τις ἔλθῃ, ὁρᾷ whenever anyone comes, he sees |

The negation of the "if" clause is μή: εἰ μὴ ἦλθεν "if he had not come".

## Indirect speech

| Introduced by | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| λέγω, εἶπον (say) | ὅτι or ὡς + the original verb | λέγει ὅτι ὁ βασιλεὺς ἥκει he says that the king has come |
| φημί, νομίζω, οἴομαι (say, think) | infinitive, subject in the accusative | νομίζω τὸν βασιλέα ἥκειν I think the king has come |
| οἶδα, ὁράω, ἀκούω, ἀγγέλλω (know, see, report) | participle | οἶδα τὸν βασιλέα ἥκοντα I know that the king has come |

The tense of the original statement is kept: ἔφη τὸν βασιλέα ἥκειν "he said the king *had* come" (he said "the king has come"). When the subject is the same as that of the main verb, it stays in the nominative and is often left out: ἔφη σοφὸς εἶναι "he said he was wise". After a past main verb, the verb after ὅτι may change to the optative: εἶπεν ὅτι ὁ βασιλεὺς ἥκοι.

::: summary
- εἰ + indicative (simple); ἐάν + subjunctive / future (likely); εἰ + optative / optative + ἄν (remote).
- Contrary to fact: εἰ + imperfect or aorist, the same tense + ἄν; negation μή in the "if" clause.
- Indirect speech: ὅτι after "say", infinitive after φημί, νομίζω, participle after "know, see"; the tense is kept.
:::
