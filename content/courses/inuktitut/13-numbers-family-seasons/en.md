# 13. Numbers, family, seasons

Inuktitut numbers run in fives, like the fingers of a hand; in daily life many speakers count past five in English. The family words distinguish older and younger siblings, and the year turns through seasons named for ice and light. This lesson gives all three.

## Numbers

| | Inuktitut | Roman |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ᐊᑕᐅᓯᖅ | atausiq |
| 2 | ᒪᕐᕉᒃ | marruuk |
| 3 | ᐱᖓᓱᑦ | pingasut |
| 4 | ᓯᓴᒪᑦ | sisamat |
| 5 | ᑕᓪᓕᒪᑦ | tallimat |

- The number follows the noun, and the noun takes its number: ᕿᒻᒦᒃ ᒪᕐᕉᒃ *qimmiik marruuk* "two dogs", ᐃᒡᓗᑦ ᐱᖓᓱᑦ *iglut pingasut* "three houses".
- *marruuk* "two" itself ends in the dual *-k*; *pingasut*, *sisamat*, *tallimat* in the plural *-t*.
- Above five, the old numbers differ from community to community.

## Family

| Inuktitut | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ᐊᓈᓇᑦᓯᐊᖅ | grandmother |
| ᐊᑖᑕᑦᓯᐊᖅ | grandfather |
| ᐃᕐᓂᖅ | son |
| ᐸᓂᒃ | daughter |
| ᐊᖓᔪᒃ | older brother of a man, older sister of a woman |
| ᓄᑲᖅ | younger brother of a man, younger sister of a woman |
| ᓄᓕᐊᖅ | wife |
| ᐅᐃ | husband |

The grandparents are built with *-tsiaq-* "well, nicely": *anaana-tsiaq*, *ataata-tsiaq*. *angajuk* and *nukaq* name a sibling of one's own sex, older or younger.

:::

## Seasons

| Inuktitut | Season |
|---|---|
| ᐅᑭᐅᖅ | winter |
| ᐅᐱᕐᖔᖅ | spring |
| ᐊᐅᔭᖅ | summer |
| ᐅᑭᐊᒃᓴᖅ | autumn |

*ukiuq* also means "year": years are counted in winters.

- ᕿᒻᒦᑦ ᑕᓪᓕᒪᑦ → {{Five dogs.}}

::: summary
- ᐊᑕᐅᓯᖅ *atausiq* 1, ᒪᕐᕉᒃ *marruuk* 2, ᐱᖓᓱᑦ *pingasut* 3, ᓯᓴᒪᑦ *sisamat* 4, ᑕᓪᓕᒪᑦ *tallimat* 5; the number follows the noun.
- ᐊᓈᓇᑦᓯᐊᖅ *anaanatsiaq*, ᐊᑖᑕᑦᓯᐊᖅ *ataatatsiaq* grandparents; ᐃᕐᓂᖅ *irniq* son, ᐸᓂᒃ *panik* daughter; ᐊᖓᔪᒃ *angajuk*, ᓄᑲᖅ *nukaq* older, younger sibling.
- ᐅᑭᐅᖅ *ukiuq* winter, year; ᐅᐱᕐᖔᖅ *upirngaaq* spring; ᐊᐅᔭᖅ *aujaq* summer; ᐅᑭᐊᒃᓴᖅ *ukiaksaq* autumn.
:::
