# 8. One, two, many; mine and yours

Nouns count like pronouns: one, two, many. *Inuit*, "people", is simply the plural of *inuk*, "a person". "My" and "your" are endings on the noun, and one of them is in the name *Nunavut*. This lesson gives number and possession.

## Number

| One | Two | Many | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ᐃᒡᓗ | ᐃᒡᓘᒃ | ᐃᒡᓗᑦ | house |
| ᐃᓄᒃ | ᐃᓅᒃ | ᐃᓄᐃᑦ | person |
| ᕿᒻᒥᖅ | ᕿᒻᒦᒃ | ᕿᒻᒦᑦ | dog |

- Two ends in *-k*, many in *-t*.
- A final *k* or *q* drops before them, and the vowel before it is lengthened: *qimmiq*, *qimmiik*, *qimmiit*.
- *inuk* has the plural *inuit*: the people's name for themselves is "people".

:::

## Possession

| Owner | iglu house | anaana mother |
|---|---|---|
| my | ᐃᒡᓗᒐ | ᐊᓈᓇᒐ |
| your | ᐃᒡᓗᐃᑦ | ᐊᓈᓇᐃᑦ |
| his, her | ᐃᒡᓗᖓ | ᐊᓈᓇᖓ |
| our | ᐃᒡᓗᕗᑦ | ᐊᓈᓇᕗᑦ |

- *-ga* "my", *-it* "your", *-nga* "his, her", *-vut* "our".
- ᓄᓇᕗᑦ *nunavut* is *nuna* "land" with *-vut* "our": "our land".
- A final consonant drops before them: *qimmiq*, ᕿᒻᒥᒐ *qimmiga* "my dog", ᕿᒻᒥᖓ *qimminga* "her dog".
- *igluit* "your house" looks like a plural; the sentence tells them apart.

- her mother → [[ᐊᓈᓇᖓ]]

:::

Whose is it?

::: summary
- One, two, many: ᐃᒡᓗ *iglu*, ᐃᒡᓘᒃ *igluuk*, ᐃᒡᓗᑦ *iglut*; ᐃᓄᒃ *inuk*, ᐃᓅᒃ *inuuk*, ᐃᓄᐃᑦ *inuit*.
- *-k* two, *-t* many; a final *k*, *q* drops: ᕿᒻᒦᑦ *qimmiit*.
- *-ga* my, *-it* your, *-nga* his, her, *-vut* our: ᐃᒡᓗᒐ *igluga*, ᓄᓇᕗᑦ *nunavut*.
:::
