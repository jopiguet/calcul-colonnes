# 4. Greetings and pronouns

A first conversation in Inuktitut is short and full of words built from suffixes: "I am fine" is literally "I am not how". This lesson gives greetings and the pronouns, which count one, two, and more than two.

## Greetings

| Inuktitut | Roman | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ᐊᐃ | ai | hello |
| ᐅᓪᓛᒃᑯᑦ | ullaakkut | good morning |
| ᖃᓄᐃᑉᐱᑦ | qanuippit? | how are you? |
| ᖃᓄᐃᙱᑦᑐᖓ | qanuinngittunga | I am fine |
| ᓇᑯᕐᒦᒃ | nakurmiik | thank you |
| ᐃᓛᓕ | ilaali | you're welcome |
| ᐄ ᐋᒃᑲ | ii, aakka | yes, no |
| ᑭᓇᐅᕕᑦ | kinauvit? | who are you? |
| ᔮᓂᒥᒃ ᐊᑎᖃᖅᑐᖓ | Jaanimik atiqaqtunga | my name is Jaani |
| ᑕᕝᕙᐅᕗᑎᑦ | tavvauvutit | goodbye (to one person) |
| ᑕᕝᕙᐅᕗᓯ | tavvauvusi | goodbye (to several) |

- ᖃᓄᐃᑉᐱᑦ *qanuippit* is *qanuq* "how" with *-pit* "are you?"; ᖃᓄᐃᙱᑦᑐᖓ *qanuinngittunga* is *qanu-* "how", *-nngit-* "not", *-tunga* "I am".
- "My name is" is "I have as a name": *atiq* "name", *-qaq-* "have", *-tunga* "I"; the name takes *-mik*.
- Jaani is the Inuktitut form of John.

## Pronouns: one, two, many

Inuktitut counts people three ways: one, two, and more than two, the dual.

| | One | Two | Many |
|---|---|---|---|
| I, we | ᐅᕙᖓ *uvanga* | ᐅᕙᒍᒃ *uvaguk* | ᐅᕙᒍᑦ *uvagut* |
| you | ᐃᕝᕕᑦ *ivvit* | ᐃᓕᑦᑎᒃ *ilittik* | ᐃᓕᔅᓯ *ilissi* |

For "he, she, it, they", Inuktitut points: ᐅᓇ *una* "this one here", ᑖᓐᓇ *taanna* "that one". There is no gender: one word for "he" and "she".

::: summary
- ᐊᐃ *ai* hello, ᖃᓄᐃᑉᐱᑦ *qanuippit* how are you?, ᖃᓄᐃᙱᑦᑐᖓ *qanuinngittunga* I am fine, ᓇᑯᕐᒦᒃ *nakurmiik* thank you.
- ᐄ *ii* yes, ᐋᒃᑲ *aakka* no; ᑕᕝᕙᐅᕗᑎᑦ *tavvauvutit* goodbye to one, ᑕᕝᕙᐅᕗᓯ *tavvauvusi* to several.
- One, two, many: ᐅᕙᖓ *uvanga*, ᐅᕙᒍᒃ *uvaguk*, ᐅᕙᒍᑦ *uvagut*; ᐃᕝᕕᑦ *ivvit*, ᐃᓕᑦᑎᒃ *ilittik*, ᐃᓕᔅᓯ *ilissi*.
- ᐅᓇ *una* this one, ᑖᓐᓇ *taanna* that one: no gender.
:::
