# 1. Inuktitut and its syllabics

Inuktitut is the language of the Inuit of the eastern Canadian Arctic, spoken in Nunavut and Nunavik by some forty thousand people. It is written in syllabics, a script in which one shape stands for a consonant and its turn stands for a vowel. This lesson places the language and teaches how the script works.

## The Inuit languages

The Inuit languages run from Alaska to Greenland, and neighbours understand each other better than distant ones.

| Language | Where |
|---|---|
| Iñupiaq | northern Alaska |
| Inuvialuktun | western Canadian Arctic |
| Inuinnaqtun | western Nunavut |
| Inuktitut | eastern Nunavut, Nunavik (Quebec), Labrador |
| Kalaallisut | Greenland |

*Inuktitut* means "in the manner of the Inuit": ᐃᓄᒃ *inuk* is "a person", ᐃᓄᐃᑦ *inuit* "people". In Nunavut, Inuktitut and Inuinnaqtun together are called ᐃᓄᒃᑐᑦ *inuktut* and are official languages. This course teaches the Inuktitut of Nunavut, in syllabics and in the Roman spelling that goes with them.

## A shape for the consonant, a turn for the vowel

Syllabics were made in the 1840s for Cree and adapted for Inuktitut by missionaries later in the nineteenth century. Inuktitut has three vowels, *i*, *u*, *a*. Each consonant has one shape, turned four ways: three for the vowels and a small raised form for the consonant alone.

| | i | u | a | alone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| no consonant | ᐃ | ᐅ | ᐊ | |
| p | ᐱ | ᐳ | ᐸ | ᑉ |
| t | ᑎ | ᑐ | ᑕ | ᑦ |
| k | ᑭ | ᑯ | ᑲ | ᒃ |

A dot over a syllable makes its vowel long: ᐱ *pi*, ᐲ *pii*; ᑕ *ta*, ᑖ *taa*. The small raised form, the final, closes a syllable: ᐃᓄᒃ is *i-nu-k*, ᐃᓄᒃ *inuk*.

::: exercise read
Read the syllable.

- ᐳ → ((pi / pu* / pa))
- ᑕ → ((ti / tu / ta*))
- ᑭ → ((ki* / ku / ka))
- ᐊ → ((i / u / a*))
:::

::: exercise turn
Which way does the vowel turn the shape?

- ᐱ, then ᐳ: the vowel changes from i to → ((u* / a))
- ᑎ, then ᑕ: the vowel changes from i to → ((u / a*))
- ᑉ is → ((p alone* / pa long))
- ᐲ is → ((pi with a long vowel* / pi followed by a final))
:::

## Two spellings

Nunavut writes Inuktitut in syllabics and in a Roman spelling, letter for letter: a long vowel is written twice, *ii*, *uu*, *aa*. Signs, newspapers and books use syllabics; this course writes both, and accepts either in typed answers.

| Syllabics | Roman | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ᐃᓄᒃ | inuk | person |
| ᐃᓄᐃᑦ | inuit | people |
| ᓄᓇ | nuna | land |
| ᓄᓇᕗᑦ | nunavut | "our land": Nunavut |
| ᐃᒡᓗ | iglu | house |

::: exercise write
Write the word in syllabics or in Roman spelling; the table helps.

- person → [[ᐃᓄᒃ]]
- land → [[ᓄᓇ]]
- house → [[ᐃᒡᓗ]]
- people → [[ᐃᓄᐃᑦ]]
:::

::: exercise meaning
What does the word mean?

- ᓄᓇᕗᑦ → ((our land* / the people / the house))
- ᐃᓄᒃ → ((land / a person* / people))
- ᐃᓄᒃᑎᑐᑦ → ((in the manner of the Inuit* / the land of the Inuit / one Inuk))
- ᐃᒡᓗ → ((a house* / land / a person))
:::

::: summary
- Inuktitut is spoken in Nunavut and Nunavik; with Inuinnaqtun it forms ᐃᓄᒃᑐᑦ *inuktut*.
- Syllabics: one shape per consonant, turned for *i*, *u*, *a*; a small raised final for the consonant alone.
- A dot makes the vowel long: ᐱ *pi*, ᐲ *pii*; Roman doubles it: *ii*, *uu*, *aa*.
- ᐃᓄᒃ *inuk* a person, ᐃᓄᐃᑦ *inuit* people, ᓄᓇ *nuna* land, ᐃᒡᓗ *iglu* house.
:::
