# 12. "A dog" or "the dog"

Inuktitut says "I see a dog" and "I see the dog" with two different verb forms. For "a dog", the verb keeps its usual ending and the noun takes *-mik*; for "the dog", the verb takes endings that name both the doer and the thing, and the noun stays bare. This lesson gives both, and the case that marks the doer.

## Some dog, the dog

| Inuktitut | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ᕿᒻᒥᒥᒃ ᑕᑯᔪᖓ | I see a dog |
| ᕿᒻᒥᖅ ᑕᑯᔭᕋ | I see the dog |

- With *-mik*, the thing is any one, new to the talk: *qimmiq*, ᕿᒻᒥᒥᒃ *qimmimik*.
- The ending -ᔭᕋ *-jara* means "I … it": the thing is known, already spoken of.
- The same choice exists for every verb that takes an object: ᑐᒃᑐᒥᒃ ᓂᕆᔪᖓ *tuktumik nirijunga* "I eat caribou meat", ᑐᒃᑐ ᓂᕆᔭᕋ *tuktu nirijara* "I eat the caribou".

## I it, you it, he it

| Doer | taku- see | niri- eat |
|---|---|---|
| I … it | ᑕᑯᔭᕋ | ᓂᕆᔭᕋ |
| you … it | ᑕᑯᔭᐃᑦ | ᓂᕆᔭᐃᑦ |
| he, she … it | ᑕᑯᔭᖓ | ᓂᕆᔭᖓ |

After a consonant the endings start with *t*, as before.

## The doer: -up

When the doer of a "the" verb is a noun, it takes *-up* (after a vowel) and the verb takes "he … it":

| Inuktitut | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ᐊᓈᓇᐅᑉ ᕿᒻᒥᖅ ᑕᑯᔭᖓ | Mother sees the dog |
| ᐊᑖᑕᐅᑉ ᑐᒃᑐ ᓂᕆᔭᖓ | Father eats the caribou |

The same *-up* means "of": ᐊᓈᓇᐅᑉ ᐃᒡᓗᖓ *anaanaup iglunga* "mother's house", "of mother her house". Linguists call this an ergative pattern: the doer of a verb with an object is marked, the doer of a verb without one is not, ᐊᓈᓇ ᐱᓱᒃᑐᖅ *anaana pisuktuq* "mother walks".

::: summary
- "A": the noun takes *-mik*, the verb its usual ending: ᕿᒻᒥᒥᒃ ᑕᑯᔪᖓ *qimmimik takujunga*.
- "The": the noun is bare, the verb names doer and thing: ᕿᒻᒥᖅ ᑕᑯᔭᕋ *qimmiq takujara*.
- -ᔭᕋ *-jara* I … it, -ᔭᐃᑦ *-jait* you … it, -ᔭᖓ *-janga* he, she … it.
- A noun doing it takes *-up*, which also means "of": ᐊᓈᓇᐅᑉ ᐃᒡᓗᖓ *anaanaup iglunga*.
:::
