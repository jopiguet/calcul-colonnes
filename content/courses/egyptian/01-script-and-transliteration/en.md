# 1. The script and its transliteration

Hieroglyphs are pictures, but they are not picture-writing: most signs stand for sounds, and only the consonants are written. This course teaches **Middle Egyptian**, the classical language of the Middle Kingdom (about 2000–1650 BC), read and copied until the end of pharaonic Egypt. This first lesson shows how the script works, which way to read it, and how Egyptologists write it in Latin letters: the **transliteration** you will use in every exercise.

## Three kinds of sign

| Kind | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Phonogram | stands for one, two or three consonants | 𓂋 *r*, 𓉐 *pr*, 𓄤 *nfr* |
| Logogram | stands for a whole word, often with a stroke 𓏤 | 𓉐𓏤 *pr* "house" |
| Determinative | silent; shows the class of meaning at the end of a word | 𓀀 a man, 𓁐 a woman, 𓇳 the sun, time |

One sign can play several roles: 𓉐 is the sound *pr* in *prj* "go out", the word "house" with its stroke, and the determinative of buildings.

## Which way to read

Hieroglyphs run right to left, left to right, or in columns. The rule: **the signs face the beginning of the line**. Read towards the faces of the people and animals. In this course the signs are printed left to right, as in most grammars.

Signs are grouped in balanced squares (quadrats): a low sign under a low sign, a tall sign alone. Within a group, read top before bottom, front before back.

## No vowels

Like Hebrew or Arabic, Egyptian writes consonants only. *nfr* "good" was perhaps pronounced *nāfir*; the Coptic descendant says *noufe*. Egyptologists therefore read with conventional vowels: an *e* between consonants, *ꜣ* and *ꜥ* read *a*, *j* read *i*, *w* read *u*. So *nfr* is read *nefer*, *ꜥnḫ* "life" *ankh*, *rꜥ* "sun" *ra*, *wsjr* Osiris *usir*.

## The transliteration alphabet

Twenty-five letters, in the order of the dictionaries:

| Letter | Sound | Read | Letter | Sound | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ꜣ | a glottal stop or *l*-like sound | a | ḫ | *ch* in Scottish *loch* | kh |
| j | *y*, or a glottal stop | i | ẖ | *ch* in German *ich* | kh |
| y | *y* | y | z | *z*, merged with *s* by the Middle Kingdom | s |
| ꜥ | a sound made in the throat (Arabic ع) | a | s | *s* | s |
| w | *w* | u, w | š | *sh* | sh |
| b | *b* | b | ḳ (q) | a *k* deep in the throat | k |
| p | *p* | p | k | *k* | k |
| f | *f* | f | g | *g* | g |
| m | *m* | m | t | *t* | t |
| n | *n* | n | ṯ | *tch* | tj |
| r | *r* | r | d | an emphatic *d* or *t* | d |
| h | *h* | h | ḏ | *dj* | dj |
| ḥ | a strong *h* (Arabic ح) | h | | | |

Grammatical endings are separated by a dot: *sḏm.n.f* "he heard" (some grammars write *sḏm.n=f*). In the exercises, you may type the special letters with the buttons, or as you read them: *ankh*, *nefer*, *sedjem*. The answer is compared by its consonants.

::: summary
- Phonograms (sounds), logograms (words, with 𓏤), determinatives (silent class signs).
- Read towards the faces of the signs; top before bottom.
- Only consonants are written. Egyptological reading: *e* between consonants, *ꜣ ꜥ* = *a*, *j* = *i*, *w* = *u*.
- Twenty-five letters: ꜣ j y ꜥ w b p f m n r h ḥ ḫ ẖ z s š ḳ k g t ṯ d ḏ; endings after a dot: *sḏm.n.f*.
:::
