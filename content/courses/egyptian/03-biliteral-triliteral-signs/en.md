# 3. Biliteral and triliteral signs

Most hieroglyphic words are not spelled letter by letter. Many signs stand for two consonants (**biliterals**) or three (**triliterals**), and the scribe added uniliterals beside them to confirm the reading: the **phonetic complements**. This lesson gives the most frequent multiliteral signs and the rule for complements, which lets you read the commonest words of the language.

## Phonetic complements

A multiliteral sign is usually followed (or framed) by uniliterals that repeat **its last consonant, or its last two**. They are not read a second time.

| Spelling | Sign + complements | Read |
|---|---|---|
| 𓋹𓈖𓐍 | ꜥnḫ + n + ḫ | *ꜥnḫ*, not *ꜥnḫnḫ* |
| 𓄤𓆑𓂋 | nfr + f + r | *nfr* |
| 𓊵𓏏𓊪 | ḥtp + t + p | *ḥtp* |
| 𓏠𓈖 | mn + n | *mn* |
| 𓆣𓂋 | ḫpr + r | *ḫpr* |
| 𓁹𓂋 | jr + r | *jr* (in *jrj*) |
| 𓇋𓏠𓈖 | j + mn + n | *jmn*, Amun |

The rule gives the reading of a new sign: if 𓅨𓂋 is *wr*, the swallow is *wr* and the mouth only confirms the *r*.
