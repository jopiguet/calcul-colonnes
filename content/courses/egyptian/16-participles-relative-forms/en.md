# 16. Participles, relative forms and ntj

English has "who" and "which"; Egyptian builds relative clauses differently. A **participle** is a verb used as an adjective: "the one who hears", "the man who did". A **relative form** is a verb used as an adjective with its own subject: "which he heard", "what the god loves". And the relative adjective ***ntj*** "who is, which is" introduces sentences without a verb. All three agree with the noun they describe, like any adjective.

## Participles

| Participle | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| active, imperfective (repeated) | *jrr* | who does, who makes (habitually) |
| active, perfective (once) | *jr* | who did, who made |
| passive | *mrj* | loved, beloved |

Weak verbs double in the imperfective: *jrr*, *mrr*, *mꜣꜣ*. Like adjectives, participles take *-t* for the feminine and *-w* for the plural, and can be used as nouns:

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *s jr st* | the man who did it |
| *ḥmt jrt st* | the woman who did it |
| *jrrw mꜣꜥt* | those who do what is right |
| *mrj Jmn* | beloved of Amun (a royal epithet) |
| *ḏd mdw* | who speaks words |

The passive participle takes the agent directly: *mrj Jmn* "loved (by) Amun", *ḥsj nb.f* "praised by his lord".

## Relative forms

A relative form is a participle with a subject: it says what someone does to the noun.

| Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| imperfective | *mrrt nṯr* | what the god loves |
| perfective (with *.n*) | *jrt.n.f* | what he did |
| with an antecedent | *mdw ḏdw.n.f* | the words that he said |

It agrees with the noun it describes (*-t*, *-w*), not with its subject. Without a noun, the feminine means "what, that which": *jrt.n.f* "what he did".

## The relative adjective ntj

For a relative clause without a verb (adverbial, with a preposition), Egyptian uses *ntj*, agreeing with its noun:

| Form | Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ntj* | masculine singular | *pr ntj sw jm.f* | the house in which he is |
| *ntt* | feminine singular | *nwt ntt m tꜣ pn* | the town that is in this land |
| *ntjw* | plural | *ntjw m pr* | those who are in the house |

Its negative is *jwtj* (lesson 15): *jwtj sꜣ.f* "who has no son".

::: summary
- Participles: active *jrr* (repeated), *jr* (once), passive *mrj*; agree like adjectives; *mrj Jmn* beloved of Amun.
- Relative forms: participle + subject: *mrrt nṯr* what the god loves, *jrt.n.f* what he did; agree with the noun described.
- *ntj, ntt, ntjw* for clauses without a verb: *pr ntj sw jm.f*; negative *jwtj*.
:::
