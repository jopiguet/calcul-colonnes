# 6. The genitive and adjectives

"The house of the king", "a good son", "the beautiful sister": Egyptian builds noun phrases by simple juxtaposition, in a fixed order. The modifier always **follows** the noun. This lesson shows the two genitives, the agreement of adjectives, and the **nisbe**, an adjective made from a noun or a preposition that gives Egyptian many of its titles.

## Two genitives

| Construction | Form | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct genitive | noun + noun | *pr nswt* | the king's house |
| Indirect genitive | noun + *n / nt / nw* + noun | *sꜣ n ḥqꜣ* | a son of the ruler |

The direct genitive is used for close, fixed links (titles, names, the body): *nb tꜣwj* "lord of the Two Lands", *ḥr nṯr* "the god's face". The indirect genitive uses a linking word that **agrees with the first noun**:

| First noun | Linking word | Example |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | *n* 𓈖 | *sꜣ n ḥqꜣ* a son of the ruler |
| feminine singular | *nt* 𓈖𓏏 | *sꜣt nt ḥqꜣ* a daughter of the ruler |
| plural (both genders) | *nw* 𓏌 | *snw nw ḥqꜣ* brothers of the ruler |

**Honorific transposition**: a word for the king or a god is written first out of respect, but read in its grammatical place. 𓇓𓏏𓈖𓉐𓏤 is written "king-house" but read *pr nswt*.

## Adjectives

An adjective follows its noun and takes the same endings:

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | *sꜣ nfr* a good son | *sꜣt nfrt* a good daughter |
| Plural | *snw nfrw* good brothers | *snwt nfrwt* good sisters |

An adjective can stand alone as a noun, the feminine for an abstract or a thing: *nfrt* "something good, goodness", *bjnt* "evil". An adjective followed by a noun says "of": *nfr ḥr* "beautiful of face", *wr pḥtj* "great of strength".

## The nisbe adjective

Adding *-j* to a noun or a preposition makes an adjective meaning "belonging to, the one who is…". It is called the *nisbe*, an Arabic grammatical term for the same thing.

| Base | Nisbe | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *nwt* town | *nwtj* | local, of the town |
| *m* in | *jmj* | who is in |
| *ḥr* upon | *ḥrj* | who is upon, upper, chief |
| *ẖr* under | *ẖrj* | who is under, lower |
| *tp* head | *tpj* | who is at the head, first |
| *nṯr* god | *nṯrj* | divine |

Nisbes make titles: *jmj-r pr* "steward" (literally "who is at the mouth of the house"), *ḥrj-tp* "chief", *ḥrj-ḥbt* "lector priest" (who carries the ritual book).

::: summary
- Direct genitive *pr nswt*; indirect genitive with *n* (m.), *nt* (f.), *nw* (pl.), agreeing with the first noun.
- Honorific transposition: king or god written first, read in place.
- Adjectives follow and agree: *-t*, *-w*, *-wt*. *nfr ḥr* beautiful of face.
- Nisbe *-j*: *jmj*, *ḥrj*, *ẖrj*, *tpj*, *nṯrj*; titles *jmj-r pr*, *ḥrj-tp*.
:::
