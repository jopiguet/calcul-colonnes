# 12. Verb classes and the infinitive

An Egyptian verb is a root of two to five consonants. Its forms are made by adding endings, and sometimes by doubling or dropping a consonant, so the first thing to know about a verb is its **class**: the shape of its root. This lesson sets out the classes, then the simplest verb form, the **infinitive**, and its most common use: *jw.f ḥr sḏm* "he is hearing", the Egyptian progressive.

## Verb classes

Grammars name the classes after their number of consonants and their weak point:

| Class | Root | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| strong 2-lit. | 2 consonants | *ḏd*, *mn* | say; remain |
| strong 3-lit. | 3 consonants | *sḏm*, *ꜥnḫ* | hear; live |
| 3ae-inf. ("third weak") | last consonant *j* or *w*, often unwritten | *mrj*, *prj*, *jrj*, *ḥsj* | love; go out; do; praise |
| 2ae-gem. ("second doubled") | last two consonants the same | *mꜣꜣ*, *wnn* | see; be, exist |
| irregular | | *rdj*, *jj*, *jnj* | give; come; bring |

The weak *j* of 3ae-inf. verbs disappears in many forms: *mrj* "love" is written *mr*. Geminating verbs show their doubled consonant in some forms only: *mꜣꜣ.f* "he sees" but *mꜣ.n.f* "he saw".

## The infinitive

The infinitive names the action: "to hear, hearing". It is a masculine noun, except in the weak classes, which add *-t*:

| Class | Root | Infinitive |
|---|---|---|
| strong | *sḏm* | *sḏm* |
| strong | *ḏd* | *ḏd* |
| 3ae-inf. | *mrj* | *mrt* |
| 3ae-inf. | *prj* | *prt* |
| 3ae-inf. | *jrj* | *jrt* |
| 2ae-gem. | *mꜣꜣ* | *mꜣꜣ* |
| irregular | *rdj* | *rdjt* |
| irregular | *jj* | *jwt* or *jjt* |
| irregular | *jnj* | *jnt* |

As a noun, the infinitive takes a suffix or a genitive. The suffix can be its subject or its object: *mrt.f* "his loving" or "loving him"; *sḏm mdw* "hearing words".

## The progressive: jw.f ḥr sḏm

The preposition *ḥr* "on" + infinitive means "in the course of doing": the action is going on.

| Construction | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| subject + *ḥr* + infinitive | *jw.f ḥr sḏm* | he is hearing, he hears |
| with an object | *jw.f ḥr sḏm mdw* | he is listening to the words |
| verb of motion: *m* + infinitive | *jw.f m jwt* | he is coming |
| after *mk* | *mk wj ḥr ḏd* | look, I am speaking |

This is the adverbial sentence of lesson 9 with an infinitive inside the prepositional phrase. Grammars call it the **pseudo-verbal construction**.

::: summary
- Classes: strong (*sḏm*, *ḏd*), 3ae-inf. (*mrj*, *prj*, *jrj*: weak *j*), 2ae-gem. (*mꜣꜣ*, *wnn*), irregular (*rdj*, *jj*, *jnj*).
- Infinitive: the root; 3ae-inf. add *-t* (*mrt*, *prt*, *jrt*); *rdjt*, *jwt*, *jnt*.
- Progressive: *jw.f ḥr* + infinitive "he is doing"; with verbs of motion *m* + infinitive.
:::
