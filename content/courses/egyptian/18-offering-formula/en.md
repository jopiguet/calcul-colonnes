# 18. The offering formula

The most copied text of ancient Egypt is not a story but a formula. Carved on thousands of stelae, false doors, coffins and statues, the **offering formula** asked the king and a god to provide the dead with food, drink and goods for ever, and kept the dead person's name alive by being read aloud. It uses almost everything you have learned: honorific transposition, the subjunctive, the genitive, nisbes and participles. After this lesson you can read the core of most funerary stelae in any museum.

## The formula

𓇓𓏏𓈖𓊵𓏏𓊪𓂞 *ḥtp-dj-nswt Wsjr nb Ḏdw nṯr ꜥꜣ nb ꜣbḏw*

*dj.f prt-ḫrw t ḥnqt kꜣ ꜣpd šs mnḫt ḫt nbt nfrt wꜥbt ꜥnḫt nṯr jm*

*n kꜣ n jmꜣḫ NN mꜣꜥ-ḫrw*

"An offering which the king gives to Osiris, lord of Busiris, the great god, lord of Abydos, that he may give an invocation offering of bread and beer, cattle and fowl, alabaster and linen, every good and pure thing on which a god lives, for the ka of the revered NN, true of voice."

## Part by part

| Part | Transliteration | Meaning | Grammar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *ḥtp-dj-nswt* | an offering which the king gives | *nswt* written first: honorific transposition |
| 2 | *Wsjr nb Ḏdw* | (to) Osiris, lord of Busiris | direct genitive |
| 3 | *dj.f* | that he may give | subjunctive *sḏm.f* of *rdj* |
| 4 | *prt-ḫrw* 𓉓 | invocation offering, "going out at the voice" | compound noun, one sign |
| 5 | *t ḥnqt kꜣ ꜣpd* | bread, beer, cattle, fowl | list without "and" |
| 6 | *ḫt nbt nfrt wꜥbt* | every good and pure thing | noun + adjectives, feminine |
| 7 | *ꜥnḫt nṯr jm* | on which a god lives | relative form, feminine |
| 8 | *n kꜣ n* | for the ka of | preposition + indirect genitive |
| 9 | *jmꜣḫ* 𓄪 | the revered one | title of the honoured dead |
| 10 | *mꜣꜥ-ḫrw* | true of voice, justified | said of the dead after judgement |

Anubis often replaces Osiris: *Jnpw tpj ḏw.f, jmj wt, nb tꜣ ḏsr* "Anubis who is on his mountain, who is in the place of embalming, lord of the sacred land".

## Why it worked

The bread and beer did not need to be real. Saying the formula, or reading it, was the offering: *prt-ḫrw* is literally "what comes out at the voice". Stelae address the passers-by: *j ꜥnḫw tpjw tꜣ* "O you living ones upon earth … say: a thousand of bread and beer". Reading these lines aloud today does exactly what they asked.

::: summary
- *ḥtp-dj-nswt* + god: "an offering which the king gives (to) …"; *nswt* first by honorific transposition.
- *dj.f prt-ḫrw* + goods: "that he may give an invocation offering of …": subjunctive.
- *n kꜣ n jmꜣḫ NN mꜣꜥ-ḫrw*: "for the ka of the revered NN, true of voice".
- Saying the formula was the offering: *prt-ḫrw*, "what comes out at the voice".
