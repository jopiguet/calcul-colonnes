# 7. Suffix pronouns

"His house", "to her", "he hears": in Egyptian all three use the same small pronouns, attached to the end of a word. These **suffix pronouns** are the most frequent words in any text. After a noun they mean "my, your, his…", after a preposition "me, you, him…", after a verb they are its subject. Transliteration joins them with a dot: *pr.f* "his house".

## The forms

| Person | Suffix | Written | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg. | *.j* | 𓀀 (a woman 𓁐), or not written | *pr.j* my house |
| 2nd sg. m. | *.k* | 𓎡 | *pr.k* your house (to a man) |
| 2nd sg. f. | *.ṯ* | 𓍿 | *pr.ṯ* your house (to a woman) |
| 3rd sg. m. | *.f* | 𓆑 | *pr.f* his house |
| 3rd sg. f. | *.s* | 𓋴 | *pr.s* her house |
| 1st pl. | *.n* | 𓈖𓏥 | *pr.n* our house |
| 2nd pl. | *.ṯn* | 𓍿𓈖𓏥 | *pr.ṯn* your house (to several) |
| 3rd pl. | *.sn* | 𓋴𓈖𓏥 | *pr.sn* their house |

The 1st person singular is drawn as the speaker: the seated man for a man, the seated woman for a woman, a seated god for a god. It is often omitted. In Middle Egyptian the 2nd singular feminine *.ṯ* is already often written *.t*.

## Three uses

| After | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a noun | possessive | *sꜣ.f* his son; *ḥmt.k* your wife |
| a preposition | object of the preposition | *n.f* to him, for him; *ḥr.s* on her; *jm.sn* in them |
| a verb | subject | *sḏm.f* he hears; *ḏd.s* she says |

A feminine noun keeps its *t* before the suffix: *ḥmt.f* his wife, *nbt.s* her lady. The preposition *m* "in" becomes *jm* before a suffix: *jm.f* "in it".

A noun subject replaces the suffix: *sḏm.f* "he hears", *sḏm sꜣ* "the son hears".

::: summary
- *.j* (𓀀 𓁐), *.k*, *.ṯ*, *.f*, *.s*; *.n*, *.ṯn*, *.sn*.
- After a noun: possessive; after a preposition: its object (*n.f*, *jm.f*); after a verb: its subject (*sḏm.f*).
- A feminine noun keeps *t*: *ḥmt.f*. A noun subject replaces the suffix: *sḏm sꜣ*.
:::
