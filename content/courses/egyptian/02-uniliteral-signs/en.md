# 2. The uniliteral signs

About twenty-five signs stand for a single consonant each. They are often called the hieroglyphic "alphabet", although the Egyptians never wrote with them alone: they mixed them with signs of two and three consonants and with determinatives. Every word can be spelled with them, and foreign names (Ptolemy, Cleopatra) were. Learn this table by heart; everything else is built on it. The Gardiner number after each sign is how Egyptologists name it in sign lists.

Two easy confusions: the owl 𓅓 *m* and the vulture 𓄿 *ꜣ*; the cobra 𓆓 *ḏ* and the viper 𓆑 *f*. The owl is the only bird that looks at you.

## Spelling a word

A word is read sign by sign, in the reading direction, top before bottom:

| Signs | Letters | Word |
|---|---|---|
| 𓂋𓈖 | r + n | *rn* "name" |
| 𓇋𓏏𓆑 | j + t + f | *jt* "father" (the viper is part of the old spelling *jtf*) |
| 𓆓𓂧 | ḏ + d | *ḏd* "to say" |
| 𓊪𓏏𓇯 | p + t + sky | *pt* "sky" |
| 𓂝𓈎 | ꜥ + ḳ | *ꜥḳ* "to enter" |

The last sign of *pt*, 𓇯, is the picture of the sky itself: a determinative, not read. You will meet determinatives in lesson 4.
