# 20. The Quran: three short suras

The last suras of the Quran are short, rhythmic and among the earliest revealed. They are learnt by heart by children and recited in daily prayer. Three of them follow: al-Ikhlāṣ, on the oneness of God; al-ʿAṣr, on time and loss; an-Nās, a prayer for refuge. Each verse ends on a rhyme, *-ad*, *-r*, *-ās*, which you will hear as you read aloud.

## Sura 112, al-Ikhlāṣ, "Sincerity"

| Verse | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝١ | Say: He is God, One, |
| 2 | ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ ۝٢ | God, the Eternal Refuge. |
| 3 | لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝٣ | He did not beget, nor was He begotten, |
| 4 | وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ ۝٤ | and there is none comparable to Him. |

قُلْ is the imperative of قَالَ (lesson 11). لَمْ + jussive three times (lesson 12), the second one passive: يُولَدْ. In verse 4, كُفُوًا is the predicate of يَكُنْ in the accusative (lesson 13) and أَحَدٌ its subject, placed last.

## Sura 103, al-ʿAṣr, "Time"

| Verse | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | وَٱلْعَصْرِ ۝١ | By time, |
| 2 | إِنَّ ٱلْإِنْسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ ۝٢ | man is surely in loss, |
| 3 | إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا ٱلصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِٱلصَّبْرِ ۝٣ | except those who believe and do good works, and urge one another to the truth, and urge one another to patience. |

وَ with the genitive at the start is an oath: "By time". إِنَّ with لَـ for stress (lesson 13). تَوَاصَوْا is form VI, "they urged each other" (lesson 10).

## Sura 114, an-Nās, "Mankind"

| Verse | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝١ | Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, |
| 2 | مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝٢ | the King of mankind, |
| 3 | إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝٣ | the God of mankind, |
| 4 | مِنْ شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ ۝٤ | from the evil of the whisperer who withdraws, |
| 5 | ٱلَّذِي يُوَسْوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝٥ | who whispers in the hearts of mankind, |
| 6 | مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ ۝٦ | from among the jinn and mankind. |

Three iḍāfas with the same second noun: رَبِّ، مَلِكِ، إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ. The text is given in common print spelling; the translations are of the meaning.
