# 1. The alphabet

Arabic is written from right to left in an alphabet of 28 letters, all consonants; the vowels are added as small marks, or left out. Most letters join to the next one and change shape slightly with their place in the word. The same alphabet, with a few additions, writes Persian, Urdu and once Turkish and Malay; it is also the script of the Quran, which fixed the classical language. In this course Arabic comes in its script with a transliteration in italics: كِتَاب *kitāb* "book".

The four **emphatic** letters ص ض ط ظ are said with the back of the tongue raised; they darken the vowels around them. Arabic calls itself *lughat aḍ-ḍād*, "the language of ḍād", because that sound is its own.

## Joining

Six letters never join to the next one: ا د ذ ر ز و. Everything else joins on both sides.

| Letter | Alone | Initial | Medial | Final |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ب | ب | بـ | ـبـ | ـب |
| ع | ع | عـ | ـعـ | ـع |
| ه | ه | هـ | ـهـ | ـه |
| ي | ي | يـ | ـيـ | ـي |

## Typing Arabic in this course

Answers can be typed in the script, with or without vowel marks, or in transliteration with or without dots and lines: كتاب, كِتَاب, *kitāb* and *kitab* are one answer.
