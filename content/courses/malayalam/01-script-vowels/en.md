# 1. The script and the vowels

Malayalam is the language of Kerala, the strip of coast in the south-west of India, and of the Lakshadweep islands; some 35 million people speak it. It is a Dravidian language, related to Tamil, Kannada and Telugu, not to Hindi; but it took a great many words from Sanskrit, and with them the sounds Sanskrit needs.

Its script descends, like all Indian scripts, from Brahmi. It is an *abugida*: a consonant letter carries the vowel *a* unless a sign changes it, and a vowel gets a letter of its own only at the start of a word. Malayalam is written left to right, without capitals. Its rounded letters are said to come from writing on palm leaves, which a straight stroke would have split.

## The vowels

| Letter | Sound | Like | Letter | Sound | Like |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| അ | *a* | about | ആ | *ā* | father |
| ഇ | *i* | bit | ഈ | *ī* | machine |
| ഉ | *u* | put | ഊ | *ū* | rule |
| എ | *e* | bet | ഏ | *ē* | café, held |
| ഒ | *o* | for, short | ഓ | *ō* | go, held |
| ഐ | *ai* | eye | ഔ | *au* | how |
| ഋ | *r̥* | *ri* or *ru* | | | |

Length matters: a short and a long vowel make different words. ഋ is a Sanskrit letter, read *ri* or *ru*, and found in learned words. Each long vowel is held about twice as long as its short one.

## Transliteration

This course writes Malayalam in Latin letters with the ISO 15919 system: a line over a long vowel (*ā*), a dot under a retroflex consonant (*ṭ*, *ṇ*, *ḷ*), *ḻ* for the letter written *zh* in English (Kozhikode). Online, Malayalis type "Manglish": *aa* or *a* for *ā*, *ee* for *ī*, *oo* for *ū*. Both are accepted in the exercises, and so is the script.

The **🔤 Letter cards** of the course (on its page) drill every letter with spaced repetition: the letter shown, its sound asked, then the sound shown and the letter chosen.

::: summary
- Malayalam: Kerala, Dravidian, much Sanskrit vocabulary; a Brahmi script written left to right.
- Vowels in pairs, short and long: അ ആ, ഇ ഈ, ഉ ഊ, എ ഏ, ഒ ഓ; and ഐ, ഔ, ഋ.
- Vowel letters stand at the start of a word; elsewhere a vowel is a sign on a consonant.
- Answers in the script, in ISO 15919 (*ā*, *ṭ*, *ḻ*) or in Manglish (*aa*, *ee*, *zh*).
:::
