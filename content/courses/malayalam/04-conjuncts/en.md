# 4. Conjuncts and reading

When two consonants meet with no vowel between them, Malayalam usually fuses them into one **conjunct** (കൂട്ടക്ഷരം *kūṭṭakṣaraṁ*). Doubled consonants are everywhere, since Malayalam loves them: അമ്മ *amma*, പൂച്ച *pūcca*. A conjunct may also be written as the first letter with ് and the second after it: ക്ക and ക്‌ക are the same *kka*.

## Common conjuncts

## Special readings

Two conjuncts are not read as written: റ്റ is *ṯṯa*, a doubled alveolar *t* (കാറ്റ് *kāṯṯŭ* "wind"), and ന്റ is *nṯa*, close to *nda* (എന്റെ *enṯe* "my"). A small ്യ, ്ര or ്വ attached to a letter adds *y*, *r* or *v*: വിദ്യ *vidya*, ക്രമം *kramaṁ*, സ്വരം *svaraṁ*. ജ്ഞ, in Sanskrit words, is read *jña* or *gña*: ജ്ഞാനം *jñānaṁ* "knowledge".

## Old and new script

In 1971 Kerala simplified printing to suit typewriters: many conjuncts were split with a visible ്, and the *u* signs were detached. Computers brought the traditional shapes back, so both styles are printed today, often side by side. A word does not change by being written one way or the other.
