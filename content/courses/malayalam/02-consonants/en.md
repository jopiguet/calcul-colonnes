# 2. The consonants

The consonants are set out as in Sanskrit: by where the mouth makes them, from the throat forwards, each row in the same order: plain, aspirated, voiced, voiced aspirated, nasal. Every consonant letter is read with the vowel *a*: ക is *ka*.

## The five rows

| | plain | aspirated | voiced | voiced aspirated | nasal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| throat | ക *ka* | ഖ *kha* | ഗ *ga* | ഘ *gha* | ങ *ṅa* |
| palate | ച *ca* | ഛ *cha* | ജ *ja* | ഝ *jha* | ഞ *ña* |
| retroflex | ട *ṭa* | ഠ *ṭha* | ഡ *ḍa* | ഢ *ḍha* | ണ *ṇa* |
| teeth | ത *ta* | ഥ *tha* | ദ *da* | ധ *dha* | ന *na* |
| lips | പ *pa* | ഫ *pha* | ബ *ba* | ഭ *bha* | മ *ma* |

ച *ca* is the *ch* of "church". The **retroflex** row is made with the tip of the tongue curled back against the roof of the mouth; the **dental** row with the tongue against the teeth. English *t* sits between the two, so a learner must work at both. The aspirates (ഖ, ഥ…) and the voiced letters (ഗ, ദ…) are frequent in Sanskrit words, rarer in native ones.

## The others

| Letter | Sound | Letter | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| യ | *ya* | ശ | *śa*, soft *sh* |
| ര | *ra*, a tap | ഷ | *ṣa*, retroflex *sh* |
| ല | *la* | സ | *sa* |
| വ | *va*, between *v* and *w* | ഹ | *ha* |
| ള | *ḷa*, retroflex *l* | ഴ | *ḻa*, written *zh* |
| റ | *ṟa*, a trilled *r* | | |

ഴ *ḻa* is the pride of Malayalam (and Tamil): the tongue curls back as for ട but does not touch, and the sound is between an *r* and an American *l*. It is in മഴ *maḻa* "rain", and in കോഴിക്കോട് *Kōḻikkōṭŭ* (Kozhikode). The language's own name, മലയാളം, has ള, the retroflex *l*, not ഴ; it comes from മല *mala* "mountain".

::: summary
- Five rows of five, throat to lips: plain, aspirated, voiced, voiced aspirated, nasal.
- Retroflex ട ഠ ഡ ഢ ണ (tongue curled back) against dental ത ഥ ദ ധ ന (tongue on the teeth).
- യ ര ല വ, ശ ഷ സ ഹ, and the three Dravidian letters ള *ḷa*, ഴ *ḻa*, റ *ṟa*.
- A consonant letter alone is read with *a*: മല *mala*.
