# 3. Vowel signs, the virama and the chillus

After a consonant, a vowel is not written with its letter but with a **sign** attached to the consonant: on the right, on the left, below, or on both sides. The sign replaces the *a* the consonant would otherwise have.

## The signs, on ക

| Vowel | Sign | With ക | Vowel | Sign | With ക |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *a* | — | ക *ka* | *ā* | ാ | കാ *kā* |
| *i* | ി | കി *ki* | *ī* | ീ | കീ *kī* |
| *u* | ു | കു *ku* | *ū* | ൂ | കൂ *kū* |
| *e* | െ | കെ *ke* | *ē* | േ | കേ *kē* |
| *o* | ൊ | കൊ *ko* | *ō* | ോ | കോ *kō* |
| *ai* | ൈ | കൈ *kai* | *au* | ൗ | കൗ *kau* |
| *r̥* | ൃ | കൃ *kr̥* | | | |

The signs for *e*, *ē* and *ai* stand **before** the consonant though they are read after it; *o*, *ō* and *au* wrap around it. In the reformed print of 1971 the *u* and *ū* signs stand apart after the letter (കു); traditional type joins them to it, sometimes in a new shape. Both are read the same.

## No vowel at all

| Mark | Name | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ് | *candrakkala*, virama | kills the *a*; at the end of a word a short, unrounded *ŭ* is heard | വീട് *vīṭŭ* house |
| ം | *anusvāraṁ* | *m* at the end of a syllable | മരം *maraṁ* tree |
| ഃ | *visargaṁ* | a breath, in Sanskrit words | ദുഃഖം *duḥkhaṁ* sorrow |

The **chillus** (ചില്ല്) are consonants written without a vowel and without a virama: ൻ *n*, ർ *r*, ൽ *l*, ൾ *ḷ*, ൺ *ṇ*. They end many words: അവൻ *avan* "he", കടൽ *kaṭal* "sea". Where a word ends in a chillu, nothing follows; where it ends in ്, the half-u *ŭ* is heard (often typed *u*).

::: exercise mark
What does the mark do?

- ് at the end of വീട് → ((a short ŭ is heard* / the word is plural / the vowel is long))
- ം in മരം → ((m* / n / a long a))

:::

::: summary
- A vowel after a consonant is a sign: ാ ി ീ ു ൂ ൃ െ േ ൈ ൊ ോ ൗ; െ േ ൈ stand before the letter.
- ് removes the *a*; at the end of a word it is read *ŭ*: വീട് *vīṭŭ*.
- ം is *m* (മരം), ഃ a breath; the chillus ൻ ർ ൽ ൾ ൺ end a syllable with no vowel.
:::
