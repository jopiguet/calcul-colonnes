# 1. Imperial Aramaic

Aramaic was the language of the Aramaeans of Syria, written from the 9th century BCE. The Assyrian and then the Persian empires made it the language of their administration, from Egypt to the Indus: this written standard, used from about 600 to 330 BCE, is called Imperial or Official Aramaic. This lesson gives its script and the first eleven letters.

## A script of twenty-two consonants

- The script has 22 letters, in the order of the Phoenician alphabet, the order Hebrew still keeps. It is written right to left, and words are separated by spaces.
- Only consonants are written. ʾ (aleph), h, w and y also mark some long vowels: 𐡁𐡉𐡕 *byt* is *bayt*, "house"; 𐡉𐡅𐡌 *ywm* is *yōm*, "day".
- The transliteration gives the consonants alone: 𐡌𐡋𐡊 *mlk*, "king", was probably said *malk*. You type the transliteration: the keys under the answer give ʾ ʿ ḥ ṭ ṣ š, ʾ can also be typed ', and vowels you add are ignored.
- The Hebrew square letters, the Nabataean script that became the Arabic one, Syriac, and Sogdian, mother of the Uyghur and Mongolian scripts, all come from this one.

## The first eleven letters

| Letter | Sound | Name |
|---|---|---|
| 𐡀 | ʾ | aleph |
| 𐡁 | b | beth |
| 𐡂 | g | gimel |
| 𐡃 | d | daleth |
| 𐡄 | h | he |
| 𐡅 | w | waw |
| 𐡆 | z | zayin |
| 𐡇 | ḥ | heth |
| 𐡈 | ṭ | teth |
| 𐡉 | y | yodh |
| 𐡊 | k | kaph |

- ʾ is a glottal stop, the catch in English *uh-oh*. At the end of a word it often only marks a vowel *ā*.
- ḥ is an h pressed in the throat, as Arabic ح; ṭ is an "emphatic" t, said further back. You may type them without the dot; the exercises with choices tell them apart.
- In this script 𐡃 and 𐡓 (next lesson) are nearly the same sign: a small head on a stroke. The word tells which one it is.

## First words

| Letters | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 𐡀𐡁 | ʾb | father |
| 𐡀𐡇 | ʾḥ | brother |
| 𐡀𐡇𐡕 | ʾḥt | sister |
| 𐡁𐡉𐡕 | byt | house |
| 𐡉𐡃 | yd | hand |
| 𐡀𐡋𐡄 | ʾlh | god |

- 𐡀𐡋𐡄 *ʾlh* was said *ʾelāh*: the same word as Hebrew *ʾĕlōah* and Arabic *ʾilāh*.
- 𐡀𐡇𐡕 *ʾḥt* is 𐡀𐡇 *ʾḥ* with the feminine *-t*.
