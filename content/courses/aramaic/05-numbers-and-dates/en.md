# 5. Numbers and dates

The scribes wrote numbers with signs of their own, not with letters. Contracts begin with a date: the day, the month and the year of the king. This lesson reads the number signs and the dating of a document.

## The number signs

| Sign | Value | Sign | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| 𐡘 | 1 | 𐡛 | 10 |
| 𐡙 | 2 | 𐡜 | 20 |
| 𐡚 | 3 | 𐡝 | 100 |

- Units are strokes, grouped by threes; 10 is a curved stroke, 20 a sign of its own. Hundreds and thousands have their own signs, with the units that count them.
- A number is read right to left, the largest value first, and the values are added: 𐡜𐡛 is 20 + 10 = 30, 𐡛𐡚 is 13.
- Words for numbers exist, but documents give amounts and dates in signs.

## The date of a contract

| Letters | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 𐡉𐡅𐡌 | ywm | day |
| 𐡉𐡓𐡇 | yrḥ | month |
| 𐡔𐡍𐡄 | šnh | year |
| 𐡔𐡍𐡕 | šnt | year of |
| 𐡍𐡉𐡎𐡍 | nysn | Nisan, the first month |
| 𐡕𐡔𐡓𐡉 | tšry | Tishri, the seventh month |
| 𐡔𐡒𐡋 | šql | shekel |

𐡔𐡍𐡕 𐡛𐡚 𐡃𐡓𐡉𐡄𐡅𐡔 𐡌𐡋𐡊𐡀

"Year 13 of Darius the king." The years count from the king's accession; 𐡔𐡍𐡕 *šnt* is 𐡔𐡍𐡄 *šnh* followed by its owner.

- The months bear their Babylonian names, as in the Jewish calendar today: 𐡍𐡉𐡎𐡍 *nysn* Nisan in spring, 𐡕𐡔𐡓𐡉 *tšry* Tishri in autumn.
- Many contracts give two dates, the Babylonian and the Egyptian month.
- The 𐡔𐡒𐡋 *šql*, shekel, was a weight, of silver: 𐡊𐡎𐡐 𐡔𐡒𐡋𐡍 𐡛, *ksp šqln 10*, "silver, 10 shekels" (*šqln* is the plural).

:::

- 𐡔𐡍𐡕 𐡜𐡛𐡘 𐡀𐡓𐡕𐡇𐡔𐡎𐡔 𐡌𐡋𐡊𐡀 → ((year 31 of Artaxerxes the king* / year 13 of Artaxerxes the king / year 31 of Darius the king))
:::

::: summary
- Number signs: 𐡘 1, 𐡙 2, 𐡚 3, 𐡛 10, 𐡜 20, 𐡝 100; read right to left, largest first, added.
- 𐡉𐡅𐡌 *ywm* day, 𐡉𐡓𐡇 *yrḥ* month, 𐡔𐡍𐡄 *šnh* year, 𐡔𐡍𐡕 *šnt* year of; 𐡍𐡉𐡎𐡍 *nysn* Nisan, 𐡕𐡔𐡓𐡉 *tšry* Tishri; 𐡔𐡒𐡋 *šql* shekel.
- 𐡔𐡍𐡕 𐡛𐡚 𐡃𐡓𐡉𐡄𐡅𐡔 𐡌𐡋𐡊𐡀 *šnt 13 dryhwš mlkʾ*: year 13 of Darius the king.
:::
