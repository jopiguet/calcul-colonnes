# 1. Cyrillic: the first letters

Bulgarian is a South Slavic language, spoken by about seven million people, and written in the Cyrillic alphabet, which was born in Bulgaria. This lesson tells where the alphabet comes from and gives its first twelve letters: six that look and sound as in English, six that look like Latin letters but are not.

## An alphabet from Preslav

- In 863 the brothers Cyril and Methodius, from Thessaloniki, made the first Slavic alphabet, the Glagolitic, for a mission to Moravia. Their disciples, driven out, came to Bulgaria.
- At the end of the 9th century, in the schools of the First Bulgarian Empire, a simpler alphabet took over: Greek capitals and new letters for Slavic sounds. It was named after Cyril: the Cyrillic.
- Bulgarian has 30 letters. Its spelling is close to the sound: each letter is read the same way, almost always.
- The stress is free and not written: дума *dúma*, "word". A dictionary shows it.
- Since 2007 Cyrillic has been the third alphabet of the European Union, after Latin and Greek.

| О о | o |
| Т т | t |

## Six false friends

| Letter | Sound |
|---|---|
| В в | v |
| Н н | n |
| Р р | r (rolled) |
| С с | s |
| У у | u |
| Х х | h |

- В is *v*, Н is *n*, Р is *r*, С is *s*: the shape is Greek, the sound is not the English one.
- У is the vowel *u* of "put"; Х is a rough *h*, as in Scottish *loch*.

::: summary
- Cyrillic was made in Bulgaria at the end of the 9th century, after the Glagolitic of Cyril and Methodius; Bulgarian has 30 letters, read as written; the stress is not written.
- As in English: а a, е e, к k, м m, о o, т t.
- False friends: в v, н n, р r, с s, у u, х h.
- мама, татко, сок, нос, метро, ресторант.
