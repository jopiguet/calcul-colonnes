# 3. Reading the Elder Futhark

The Elder Futhark wrote Proto-Norse and other early Germanic languages, older than any written in the Latin alphabet in the North. Its inscriptions are short: a name, an owner, a maker, a word of power. This lesson reads the most famous one, on the golden horn of Gallehus.

## The horn of Gallehus

Two golden horns were found near Gallehus, in southern Denmark, in 1639 and 1734. They were stolen from the royal collection in Copenhagen in 1802 and melted down; what survives are drawings and copies. The shorter horn, from about 400, bore a line of runes:

ᛖᚲ ᚺᛚᛖᚹᚨᚷᚨᛊᛏᛁᛉ ᚺᛟᛚᛏᛁᛃᚨᛉ ᚺᛟᚱᚾᚨ ᛏᚨᚹᛁᛞᛟ

*ek hlewagastiz holtijaz horna tawido*: "I, Hlewagastiz of Holt, made the horn."

| Runes | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ᛖᚲ | ek | I |
| ᚺᛚᛖᚹᚨᚷᚨᛊᛏᛁᛉ | hlewagastiz | Hlewagastiz, a name: "shelter-guest" or "fame-guest" |
| ᚺᛟᛚᛏᛁᛃᚨᛉ | holtijaz | of Holt, or Holt's son |
| ᚺᛟᚱᚾᚨ | horna | the horn |
| ᛏᚨᚹᛁᛞᛟ | tawido | made |

- ᛖᚲ *ek* is the old form of English *I*, Danish and Norwegian *jeg*, German *ich*.
- *-az* and *-iz* end the subject, *-a* the object: *horna* "the horn" is what was made.
- The rune ᛉ ends *hlewagastiz* and *holtijaz*; in later Norse this ending became *-r*.
- The maker signs his work: "I, X, made", is the oldest formula in runes.

## Words of the inscriptions

| Runes | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ᚱᚢᚾᛟ | runo | rune, secret |
| ᚨᛚᚢ | alu | a word of power, meaning debated |
| ᛚᚨᚦᚢ | laþu | invitation, summons |
| ᛖᚱᛁᛚᚨᛉ | erilaz | rune master, a title |

- ᚨᛚᚢ *alu* appears on many gold pendants, the bracteates. It may be the word for "ale", drunk in rites, or a word of protection: no one knows.
- Many inscriptions are signed by an ᛖᚱᛁᛚᚨᛉ *erilaz*, a rune master: "I, the rune master..."
- *runo* means both a written sign and a secret: German *raunen* "to whisper" is the same root.
