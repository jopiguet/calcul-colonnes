# 1. The Elder Futhark

Runes are the letters the Germanic peoples carved before, and for a long time beside, the Latin alphabet. The oldest finds date from the 2nd century AD, in Denmark and northern Germany; the last runes were still cut in Scandinavian villages in the 19th century. This lesson gives the oldest rune row, the Elder Futhark, and its first eight runes.

## A row of twenty-four

The Elder Futhark has 24 runes. The row is named after its first six: ᚠᚢᚦᚨᚱᚲ *fuþark*. Its order is not that of the Latin alphabet, and it is old: complete rows are carved on stones and brooches from around 400.

- The runes are angular. They were cut into wood, bone and metal, and a stroke along the grain of the wood would vanish, so there are almost no horizontal lines.
- Most inscriptions run left to right, some right to left, a few turn back on each line. Words are often run together, or separated by dots: ᛫ or ᛬.
- The row is split into three groups of eight, called *ættir* (singular *ætt*), "families".
- Each rune has a name that is a word beginning with its sound. The names of the Elder Futhark are not written anywhere: they are reconstructed from the later rune poems of England, Norway and Iceland.

## The first ætt

| Rune | Sound | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ᚠ | f | *fehu* | cattle, wealth |
| ᚢ | u | *ūruz* | aurochs |
| ᚦ | þ (th) | *þurisaz* | giant |
| ᚨ | a | *ansuz* | a god |
| ᚱ | r | *raidō* | ride, journey |
| ᚲ | k | *kaunan* | ulcer, or torch |
| ᚷ | g | *gebō* | gift |
| ᚹ | w | *wunjō* | joy |

- ᚦ *þ* is the sound of English *th* in *thing*. The letter þ, "thorn", still used in Icelandic, comes from this rune. You can type *th* for it.
- Wealth was counted in cattle: *fehu* is the same word as German *Vieh* and English *fee*.
- ᚷ is a cross, not an *x*: it is *g*.
