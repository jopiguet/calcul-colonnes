# 2. The second and third ætt

The other sixteen runes of the Elder Futhark complete the row. With them you can write any word of the oldest Norse and Germanic inscriptions, and, letter by letter, any word of English.

## The second ætt

| Rune | Sound | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ᚺ | h | *hagalaz* | hail |
| ᚾ | n | *naudiz* | need |
| ᛁ | i | *īsaz* | ice |
| ᛃ | j | *jēra* | year, harvest |
| ᛇ | ï | *eihwaz* | yew |
| ᛈ | p | *perþō* | unknown |
| ᛉ | z | *algiz* | elk (uncertain) |
| ᛊ | s | *sōwilō* | sun |

- ᛃ *j* is the sound of English *y* in *year*: *jēra* is the same word.
- ᛇ is a vowel whose exact sound is debated; it is written *ï* or *ei*. You can type *i*.
- ᛉ *z* was a sound between *z* and *r*. In later Norse it became *r*, which is why it is also written *R*.
- ᛈ *p* is rare: early Germanic had few words with *p*, and the meaning of *perþō* is lost.

## The third ætt

| Rune | Sound | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ᛏ | t | *tīwaz* | the god Týr |
| ᛒ | b | *berkanan* | birch |
| ᛖ | e | *ehwaz* | horse |
| ᛗ | m | *mannaz* | man, person |
| ᛚ | l | *laguz* | water, lake |
| ᛜ | ŋ (ng) | *ingwaz* | the god Ing |
| ᛞ | d | *dagaz* | day |
| ᛟ | o | *ōþila* | inherited land |

- ᛜ *ŋ* is the *ng* of *sing*. You can type *ng*.
- *tīwaz* is the god whose day is *Tuesday*; *dagaz* is English *day*.
- The last two places vary: some rows end ᛞ ᛟ, others ᛟ ᛞ. In the second ætt, ᛇ and ᛈ swap places in the same way.

::: summary
- Second ætt: ᚺ *h* hagalaz, ᚾ *n* naudiz, ᛁ *i* īsaz, ᛃ *j* jēra, ᛇ *ï* eihwaz, ᛈ *p* perþō, ᛉ *z* algiz, ᛊ *s* sōwilō.
- Third ætt: ᛏ *t* tīwaz, ᛒ *b* berkanan, ᛖ *e* ehwaz, ᛗ *m* mannaz, ᛚ *l* laguz, ᛜ *ŋ* ingwaz, ᛞ *d* dagaz, ᛟ *o* ōþila.
- ᛃ is the *y* of *year*, ᛜ the *ng* of *sing*, ᛉ later became *r*.
- The order of ᛇ ᛈ and of ᛞ ᛟ varies from row to row.
