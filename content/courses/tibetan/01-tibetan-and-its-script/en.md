# 1. Tibetan and its script

Tibetan is spoken across the Tibetan plateau and the Himalaya, in many varieties; its script has been written for some thirteen centuries. This course teaches the standard language of Lhasa, written in Tibetan script. This lesson places the language and shows the three ways a Tibetan word is given here: in script, in Wylie transliteration, and as pronounced.

## The language

Tibetan belongs to the Sino-Tibetan family, with Burmese and, more distantly, Chinese. Its spoken varieties, the dialects of Ü-Tsang (with Lhasa), Kham and Amdo, differ as much as separate languages, but they share one written language. Standard Tibetan, based on the speech of Lhasa, is the common spoken form; it is also used in exile communities in India and Nepal.

Tibetans call their country བོད *bod*, said *pö*, and their language བོད་སྐད *bod skad*, *pö-ké*, "the speech of Tibet".

## The script

The Tibetan script was made from an Indian script in the seventh century; tradition credits the minister Thönmi Sambhota. It is an abugida, like the scripts of India: each consonant letter carries the vowel *a* unless another vowel sign is added.

| Sign | Name | Use |
|---|---|---|
| ཀ | ka | a consonant with its vowel *a* |
| ་ | tsheg | the dot after every syllable |
| ། | shad | the stroke that ends a phrase |

Words are not separated by spaces: syllables are, by the *tsheg* dot. བཀྲ་ཤིས་བདེ་ལེགས། is four syllables, *bkra*, *shis*, *bde*, *legs*, and a shad.

## Three ways to write a word

Tibetan spelling keeps the sounds of a thousand years ago; Lhasa speech has dropped many of them. This course gives each word three ways.

| Script | Wylie | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| བོད | bod | pö | Tibet |
| བོད་སྐད | bod skad | pö-ké | the Tibetan language |
| ལྷ་ས | lha sa | lhasa | Lhasa, "place of the gods" |
| བཀྲ་ཤིས་བདེ་ལེགས | bkra shis bde legs | tashi delek | hello, good luck |

- **Wylie** writes each letter of the spelling with Latin letters; it is the standard transliteration and is accepted in typed answers.
- The pronunciation, in italics, is a simple guide to Lhasa speech.

::: summary
- Standard Tibetan is the language of Lhasa; the dialects of Kham and Amdo differ, the written language is shared.
- The script is an abugida: ཀ is *ka*; the *tsheg* ་ separates syllables, the *shad* ། ends a phrase.
- Each word is given in script, in Wylie (བོད *bod*) and as said (*pö*).
- བོད *bod* Tibet, བོད་སྐད *bod skad* Tibetan, ལྷ་ས *lha sa* Lhasa, བཀྲ་ཤིས་བདེ་ལེགས *bkra shis bde legs* hello.
:::
