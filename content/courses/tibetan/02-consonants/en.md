# 2. The thirty letters

The Tibetan alphabet has thirty consonants, recited in rows of four, as Indian alphabets are. Each letter is said with the vowel *a*. Their columns matter for speech: in Lhasa, the first two columns are said with a high tone, the last two with a low one. This lesson gives the thirty letters.

## The alphabet

| | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k | ཀ ka | ཁ kha | ག ga | ང nga |
| c | ཅ ca | ཆ cha | ཇ ja | ཉ nya |
| t | ཏ ta | ཐ tha | ད da | ན na |
| p | པ pa | ཕ pha | བ ba | མ ma |
| ts | ཙ tsa | ཚ tsha | ཛ dza | ཝ wa |
| zh | ཞ zha | ཟ za | འ 'a | ཡ ya |
| r | ར ra | ལ la | ཤ sha | ས sa |
| h | ཧ ha | ཨ a | | |

- In the first four rows, column 1 is plain (*ka*), column 2 breathed (*kha*, *k* with a puff of air), column 3 written voiced (*ga*), column 4 nasal (*nga*).
- In Lhasa, in the first five rows, columns 1 and 2 take a high tone, columns 3 and 4 a low one; *ga*, *da*, *ba* are said almost like *ka*, *ta*, *pa* on a low pitch. In the last rows, *zha za 'a ya ra la* are low, *sha sa ha a* high.
- ང *nga* is the sound at the end of English "sing", here at the start of a syllable: ང *nga* means "I".
- འ, the *a-chung*, is mostly silent; ཨ, the *a-chen*, carries a vowel at the start of a word.

## First words

Some words are a single letter or two, each with its vowel *a*:

| Script | Wylie | Said | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| ང | nga | nga | I |
| ཇ | ja | ja | tea |
| ཤ | sha | sha | meat |
| ས | sa | sa | earth, ground |
| ཀ་བ | ka ba | kawa | pillar |

::: summary
- Thirty letters in rows of four: ཀ ཁ ག ང, and so on to ཧ ཨ.
- Column 1 plain, 2 breathed, 3 written voiced, 4 nasal; in Lhasa, in the first rows, 1 and 2 high tone, 3 and 4 low.
- Each letter carries *a*: ང *nga* I, ཇ *ja* tea, ཤ *sha* meat, ས *sa* earth.
:::
