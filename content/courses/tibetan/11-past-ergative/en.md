# 11. The past, and who did it

Many Tibetan verbs have a separate past stem: ཟ *za* "eat", བཟས *bzas* "ate"; འགྲོ *'gro* "go", ཕྱིན *phyin* "went". In the past, the one who acts on something takes a particle of its own, the ergative: ང *nga* "I" becomes ངས *ngas*. This lesson gives the past endings and the ergative.

## Past stems

| Present | Past | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ཟ | བཟས | eat |
| འཐུང | བཏུངས | drink |
| འགྲོ | ཕྱིན | go |
| འབྲི | བྲིས | write |
| ཀློག | ཀློགས | read |

The past stems are learnt with the verb, as English *go*, *went*.

## Past endings

| Who | Ending | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| I, we | པ་ཡིན | ང་ལྷ་སར་ཕྱིན་པ་ཡིན། | I went to Lhasa. |
| he, she | སོང | ཁོ་ལྷ་སར་ཕྱིན་སོང་། | He went to Lhasa. |

- པ་ཡིན *pa yin* tells what I did on purpose.
- སོང *song* tells what another did, which the speaker saw or knows.
- "Not" in the past is མ *ma* before the past stem: ང་མ་ཕྱིན། "I did not go".

## The ergative

When a verb acts on something (eat food, write a letter), its doer takes the ergative particle, spelt like the genitive with *s*:

| Last letter | Ergative | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a vowel | ས, joined | ངས I, ཁོས he, མོས she |
| ག ང | གིས | ཁོང་གིས he (respectful) |
| ད བ ས | ཀྱིས | བཀྲ་ཤིས་ཀྱིས Tashi |
| ན མ ར ལ | གྱིས | བསྟན་འཛིན་གྱིས Tenzin |

| Example | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ངས་ཁ་ལག་བཟས་པ་ཡིན། | I ate (food). |
| ཁོས་ཁ་ལག་བཟས་སོང་། | He ate. |
| ངས་ཡི་གེ་བྲིས་པ་ཡིན། | I wrote a letter. |
| ང་ལྷ་སར་ཕྱིན་པ་ཡིན། | I went to Lhasa: no object, no ergative. |

::: summary
- Past stems: བཟས *bzas* ate, བཏུངས *btungs* drank, ཕྱིན *phyin* went, བྲིས *bris* wrote, ཀློགས *klogs* read.
- པ་ཡིན for what I did, སོང for what another did; མ + past stem for "did not".
- The doer of a verb with an object takes the ergative: ངས *ngas*, ཁོས *khos*, གིས, ཀྱིས, གྱིས.
:::
