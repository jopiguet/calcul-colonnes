# 19. Psalm 23 and Ecclesiastes

Biblical poetry does not rhyme. It thinks in pairs: the second half of a line repeats, sharpens or answers the first. This *parallelism* is easy to see in the Psalms, and it is why they translate well. The second reading is from Ecclesiastes (*Kohelet*), the Bible's most sceptical book, read in synagogues at the autumn festival of Sukkot. Read יְהוָה as *Adonai*, as in the last lesson.

## Psalm 23:1–4

**1** מִזְמוֹר לְדָוִד יְהוָה רֹעִי לֹא אֶחְסָר׃

**2** בִּנְאוֹת דֶּשֶׁא יַרְבִּיצֵנִי עַל־מֵי מְנֻחוֹת יְנַהֲלֵנִי׃

**3** נַפְשִׁי יְשׁוֹבֵב יַנְחֵנִי בְמַעְגְּלֵי־צֶדֶק לְמַעַן שְׁמוֹ׃

**4** גַּם כִּי־אֵלֵךְ בְּגֵיא צַלְמָוֶת לֹא־אִירָא רָע כִּי־אַתָּה עִמָּדִי שִׁבְטְךָ וּמִשְׁעַנְתֶּךָ הֵמָּה יְנַחֲמֻנִי׃

## Suffixes everywhere

The psalm is full of "me" and "my". On a noun, ־ִי means "my"; on a verb, ־נִי means "me".

| Form | Parts | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| רֹעִי *ro'i* | רֹעֶה shepherd + ־ִי | my shepherd |
| יַרְבִּיצֵנִי *yarbitseni* | he makes lie down + ־נִי | he makes me lie down |
| יְנַהֲלֵנִי *yenahaleni* | he leads + ־נִי | he leads me |
| נַפְשִׁי *nafshi* | נֶפֶשׁ + ־ִי | my soul |
| שְׁמוֹ *shmo* | שֵׁם name + ־וֹ | his name |
| שִׁבְטְךָ *shivtekha* | שֵׁבֶט rod + ־ְךָ | your rod |

Line 2 is a clean parallel pair: "in green pastures he makes me lie down // beside still waters he leads me". צַלְמָוֶת is "deep darkness"; the old reading as צֵל + מָוֶת "shadow of death" gave the famous English and French renderings.

## Ecclesiastes 1:2 and 3:1

הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים אָמַר קֹהֶלֶת הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים הַכֹּל הָבֶל׃

לַכֹּל זְמָן וְעֵת לְכָל־חֵפֶץ תַּחַת הַשָּׁמָיִם׃

הֶבֶל *hevel* is "breath, vapour": what is there and gone. A construct with its own plural is the Hebrew superlative: הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים "breath of breaths", that is "utter futility", like שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים "the Song of Songs", the finest song. עֵת and זְמָן both mean "time"; in Modern Hebrew זְמַן is the everyday word and עֵת the literary one.

## Translation

A psalm of David. The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not lack. In green pastures he makes me lie down; beside still waters he leads me. He restores my soul; he guides me in paths of righteousness for his name's sake. Even when I walk through the valley of deep darkness, I will fear no evil, for you are with me; your rod and your staff, they comfort me.

"Utter futility," said Kohelet, "utter futility; everything is futile." For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under the heavens.

::: summary
- Biblical poetry works in parallel pairs, not rhyme.
- Psalm 23: ־י "my" on nouns (רעי, נפשי), ־ני "me" on verbs (ינחני); שמו his name, שבטך your rod.
- Ecclesiastes: הבל "breath"; הבל הבלים = construct + plural superlative, like שיר השירים; עת and זמן "time".
:::
