# 12. Smikhut: noun + noun

Hebrew joins two nouns by putting them side by side: בֵּית סֵפֶר *beit sefer* "house of book", that is "school". The first noun is in the construct state (*smikhut*, "leaning"): it leans on the second, often changes shape, and never takes the article. The second noun is in its normal form and carries the article for the whole phrase.

## How the first noun changes

| Ending | Free form | Construct | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ־ָה | עוּגָה *uga* cake | עוּגַת *ugat* | עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד chocolate cake |
| ־ִים | יְלָדִים *yeladim* children | יַלְדֵי *yaldei* | יַלְדֵי הַכִּתָּה the children of the class |
| ־וֹת | שָׁנוֹת *shanot* years | שְׁנוֹת *shnot* | שְׁנוֹת הַשִּׁשִּׁים the sixties |
| irregular | בַּיִת *bayit* house | בֵּית *beit* | בֵּית חוֹלִים hospital |

So ־ָה becomes ־ַת, ־ִים becomes ־ֵי, and ־וֹת stays ־וֹת. Nouns that already end in ־ת, such as מְכוֹנִית "car", do not change. Without niqqud only the first two are visible: עוגה → עוגת, ילדים → ילדי.

## Where the article goes

| Indefinite | Definite |
|---|---|
| בֵּית סֵפֶר a school | בֵּית הַסֵּפֶר the school |
| עוּגַת שׁוֹקוֹלָד a chocolate cake | עוּגַת הַשּׁוֹקוֹלָד the chocolate cake |
| חֲדַר אֹכֶל a dining room | חֲדַר הָאֹכֶל the dining room |

An adjective comes after the whole phrase and agrees with the first noun: בֵּית סֵפֶר גָּדוֹל "a big school". Many everyday words are smikhut: בֵּית כְּנֶסֶת synagogue, עוֹרֵךְ דִּין lawyer, יוֹם הֻלֶּדֶת birthday, כַּדּוּר רֶגֶל football.

Spoken Hebrew often prefers שֶׁל when the phrase is not a fixed term: הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁל הַמּוֹרֶה "the teacher's book".

::: summary
- Smikhut: first noun in construct, second in normal form; the article goes on the second.
- ־ה → ־ת (עוגה → עוגת), ־ים → ־י (ילדים → ילדי), ־ות unchanged; בית → בית.
- An adjective follows the whole phrase and agrees with the first noun.
:::
