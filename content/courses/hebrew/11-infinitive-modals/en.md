# 11. Infinitive, modals and negation

The infinitive starts with לְ־ "to": לִכְתֹּב *likhtov* "to write", לִלְמֹד *lilmod* "to learn", לְדַבֵּר *ledaber* "to speak". It is the form that follows every modal verb and every verb of wanting or beginning.

## Infinitives by binyan

| Binyan | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| pa'al | לִ־ ־ֹ־ | לִכְתֹּב *likhtov* to write |
| pi'el | לְ־ ־ַ־ ־ֵ־ | לְדַבֵּר *ledaber* to speak |
| hif'il | לְהַ־ ־ִי־ | לְהַתְחִיל *lehatchil* to begin |
| hitpa'el | לְהִתְ־ | לְהִתְלַבֵּשׁ *lehitlabesh* to get dressed |

## Modals

| Meaning | m. sg. | f. sg. | pl. |
|---|---|---|---|
| want | רוֹצֶה *rotse* | רוֹצָה *rotsa* | רוֹצִים *rotsim* |
| can | יָכוֹל *yakhol* | יְכוֹלָה *yekhola* | יְכוֹלִים *yekholim* |
| must | צָרִיךְ *tsarikh* | צְרִיכָה *tsrikha* | צְרִיכִים *tsrikhim* |

They agree with the subject like any present participle: אֲנִי רוֹצָה לִלְמֹד "I (f.) want to learn". Two impersonal words need no agreement: אֶפְשָׁר *efshar* "it is possible, one may" and אָסוּר *asur* "it is forbidden". So אָסוּר לְעַשֵּׁן "no smoking".

## Negation

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| לֹא *lo* | verbs, adjectives, "no" | אֲנִי לֹא יָכוֹל "I can't" |
| אֵין *ein* | "there is not" | אֵין זְמַן "there is no time" |
| אַל *al* | prohibition, with the future | אַל תִּשְׁכַּח "don't forget" |

אֵין is the negative of יֵשׁ, and with לְ it is the negative of "to have": אֵין לִי זְמַן "I have no time".

::: exercise translate
Translate.

- אֲנִי רוֹצֶה לִלְמֹד עִבְרִית. → {{I want to learn Hebrew.}}
- אֶפְשָׁר לְדַבֵּר אִתְּךָ? → {{Can I talk to you? / May I speak with you?}}
- אֵין לִי זְמַן. → {{I have no time. / I don't have time.}}
- אַתְּ צְרִיכָה לָלֶכֶת. → {{You have to go. / You must go.}}
:::

::: summary
- Infinitive: לכתוב, לדבר, להתחיל, להתלבש.
- Modals agree: רוצה, יכול, צריך + infinitive; אפשר and אסור do not.
- Negation: לא for verbs, אין for "there isn't", אל for prohibitions.
:::
