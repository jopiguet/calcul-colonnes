# 9. The past tense

The past tense marks the person with suffixes, so the pronoun can be left out in the first and second persons: כָּתַבְתִּי *katavti* "I wrote". The base is the third person masculine singular, כָּתַב *katav* "he wrote", which is also the form the dictionary gives.

## The past of pa'al

| Person | Suffix | כָּתַב write |
|---|---|---|
| I | ־תִּי *-ti* | כָּתַבְתִּי *katavti* |
| you (m.) | ־תָּ *-ta* | כָּתַבְתָּ *katavta* |
| you (f.) | ־תְּ *-t* | כָּתַבְתְּ *katavt* |
| he | — | כָּתַב *katav* |
| she | ־ָה *-a* | כָּתְבָה *katva* |
| we | ־נוּ *-nu* | כָּתַבְנוּ *katavnu* |
| you (m. pl.) | ־תֶּם *-tem* | כְּתַבְתֶּם *ktavtem* |
| you (f. pl.) | ־תֶּן *-ten* | כְּתַבְתֶּן *ktavten* |
| they | ־וּ *-u* | כָּתְבוּ *katvu* |

The same suffixes go on every verb in every binyan: דִּבַּרְתִּי *dibarti* "I spoke" (pi'el), הִתְחַלְתִּי *hitchalti* "I began" (hif'il). The negative is לֹא: לֹא כָּתַבְתִּי "I didn't write".

## "To be" in the past

| I was | you were (m. / f.) | he / she was | we were | they were |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| הָיִיתִי *hayiti* | הָיִיתָ / הָיִית *hayita / hayit* | הָיָה / הָיְתָה *haya / hayta* | הָיִינוּ *hayinu* | הָיוּ *hayu* |

הָיָה with לְ is the past of "to have": הָיָה לִי כֶּלֶב "I had a dog".

## Time words

אֶתְמוֹל *etmol* yesterday, שִׁלְשׁוֹם *shilshom* the day before yesterday, לִפְנֵי שָׁנָה *lifnei shana* a year ago, כְּבָר *kvar* already, עוֹד לֹא *od lo* not yet.

::: summary
- Past: suffixes ־תי, ־ת, ־ת, —, ־ה, ־נו, ־תם, ־תן, ־ו on every binyan; base כתב "he wrote".
- היה: הייתי, היית, היה, הייתה, היינו, היו; היה לי "I had".
- Negative לא; אתמול, כבר, עוד לא.
:::
