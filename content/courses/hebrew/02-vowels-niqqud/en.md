# 2. Vowels: niqqud and reading without it

Hebrew has five vowels, *a e i o u*. The Bible, prayer books, poetry and children's books mark them with dots and dashes under and over the letters, the **niqqud**, added by scholars in the early Middle Ages. Everyday Hebrew leaves the niqqud out, and uses the letters ו and י to show some vowels instead. You will learn both: niqqud for exact reading, and the unpointed spelling of newspapers and signs.

## The vowel signs

| Sign | Name | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| ָ ַ | kamats, patach | a | אַבָּא *aba* dad |
| ֶ ֵ | segol, tsere | e | יֶלֶד *yeled* boy; סֵפֶר *sefer* |
| ִ | chirik | i | אִמָּא *ima* mum |
| ֹ וֹ | cholam | o | תּוֹדָה *toda* thank you |
| ֻ וּ | kubuts, shuruk | u | שׁוּק *shuk* market |
| ְ | shva | silent, or a short *e* | סְפָרִים *sfarim* books |
| ֲ ֱ ֳ | chataf vowels | short *a*, *e*, *o* under a throat letter | אֲנִי *ani* I |

The dot inside a letter, the **dagesh**, makes ב כ פ hard: בּ *b*, כּ *k*, פּ *p*. In the Bible it also doubles a consonant.

## Reading without niqqud

| Pointed | Unpointed | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| תּוֹדָה | תודה | *toda* thank you |
| עִבְרִית | עברית | *ivrit* Hebrew |
| שׁוּק | שוק | *shuk* market |
| סֵפֶר | ספר | *sefer* book (or *safar* "counted", *sapar* "barber") |

In unpointed spelling ו marks *o* or *u*, and י often marks *i*; *a* and *e* are usually not written. A consonant ו is written double, וו, inside a word: תקווה *tikva* "hope". Stress usually falls on the last syllable: *to-DA*, *sha-LOM*.

::: exercise sign
What does it mark?

- the dot in בּ → ((dagesh: hard b* / the vowel i / a final form))
- ְ → ((shva: no vowel or a short e* / long a / u))
- ו in unpointed שוק → ((the vowel u* / the consonant v / nothing))
:::

::: summary
- Niqqud: ָ ַ a, ֶ ֵ e, ִ i, ֹ o, ֻ וּ u, ְ shva; dagesh בּ כּ פּ.
- Unpointed spelling: ו for o/u, י for i, וו for a consonant v; a and e not written.
- Stress mostly on the last syllable: toDA, shaLOM.
:::
