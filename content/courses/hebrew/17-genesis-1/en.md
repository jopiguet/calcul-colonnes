# 17. Genesis 1:1–5

The Hebrew Bible opens with a week of creation. The first five verses are the first day, and they use almost everything from the last lesson: the narrative וַיֹּאמֶר, the object marker אֵת, the order verb–subject–object. The text is the Masoretic text with its vowels; the cantillation marks are left out.

**1** בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ׃

**2** וְהָאָרֶץ הָיְתָה תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ וְחֹשֶׁךְ עַל־פְּנֵי תְהוֹם וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים מְרַחֶפֶת עַל־פְּנֵי הַמָּיִם׃

**3** וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יְהִי אוֹר וַיְהִי־אוֹר׃

**4** וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאוֹר כִּי־טוֹב וַיַּבְדֵּל אֱלֹהִים בֵּין הָאוֹר וּבֵין הַחֹשֶׁךְ׃

**5** וַיִּקְרָא אֱלֹהִים לָאוֹר יוֹם וְלַחֹשֶׁךְ קָרָא לָיְלָה וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם אֶחָד׃

## Verse 1, word by word

| Word | Parts | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| בְּרֵאשִׁית *bereshit* | בְּ + רֵאשִׁית (ר־א־שׁ, as in רֹאשׁ head) | in the beginning |
| בָּרָא *bara* | suffix form of ב־ר־א | created |
| אֱלֹהִים *elohim* | plural in form, singular in sense | God |
| אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם *et hashamayim* | object marker + the + heavens | the heavens |
| וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ *ve'et ha'arets* | and + object marker + the + earth | and the earth |

אֱלֹהִים looks plural (־ִים), but when it means the God of Israel its verbs are singular: בָּרָא, not בָּרְאוּ. The verse opens with the suffix form; from verse 3 the story runs on vav-consecutives.

## Notes

- תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ *tohu vavohu* "formless and empty". Modern Hebrew uses it for a total mess, and French borrowed it as *tohu-bohu*.
- יְהִי *yehi* "let there be" is a short prefix form of ה־י־ה; וַיְהִי is its narrative form, "and there was". "Let there be light, and there was light" is the same verb twice.
- כִּי־טוֹב "that it was good": כִּי here introduces what God saw.
- וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר: the day begins with the evening, which is why Shabbat and the festivals start at sunset.
- The maqaf ־ joins short words to the next one for chanting: עַל־פְּנֵי, אֶת־הָאוֹר.

## Translation

1 In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth. 2 And the earth was formless and empty, and darkness was upon the face of the deep, and the spirit of God hovered upon the face of the waters. 3 And God said, "Let there be light", and there was light. 4 And God saw the light, that it was good, and God divided between the light and the darkness. 5 And God called the light "day", and the darkness he called "night". And there was evening and there was morning, one day.

::: summary
- Genesis 1:1 opens with the suffix form בָּרָא; the story then runs on vav-consecutives: ויאמר, וירא, ויבדל, ויקרא, ויהי.
- אלהים: plural form, singular verbs.
- יהי "let there be", ויהי "and there was"; the day starts with ערב.
