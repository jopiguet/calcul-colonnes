# 10. The future and the imperative

The future marks the person with prefixes, and some persons add a suffix as well. The prefixes are the letters א ת י נ, which students remember as the word *eitan*. In pa'al the stem of כ־ת־ב is ־כְתֹב *-khtov*: the כ loses its dot after a prefix and is read *kh*.

## The future of pa'al

| Person | Pattern | כָּתַב write |
|---|---|---|
| I | אֶ־ | אֶכְתֹּב *ekhtov* |
| you (m.) | תִּ־ | תִּכְתֹּב *tikhtov* |
| you (f.) | תִּ־ ־ִי | תִּכְתְּבִי *tikhtevi* |
| he | יִ־ | יִכְתֹּב *yikhtov* |
| she | תִּ־ | תִּכְתֹּב *tikhtov* |
| we | נִ־ | נִכְתֹּב *nikhtov* |
| you (pl.) | תִּ־ ־וּ | תִּכְתְּבוּ *tikhtevu* |
| they | יִ־ ־וּ | יִכְתְּבוּ *yikhtevu* |

"You" (m.) and "she" are the same form: תִּכְתֹּב. Context decides. In spoken Hebrew the plural forms serve both genders.

## The imperative

The imperative is the future without its prefix: כְּתֹב *ktov* "write!" (m.), כִּתְבִי *kitvi* (f.), כִּתְבוּ *kitvu* (pl.). Many speakers use the future itself as a softer imperative: תִּכְתֹּב לִי "write to me".

A prohibition is never an imperative. It is אַל with the future: אַל תִּכְתֹּב "don't write". Common short imperatives: בּוֹא *bo* come!, לֵךְ *lekh* go!, שֵׁב *shev* sit!, תֵּן *ten* give!, תַּגִּיד *tagid* tell!

## Future time

מָחָר *machar* tomorrow, מָחֳרָתַיִם *mochoratayim* the day after tomorrow, בְּעוֹד שָׁבוּעַ *be'od shavua* in a week, אַחַר כָּךְ *achar kakh* afterwards.

::: summary
- Future: prefixes א ת י נ (*eitan*); ־י for "you" (f.), ־ו for the plurals.
- תכתוב is both "you" (m.) and "she".
- Imperative = future without prefix: כתוב, כתבי, כתבו. Prohibition: אל + future.
- מחר, מחרתיים, בעוד שבוע.
:::
