# 13. Numbers, time and the Hebrew calendar

Hebrew numbers have two sets, and the set that looks feminine goes with masculine nouns. This "polarity" holds from 3 to 10. For counting, prices, phone numbers and the clock, Israelis use the feminine set, which is the shorter one.

## One to ten

| | with a feminine noun (counting form) | with a masculine noun |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | אַחַת *achat* | אֶחָד *echad* |
| 2 | שְׁתַּיִם *shtayim* (שְׁתֵּי) | שְׁנַיִם *shnayim* (שְׁנֵי) |
| 3 | שָׁלוֹשׁ *shalosh* | שְׁלוֹשָׁה *shlosha* |
| 4 | אַרְבַּע *arba* | אַרְבָּעָה *arba'a* |
| 5 | חָמֵשׁ *chamesh* | חֲמִשָּׁה *chamisha* |
| 6 | שֵׁשׁ *shesh* | שִׁשָּׁה *shisha* |
| 7 | שֶׁבַע *sheva* | שִׁבְעָה *shiv'a* |
| 8 | שְׁמוֹנֶה *shmone* | שְׁמוֹנָה *shmona* |
| 9 | תֵּשַׁע *tesha* | תִּשְׁעָה *tish'a* |
| 10 | עֶשֶׂר *eser* | עֲשָׂרָה *asara* |

So שָׁלוֹשׁ בָּנוֹת "three girls" but שְׁלוֹשָׁה בָּנִים "three boys". "One" follows the noun: יֶלֶד אֶחָד "one child". Tens: עֶשְׂרִים 20, שְׁלוֹשִׁים 30, מֵאָה 100, אֶלֶף 1000.

## The clock and the week

מָה הַשָּׁעָה? "what time is it?" — הַשָּׁעָה שָׁלוֹשׁ "it is three", שָׁלוֹשׁ וָחֵצִי "half past three", אַרְבַּע וָרֶבַע "quarter past four". The week starts on Sunday, and the first six days are numbered: יוֹם רִאשׁוֹן Sunday, יוֹם שֵׁנִי Monday, יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי, יוֹם רְבִיעִי, יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי, יוֹם שִׁשִּׁי Friday, then שַׁבָּת *Shabbat*.

## Letters as numbers

Each letter has a value, which is how chapters, verses and years are numbered.

| | ×1 | ×2 | ×3 | ×4 | ×5 | ×6 | ×7 | ×8 | ×9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| units | א | ב | ג | ד | ה | ו | ז | ח | ט |
| tens | י | כ | ל | מ | נ | ס | ע | פ | צ |
| hundreds | ק | ר | ש | ת | | | | | |

The Hebrew calendar is lunar with leap months, and counts years from the traditional date of creation. The year 5787 began at Rosh Hashana in September 2026 and is written תשפ״ז, that is ת 400, ש 300, פ 80 and ז 7, with the 5000 understood. The months run from תִּשְׁרֵי (autumn, Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur) through חֶשְׁוָן, כִּסְלֵו (Hanukkah), טֵבֵת, שְׁבָט, אֲדָר (Purim), נִיסָן (Passover), אִיָּר, סִיוָן, תַּמּוּז, אָב, to אֱלוּל.

::: summary
- 3–10: the short set (שלוש, ארבע…) goes with feminine nouns and is used for counting; the long set (שלושה, ארבעה…) with masculine nouns.
- Clock: השעה שלוש, וחצי, ורבע. Week: יום ראשון … יום שישי, שבת.
- Letters are numbers: י 10, כ 20, ק 100, ת 400; תשפ״ז = 5787.
:::
