# 6. Prepositions, "of", "to have"

Four of the commonest prepositions are single letters joined to the next word: בְּ "in", לְ "to, for", מִ "from", כְּ "like". Prepositions take pronoun suffixes: לִי *li* "to me", שֶׁלִּי *sheli* "mine". And "to have" is said "there is to me": יֵשׁ לִי *yesh li*.

## Prepositions

| Preposition | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| בְּ *be-* | in, at | בְּתֵל אָבִיב in Tel Aviv |
| לְ *le-* | to, for | לִירוּשָׁלַיִם to Jerusalem |
| מִ / מִן *mi- / min* | from | מִפָּרִיז from Paris |
| כְּ *ke-* | like, as | כְּמוֹ *kmo* like |
| עַל *al* | on, about | עַל הַשֻּׁלְחָן on the table |
| עִם *im* | with | עִם חֲבֵרִים with friends |
| לְיַד *leyad* | next to | לְיַד הַבַּיִת next to the house |
| בִּשְׁבִיל *bishvil* | for (the sake of) | בִּשְׁבִילְךָ for you |

## Suffixes: לְ "to" and שֶׁל "of"

| Person | לְ | שֶׁל (possession) |
|---|---|---|
| I | לִי *li* | שֶׁלִּי *sheli* my, mine |
| you (m.) | לְךָ *lekha* | שֶׁלְּךָ *shelkha* |
| you (f.) | לָךְ *lakh* | שֶׁלָּךְ *shelakh* |
| he | לוֹ *lo* | שֶׁלּוֹ *shelo* |
| she | לָהּ *la* | שֶׁלָּהּ *shela* |
| we | לָנוּ *lanu* | שֶׁלָּנוּ *shelanu* |
| you (pl.) | לָכֶם *lakhem* | שֶׁלָּכֶם *shelakhem* |
| they | לָהֶם *lahem* | שֶׁלָּהֶם *shelahem* |

"My book" is הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁלִּי *ha-sefer sheli*, "the book of me": the noun keeps its article.

## "To have" and the object marker

יֵשׁ *yesh* "there is" and אֵין *ein* "there is not" are invariable: יֵשׁ לִי אָח *yesh li ach* "I have a brother", אֵין לָנוּ זְמַן *ein lanu zman* "we have no time". A definite direct object is introduced by אֶת *et*: אֲנִי רוֹאֶה אֶת הַיֶּלֶד "I see the boy"; without the article, no אֶת: אֲנִי רוֹאֶה יֶלֶד. With suffixes: אוֹתִי *oti* me, אוֹתְךָ *otkha* you, אוֹתוֹ *oto* him.

::: summary
- בְּ, לְ, מִ, כְּ joined to the word; עַל, עִם, לְיַד, בִּשְׁבִיל separate.
- לי, לך, לו, לה, לנו…; שלי, שלך, שלו… after a noun with the article: הספר שלי.
- יש לי / אין לי "I have / I don't have"; אֶת before a definite object.
:::
