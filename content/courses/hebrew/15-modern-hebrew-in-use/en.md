# 15. Modern Hebrew in use

For about seventeen centuries Hebrew was read, prayed, studied and written, but not spoken as anyone's first language. Its return as a mother tongue is unique. Eliezer Ben-Yehuda (1858–1922) raised his son in Hebrew in Jerusalem, coined words and compiled a large dictionary; the schools of the new settlements did the rest. In 1922 Hebrew became an official language of Mandate Palestine, and since 1953 the Academy of the Hebrew Language has coined and approved terms.

## New words from old roots

The Academy's favourite method is to pour an old root into an old pattern. The pattern מַ־ ־ְ־ ־ֵ־ makes tools, so ח־ש־ב "think, calculate" gives מַחְשֵׁב *machshev* "computer". Many international words came in as they were.

|---|---|---|
| old root, old pattern | מַחְשֵׁב *machshev* | computer (ח־ש־ב) |
| old root, old pattern | מִלּוֹן *milon* | dictionary (מִלָּה word) |
| loanword | טֶלֶפוֹן *telefon* | telephone |
| loanword | בַּנְק *bank* | bank |
| Arabic slang | יַאללָה *yalla* | come on, let's go |

## Speech today

Israelis pronounce ח and כ alike (*kh*) and ע usually like א, so spelling has to be learned word by word. Spoken Hebrew is direct and informal: people use first names and the singular "you" with almost everyone. Fixed phrases mark the moment: כָּל הַכָּבוֹד "well done", תִּתְחַדֵּשׁ "enjoy it" (for something new), בְּתֵאָבוֹן "enjoy your meal", בְּהַצְלָחָה "good luck". Abbreviations take the sign ״ before the last letter: ת״א Tel Aviv, צה״ל the Israel Defense Forces.

::: summary
- Revival: Ben-Yehuda, the schools, official status in 1922, the Academy since 1953.
- New words: old root + old pattern (מחשב, מילון), loanwords (טלפון), slang from Arabic (יאללה, סבבה), Russian (בלגן), Yiddish (נו).
- Speech: ח = כ, ע often silent; כל הכבוד, תתחדש, בתאבון, בהצלחה; abbreviations with ״.
:::
