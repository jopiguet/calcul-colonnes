# 3. Greetings, pronouns and "to be"

Hebrew has no verb "to be" in the present: אֲנִי סטוּדֶנְט *ani student* is "I (am a) student". Its pronouns distinguish "you" to a man and to a woman, and "they" for men and for women. With them and a dozen greetings you can start a conversation in the street in Tel Aviv.

## Personal pronouns

| Pronoun | Meaning | Pronoun | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| אֲנִי *ani* | I | אֲנַחְנוּ *anachnu* | we |
| אַתָּה *ata* | you (m.) | אַתֶּם *atem* | you (m. pl.) |
| אַתְּ *at* | you (f.) | אַתֶּן *aten* | you (f. pl.) |
| הוּא *hu* | he | הֵם *hem* | they (m.) |
| הִיא *hi* | she | הֵן *hen* | they (f.) |

A mixed group is masculine. In speech, *atem* and *hem* are often used for women too.

## The sentence without "to be"

| Hebrew | Meaning |
|---|---|
| אֲנִי מִיִּשְׂרָאֵל. *ani mi-Yisra'el.* | I am from Israel. |
| הִיא מוֹרָה. *hi mora.* | She is a teacher. |
| הוּא בַּבַּיִת. *hu ba-bayit.* | He is at home. |
| זֶה טוֹב. *ze tov.* | It is good. |

The past and future of "to be" do exist (lesson 10); only the present is left out.

## Greetings

| Hebrew | Meaning | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| שָׁלוֹם *shalom* | hello; goodbye | שָׁלוֹם |
| בֹּקֶר טוֹב *boker tov* | good morning | בֹּקֶר אוֹר *boker or* "morning of light" |
| עֶרֶב טוֹב *erev tov* | good evening | |
| לַיְלָה טוֹב *laila tov* | good night | |
| מָה שְׁלוֹמְךָ? / מָה שְׁלוֹמֵךְ? *ma shlomkha? / ma shlomekh?* | how are you? (to a man / to a woman) | בְּסֵדֶר *beseder* fine |
| מָה נִשְׁמָע? *ma nishma?* | what's up? (lit. what is heard?) | |
| בְּבַקָּשָׁה *bevakasha* | please; you're welcome | |
| סְלִיחָה *slicha* | sorry, excuse me | |
| לְהִתְרָאוֹת *lehitra'ot* | see you | |

::: summary
- אני, אתה, את, הוא, היא, אנחנו, אתם, אתן, הם, הן; you and they by gender.
- No "to be" in the present: אני סטודנט, הוא בבית.
- שלום, בוקר טוב / בוקר אור, מה שלומך?, בסדר, תודה, בבקשה, סליחה, להתראות.
:::
