# 14. Questions, she- and conjunctions

A yes-no question in Hebrew has the same word order as a statement; only the intonation rises: אַתָּה עִבְרִי? "are you Hebrew?" Formal writing may add הַאִם at the start. Question words come first.

## Question words

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| מִי *mi* | who | מִי זֶה? who is that? |
| מָה *ma* | what | מָה אַתָּה רוֹצֶה? what do you want? |
| אֵיפֹה *eifo* | where | אֵיפֹה הַתַּחֲנָה? where is the station? |
| לְאָן *le'an* | where to | לְאָן אַתֶּם הוֹלְכִים? where are you going? |
| מֵאַיִן / מֵאֵיפֹה *me'ayin / me'eifo* | where from | מֵאֵיפֹה אַתְּ? where are you from? |
| מָתַי *matai* | when | מָתַי הַשִּׁעוּר? when is the lesson? |
| לָמָּה *lama* | why | לָמָּה לֹא בָּאתָ? why didn't you come? |
| אֵיךְ *eikh* | how | אֵיךְ אוֹמְרִים...? how do you say...? |
| כַּמָּה *kama* | how much, how many | כַּמָּה זֶה עוֹלֶה? how much does it cost? |

## The prefix שֶׁ־

שֶׁ־ is written onto the next word and does two jobs. It is the relative "who, which, that": הַסֵּפֶר שֶׁקָּרָאתִי "the book that I read". And it is the conjunction "that" after verbs of saying and knowing: אֲנִי יוֹדֵעַ שֶׁהוּא כָּאן "I know that he is here". The literary equivalent is אֲשֶׁר, which you will meet in the Bible.

When the relative is the object of a preposition, Hebrew keeps a pronoun in the clause: הָעִיר שֶׁגַּרְתִּי בָּהּ "the city that I lived in it", that is "the city I lived in".

## Conjunctions

| ו־ *ve-* and | אוֹ *o* or | אֲבָל *aval* but |
|---|---|---|
| כִּי *ki* because | אִם *im* if | כְּשֶׁ־ *kshe-* when (not a question) |

ו־ is written onto the next word, like שֶׁ־: אַבָּא וְאִמָּא "Dad and Mum".

::: summary
- Yes-no questions: same order, rising tone (formal הַאִם).
- מי, מה, איפה, לאן, מאיפה, מתי, למה, איך, כמה.
- ־ש: relative "who, which" and conjunction "that"; literary אשר; a pronoun stays after a preposition.
- ־ו, או, אבל, כי, אם, ־כש.
:::
