# 3. Hissing sounds, and clusters

The last ten letters bring sh, ch, ts, dz, gh and kh, with two more ejectives: ჭ *ch'* and წ *ts'*. Georgian piles consonants up without a vowel between them; with the full alphabet you can read even its longest clusters, and order at the table.

## Ten letters

| Letter | Sound | Letter | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| შ | sh | ჟ | zh, the s of *pleasure* |
| ჩ | ch | ჭ | ch', ejective |
| ც | ts | წ | ts', ejective |
| ძ | dz | ჯ | j, as in *jam* |
| ღ | gh, a soft French r | ხ | kh, as in Scottish *loch* |

- ჩ *ch* and ც *ts* are aspirated; ჭ *ch'* and წ *ts'* are their ejectives, as in the last lesson.
- ღ *gh* is a voiced sound at the back of the mouth, close to the French or German *r*; ხ *kh* is its voiceless pair.
- You may also type the scholarly letters: *š* for შ, *č* for ჩ, *ž* for ჟ, *x* for ხ.

## At the table

| Georgian | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ღვინო | *ghvino* | wine |
| წყალი | *ts'q'ali* | water |
| ხაჭაპური | *khach'ap'uri* | khachapuri, cheese bread |
| ჩაი | *chai* | tea |
| ძაღლი | *dzaghli* | dog |
| ცა | *tsa* | sky |

- ღვინო *ghvino*, wine: Georgia has made it for some 8,000 years, fermented in large clay jars buried in the ground, the ქვევრი *kvevri*.
- ხაჭაპური *khach'ap'uri* joins ხაჭო *khach'o*, curd cheese, and პური *p'uri*, bread.
- Consonants stack without vowels: წყალი begins *ts'q'*. The verb გვბრდღვნის *gvbrdghvnis*, "he tears us to pieces", has eight consonants in a row.
