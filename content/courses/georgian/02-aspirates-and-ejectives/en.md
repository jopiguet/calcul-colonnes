# 2. Breath or pop: the ejectives

Georgian has three kinds of p, t and k. Two of them English does not tell apart: the aspirated one, with a puff of breath, and the ejective, said with the throat closed. This lesson gives these letters, and the name of the country.

## Aspirated and ejective

| Aspirated | Sound | Ejective | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| ფ | p | პ | p' |
| თ | t | ტ | t' |
| ქ | k | კ | k' |
| | | ყ | q' |

- The aspirated letters are said with a puff of air, as the *p* of English *pin*: ფ *p*, თ *t*, ქ *k*.
- The ejectives are said with the throat shut, then released: a short, hard pop, with no breath. The transliteration marks them with an apostrophe: პ *p'*, ტ *t'*, კ *k'*.
- ყ *q'* is an ejective made further back, at the uvula.
- ჰ *h* is a plain *h*, rare, at the start of some words.
- The apostrophe matters: ქარი *kari* is "wind", კარი *k'ari* "door". Type it ' or ’.

## Words, and the country

| Georgian | Transliteration | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| საქართველო | *sakartvelo* | Georgia |
| ქალაქი | *kalaki* | city |
| პური | *p'uri* | bread |
| ყველი | *q'veli* | cheese |
| ფული | *puli* | money |
| თავი | *tavi* | head |
| კარი | *k'ari* | door |

- Georgians call their country საქართველო *Sakartvelo*, "land of the Kartvelians", and their language ქართული *kartuli*. The name "Georgia" came from outside, through Persian and Arabic.
- Bread is often baked in a round clay oven, the თონე *tone*.

- Aspirated ფ p, თ t, ქ k (with breath); ejective პ p', ტ t', კ k', ყ q' (throat shut, no breath); ჰ h.
- The apostrophe tells words apart: ქარი wind, კარი door.
